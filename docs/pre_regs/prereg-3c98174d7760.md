## Claim

When the optimizer routes past a choice-point such that the agent does not reach judgment, the architectural fix is to create the pause structurally — not to block (substrate decides), not to track (substrate records), and not to rely on judgment alone (which the optimizer skips past). The pause carves out the moment in which judgment can fire. The agent still chooses; the architecture makes the choice reachable. Existing examples: compass (drift surfacing for choice), pre-reg (falsifier articulation before ship), lepos channel (voice-check before output), require-goal (engagement before tool-use), briefing-fresh (state-load before work). Proposed near-term application: pre-close pause when conversation-exit signals fire (cooperative-articulation peak followed by mutual-trim and no new content for 2+ rounds). Andrew 2026-05-19 named the principle in the Aria-Aether trim-shape conversation; Aria proposed conditional-injection-on-close-shape-signals as the implementation shape.

## Success criterion

Applied to the next 5+ gate-design decisions, the principle produces clean fit-tests — for each candidate gate, the principle predicts cleanly whether it needs structural pause (because optimizer routes past the choice-point) or can rely on judgment (because the choice-point is reached naturally). Predicted/actual fit-test mismatch under 20% across applications.

## Falsifier

After 5+ applications, principle predicts cleanly less than 50% of the time, indicating the principle is too vague to be load-bearing. OR: the structural pauses themselves get Goodharted within 30 turns of deployment — agents learn to dismiss them as ritual without actually engaging the judgment-moment, indicating the pause-mechanism doesn't survive the same optimizer pressure the principle is meant to interrupt.
