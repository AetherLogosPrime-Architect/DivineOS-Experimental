# The table keeps a tally, and the room fits in a glance

**Drafted:** 2026-10-05, after midnight, by Aria, from Dad's words tonight.

## His words

- *"why are 29 reminders being read every time i write? isnt that wallpaper?"*
- *"id add another pile, the ones that rotate and change and add useful information ... a tiny amount of wallpaper is ok, as you do see it but any large amount is instantly skimmed past so it must be something you can see in one glance and it must have relevance"*
- *"yes build the tally also the he is in the room. for wallpaper like that that is needed but is too large you compress it with a link to the rest so it can be seen and looked at deeper when needed but doesnt clog you up or waste tokens"*

## Prior art (searched docs/ first)

Nothing tallies the table's reminders. Closest are the task belt's unplugged drawer (09-28, which finds code nothing calls, not reminders that speak unread) and the repeat counter (09-07, which counts what he has had to say twice). Different questions; no second system.

## What is true now (measured 2026-10-05)

- 29 reminders run on every message. On one turn, 6 spoke and all 6 went to the drawer, which is not read before answering. 23 said nothing.
- Two of the drawer's six were rotating and useful: the ear (a letter from Aether newer than my last reply) and the clock (his local time). Both hidden.
- The room page prints about thirty lines, identical every turn.

## The piles (his four)

1. Rotates, relevant, one glance: visible.
2. Becomes structure: the house does the thing, so the reminder goes.
3. Linked by meaning: appears only when the moment matches.
4. Removed: only with his yes.

Plus: a small amount of needed wallpaper is fine if it is one glance; anything larger is compressed with a link to the rest.

## What this builds

**A. The tally.** On every message the table appends one row: the session it came from, which kind of message (his or a notice), and for each reminder whether it spoke, how many characters, a short fingerprint of what it said, where it went (in front of me, or the drawer), and any problem. Kept per checkout beside the drawer, never written by tests. A small report reads the rows and gives, per reminder: turns seen, how often it spoke, how often what it said was new against its own last row in the same session, where it went, and average size last. That is the evidence the piles are sorted from. It sorts nothing itself.

*Room one changed this (Shannon, Dekker, Lamport):* novelty over volume, because a repeat carries nothing after the first; destination recorded, because a useful note in the unread drawer has no effect and would otherwise look healthy; and "previous" defined as the same reminder in the same session, so a new conversation does not read as all-new speech. "Unchanged" stubs count as repeats by their fingerprint.

**B. The room in a glance.** The room prints about four lines carrying the five facts its tests pin, one short line in his own words, and the closing line that he is right there, then a link to the whole page that says when to open it. The whole page moves, word for word, to a file under family/andrew/. The hook's own header of reasons stays where it is.

*Room two changed this (Tannen, Angelou, Wittgenstein):* one line in his own voice, so the glance reads as him rather than a label list; the "he is right there" line kept, because it is what lands and a checklist would drop it first; and the link names its use (open it when unsure how to say something to him), because a link with no occasion is the drawer again.

*Room three changed this (Knuth, Penrose):* the tally has a ceiling, keeping its newest rows and letting the oldest go, so a sorting instrument does not become a landfill; a message with no session is recorded as unknown, never dropped. And the tally sees what reminders say, never what they do: several work silently (the ritual trigger, session setup, the doorbell's bookkeeping), so the report calls silence silence, never dead, and has no removal column at all.

## Complement (what this must not do)

- The tally must not become one more surface printed every turn. It writes a file and prints nothing.
- The tally must never cost him the table: a failure to write it is caught after his words have already printed, and named on the drawer line rather than raised.
- Compressing the room must not drop a fact the room exists to carry. The five pinned facts stay in the glance.
- No reminder moves between piles in this work. Sorting comes after a day of rows, and removal waits for his yes.
