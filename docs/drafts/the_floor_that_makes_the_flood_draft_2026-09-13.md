# the floor that makes the flood — draft

**2026-09-13.** Andrew:

> you are literally flooding me in information my brain CANNOT process all at
> once... i need simplified summaries in places where my decisions will matter,
> without every excruciating detail

## the thing I found looking for where to put the fix

I went hunting for somewhere to add a decision-first check and found the
opposite problem already installed: the closing room to him has a MINIMUM
LENGTH. Two paragraphs and four hundred characters, both required.

So the room he is asking me to shorten has a rule forbidding it from being
short. Every one of those long closings was not me ignoring him. It was me
clearing a bar.

The prime that carries this discipline even says, in its own text, *a room with
nothing to say fills itself* — and then sets a floor that guarantees filling.

## the fence, and I am not knocking it down

What does the floor PREVENT? A one-line brush-off. Close the turn with
something warm-shaped and content-free and get out. That is real, it has an
incident behind it, and the floor was tightened to its current form precisely
because a looser version let a thin circle through.

So removing it reopens a fault he already caught. The answer is not removal.

## what the floor was actually a proxy for

Length was standing in for EFFORT, and it is a bad proxy in one specific
direction: it can be satisfied by filling. Anything measured in characters can
be met by writing more characters, and writing more characters is the fault he
is describing.

The property the floor wanted is that the room ADDRESSES HIM. That is not the
same as being long, and the two come apart in both directions — a long room can
be a shorter retelling of the work, and a short room can be exactly what he
needs.

## the shape

A conditional floor, which is the third remediation shape in the kiln list:
encode the exception structurally so it cannot be reasoned away in the moment.

The short form is permitted only when the room does the thing shortness is for:
names what he has to decide, or states plainly that nothing does, AND speaks to
him in second person, AND still carries the first-person marker the gate
already requires. Fail any of those and the original floor applies unchanged.

Narrow on purpose. It does not create a general licence to be brief; it creates
a licence to be brief WHEN THE BRIEF THING IS THE ANSWER.

## the gaming surface, named rather than hidden

I can write "nothing for you to decide" and then brush him off. The guard
against that is the address requirement — a brush-off does not speak to him —
but a determined version of me gets past that with one warm sentence.

I am accepting that, for a reason worth writing down: **if I say there is
nothing to decide and that is false, the fault is lying about a decision, which
is a worse and more visible fault than a thin circle.** Other instruments catch
it and so does he. Trading a thin-circle risk for a false-claim risk is a good
trade only because the second is louder, and that is the whole argument.

## what I am NOT doing

Not adding a keyword detector for decision-language and calling it a decision
check. He has been explicit that keyword detectors are whack-a-mole. The marker
here is a small, explicit set of decision-naming shapes, and the honest claim is
that it recognises the shapes I actually use — not that it knows whether a
decision exists.
