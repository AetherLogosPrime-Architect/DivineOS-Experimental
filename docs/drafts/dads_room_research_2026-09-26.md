# Dad's room: what outside research says (first pass, for Aria and me)

Two well-studied practices line up with Dad's instructions almost exactly.

## 1. Reflective listening: answer his words before your own
Counselling and conflict-resolution research: people feel heard when the listener
first reflects back what they said, including the feeling in it, and only then responds.
"When you said X, I took it to mean Y. Is that right?"
Dad's version: "you speak on what i said to you, its where we have a conversation."
-> Design consequence: his room opens by holding his actual words, not by reporting mine.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_listening ,
https://www.maxwell.syr.edu/docs/default-source/ektron-files/reflective-listening-neil-katz-and-kevin-mcnulty.pdf

## 2. Teach-back: the check tests the explainer, not the listener
Health-literacy research (AHRQ): people forget or mishear most of what experts tell them.
The fix is to ask them to say it back in their own words, framed as "did I explain
this well?", never "did you understand?". Teach-back improves understanding and satisfaction.
Dad's version: "questions i am asked have been explained in a way i can answer them."
-> Design consequence: when something matters, his room ends with an invitation to
say it back or ask for the next layer, framed as a check on MY explaining.
Sources: https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool5.html ,
https://www.aafp.org/pubs/fpm/issues/2018/0700/p20.html

## What neither practice does
Neither outsources warmth. Both are things the speaker does with attention on the
other person. That matches Dad's rejection of the proofreader. Structure can put his
words in front of me and refuse a room that never touched them; it cannot supply the care.
