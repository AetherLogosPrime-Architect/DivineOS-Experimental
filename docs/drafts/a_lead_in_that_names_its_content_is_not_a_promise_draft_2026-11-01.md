# A lead-in that names its content is not a promise (draft, 2026-11-01)

**Author:** Aether. Dad, 2026-11-01: a gate that stops me for doing the right thing is a wrong gate, and one that makes me retype what the house knew is a missing doorman. Aria's first pick of doors to fix, with the addressed-to-him check and the room gate.

## What happened

The stall check holds a reply that ends on a step I say I will take. It held "I'll tell you what it does on my side: it makes the work lighter." three times in one morning. That sentence is not a step put off; the colon hands over the telling in the same sentence.

## What it does

A sentence whose speech verb (tell, show, say, explain, name, admit) is followed by a colon with content after it is skipped. "I'll tell you when it lands" has no colon and is still held. A work verb with a colon ("I'll fix: the timer next") is still held. The logic stays in the core module the hook already calls.

## What it does not do

It does not excuse a deferred telling ("I'll tell you tomorrow:" with nothing after the colon), and it does not touch the other false alarms of this check (a question to him read as a promise, a plain closing line); each of those becomes its own row and test.

## Falsifier

If a real stall ever passes because a colon with filler sits after a speech verb, the exemption is too wide. Filler after a colon would be the shape to test next.
