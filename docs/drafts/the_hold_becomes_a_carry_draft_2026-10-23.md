## What the hold has cost (measured)

Arms are not cost; refusals are. My hold log (`~/.divineos-aria/question_hold_log.jsonl`, 2026-10-01 to 10-23): 474 holds armed, **58 refusals of a tool call (Bash 40, Write 10, Edit 8), about 2.6 a day**, which are **34 distinct questions** (the same one refused several times in a row on 10-12). Aether's seat: 1202 armed, 75 refusals, 3.6 a day. Hours held are not a count of cost and I withdraw the figure I first gave.

Read by hand, of the 34 distinct questions refused: about 27 are design, order or check-in questions and conversation ("Is that order okay with you?", "Do you want the first of those two steps built as one piece, or the two together?", "Is this reading easier on your eyes?"), none of them a decision only he can make; about 4 touch an action that has its own permission (his bypass key, the terminal panel, sending a draft up, starting something again); about 3 are unclear. The 4 are not yet checked against a gate that would have refused the action.

## The proposal

1. **The hold becomes a pure carry. No exception lives inside it.** A question to him is one row (one per question, not one per mention), shown to me before I speak, with its age in his turns, oldest first, and on the board. Nothing it carries stops a letter, a tool call or a reply.
2. **His exception is kept where it already lives.** *"If its a decision that truly needs me then yes we pause and wait"*: a decision that needs him is an action (merging to main, deleting his files, spending, sending outward, using his bypass key), and each already has its own gate that refuses the action until his yes. The pause is the action's gate, not the question's hold. This removes the label from the hold: nobody, including me, decides a question "truly needs him".
3. **The gates say what they wait for, in his language,** so a pause is not invisible to him (to check: read each gate's refusal text for the four).
4. **His answer closes the row, in passing or in form.** A by-meaning reader proposes, a seat that did not build the carry confirms, verbatim and typed, never relayed (same shape as Revision 9 in the workshop draft).

## How it can fail, written first

- **The carry becomes the shelf.** A question that blocks nothing can wait for ever. Counter: age in his turns, oldest first, shown to me every time; a reply that does the thing the question was about while the question is open is shown to me beside the open question.
- **A decision needs him and has no action gate in front of it** (Aether's case 1, "which of two designs?"). His own rulings make design and build-order mine, so I do not count these; if one exists in the record, it is a missing gate, and the fix is a gate on that action, not a hold on the question.
- **Something done to HIM** (his room, his board, his messages) may cross no gate. In the refusals these appear as "Is this reading easier on your eyes?" and "What would help you feel seen?", which block nothing and want a carry, not a pause.
- **Repeats counted as new.** The same question mentioned three times is one row.

## Falsifiers, counted

- After the carry ships, a tool-call refusal attributable to a *question to him* is a defect, not a feature: the count must be **zero**, and the first one is a failure of this design.
- Over the first **30 questions to him**: more than 5 still open past 20 of his turns means the carry has become the shelf.
- Of the pauses that happen, **every one must come from an action's own gate**; one that comes from anywhere else is the hold coming back.

## Not covered

How he feels about being asked at all. The carry is about whether I stop; whether a question was worth asking is a conversation, not a mechanism. Also not covered: the four action gates have not yet been read for whether they would have refused those four actions.
