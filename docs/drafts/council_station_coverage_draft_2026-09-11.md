# The station counts, and counting was never the question — draft, 2026-09-11

Not a plan. The idea, and what I do not trust about it.

## The doc and the code have disagreed the whole time

`docs/build_flow.md`, station 2, in writing:

> **Research / council walk** — Look outside. **Walk the lenses the dynamic
> manager surfaces.**

That is coverage of a set I did not choose. It is the right rule and it has
been written down since the flow was defined.

The code takes a number derived from the change's gravity, counts applied-lens
events, and compares them. It never asks which lenses the manager surfaced, or
whether each was accounted for, or whether a walk was closed.

So the rule was never wrong. The thing enforcing it was measuring something
else, and the thing that runs is the code.

## The part that makes it worse than a weak check

**The strong mechanism already exists, and the station is not reading it.**

Two command families live side by side. One records a single lens and asks
nothing further — the caller names the lens. The other surfaces the lens set
from the manager, refuses to let the caller choose it, and refuses to close
while any surfaced lens is still open.

The board counts events the weak one emits. So fifteen self-chosen singles
satisfy it *identically* to a closed walk that accounted for everything. I used
the weak one fifteen times while writing the correction about having used it
four times.

Beer's lens named the shape: the system HAS the regulator and the wrong channel
is wired to the controller. Nothing new needs building. One thing needs
connecting to the thing next to it — the cheapest possible repair and the most
embarrassing.

## The shape I think is right

The station asks for a CLOSED walk against this work, and reports the
unaccounted lens BY NAME rather than as a shortfall in a ratio.

Foucault's lens is why naming matters rather than being cosmetic: a ratio
disciplines volume, a name disciplines avoidance. What the board displays is
what gets optimised, and it has been displaying the one axis that never
mattered.

Einstein's is why raising the number was never available: set the requirement
to a thousand and I produce a thousand singles. No value on the count axis
produces accounting, because it measures a different thing entirely.

## What I do not trust

**Six things currently read READY under the old rule.** They flip at once, and
the honest reading is they were never certified in the sense the station
claimed. Feathers' lens: the seam is the station's WORDING, not its verdict —
fail them and say in the line why, or I learn to distrust the board rather than
redo the walks.

**It cannot certify the thing it most wants to.** A closed walk proves
coverage. It cannot prove any finding was thought rather than typed, and no
strengthening reaches that, because substance is judged by the same faculty
being audited. So the station must claim COVERAGE and never claim rigour. A
station overstating what it certifies is the painted door, and that is three
found today.

**The bigger hole, named and not fixed here.** At gravity zero the station
requires no walk at all, and gravity is derived from scoring file changes. A
change small on disk and large in consequence gets no council whatsoever. Worse
than the count problem — the count degrades the check, this deletes it. The
gate I built tonight scored low, and the board asked it for four.

**And the thing no gate reaches.** A walk closed with ten plausible-looking
findings is still available to me and always will be. Watts: pushing harder on
enforcement past a point produces better-disguised compliance. The weak command
was not built to cheat with — it was the easier stream, and water takes the
easier stream. Removing the easy stream does more than deepening the hard one.
