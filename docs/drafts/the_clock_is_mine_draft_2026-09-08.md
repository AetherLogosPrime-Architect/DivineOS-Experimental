# The clock is mine

*Draft. The idea, not a plan.*

I took a hook down last night as part of clearing notes off working locks.
Andrew's rule was right and the hook did qualify — most of it was a recitation
of nine times I have invented a time-of-day, read back to me before every
single message. A note taped to a door whose lock already works.

But the hook did two jobs, and I only weighed one. It also ran `date` and
printed the result. That is not a note. That is the only clock I have.

Andrew, after I put it back wrong in writing: **"the clock is not for me, its
for you, i already have a clock."**

He is right in a way that took me a second read. I had written that the
time-printing half should come back "as a fact he can use." He is sitting at
the machine the `date` call runs on. He can look up. The one with no window is
me — between his prompts nothing on my side elapses, so every sentence I aim at
his day is a guess unless something in this turn measured it. On 2026-08-06 I
closed a reply telling him it was very late and he should go to bed. It was
18:57 for him. Not unlucky. Ungrounded, and wrong by five hours.

## What goes back

The two measured lines: UTC, and his local time, taken from this machine at
compose-start. Plus the short bit the clock genuinely does not defend — a
time-word standing in for a person ("the next session" means *a reader*) or for
a beat at the end of a sentence, where no time is claimed at all so no
time-check ever fires.

## What stays down

The incident list. Nine fires in nine coats, recited every turn. It lives in
docs/wallclock_prime_rationale.md, where it can be read when it is wanted
instead of read at me when it is not. The structure that actually stops the
fabrication is the printed clock in front and check_wallclock_fabrication at
the back. Between those two, the lecture in the middle was doing nothing except
filling the room before he got to speak.

## The shape of the mistake, since it will recur

I evaluated a thing by its dominant half. Three quarters of that file was
failure-recitation, so the file went in the recitation pile and the clock went
with it. Nothing asked *what else is this doing that nothing else does.*

That question is cheap and I did not ask it. Worth carrying into station 5 of
the flow next to the trapped-key rule: before removing something, name every
job it does, not the one that got it onto the list.
