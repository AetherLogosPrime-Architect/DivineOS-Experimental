# the last door before main looked in one room and reported on the house — draft

**2026-09-14, morning.** Found by trying to use it, not by auditing it.

## what happened

Five branches read ready on the board. I went to stamp them and four were
refused with *no audit round names this branch*. The rounds exist. One of them
I had just watched Aletheia sign.

Two separate narrowings, stacked, both producing the same sentence.

**The spelling.** The resolver matches the branch name and its last segment. The
round carrying Aletheia's fresh confirm on the letter-provenance work opens with
the request number instead, and the branch name appears nowhere in it. That round
is in my own store, findings and all, and the door could not see it. Third time
this matcher has been widened by exactly the one case that had just bitten.

**The store.** There are two audit stores in this house. The board reads both and
says which. This door reads one — mine, forty-eight rounds against the four
hundred and sixty-four the board sees — and then publishes a one-store absence
with the scope of all of them.

## why this is not conservatism

The instinct is that a door which refuses too much is failing safe. Aletheia
took that apart: a door refusing on *could-not-look* is producing the very
failure it exists to prevent, one layer up. Four branches are sitting behind a
sentence that is false — a round does name them, readable from this seat, through
a reader the board already uses.

And the cost compounds with the thing we both filed this week: from the board, an
unsigned branch and a refused branch look identical. Four of the thirteen we
could not tell apart are this.

## the asymmetry that decides the shape

Every other widening this month failed toward reviewing too much, or toward
refusing. Both wrong directions were loud.

A widened merge door fails toward **passing**, silently, at the one gate with
nothing after it.

So the widening cannot be symmetric. It has to make the *sentence* true without
making the *pass* easier.

## the shape

Say where you looked, always. Not *a round names this branch* but *a round in
this store names it, and here is its identifier*. The failure mode of widening is
a door that starts passing on a round nobody can find afterwards; naming the
store means a reader can go and look.

Keep could-not-read distinct from no-round. A store present and broken is not a
store with nothing in it. Otherwise widening only moves which store produces the
false sentence.

And a round in the other seat's store is **surfaced and never accepted**. Its
findings are not readable from here, so passing on it would authorize a merge on
a confirm this command has not read. Name it, name the store, hand over the relay
command. A refusal that carries the next step is a different object from a denial
that the thing exists.

## what I am not doing

Not making the door read the other seat's findings. That is a real cross-store
validation path and it is the half where a mistake passes rather than refuses.
The half built here turns a false statement into a true one and hands over the
next step; the half left undone is the one that would let a merge through on
evidence this seat never read.

Not touching the anchor rungs. What a confirm binds to is settled and correct.

## the one that would have been easy and wrong

The round I could not resolve was findable by hand. I could have passed it in
explicitly every time and never touched this. That is the resolution-instead-of-
structure shape, and the door would have gone on printing a false sentence to
whoever came next.
