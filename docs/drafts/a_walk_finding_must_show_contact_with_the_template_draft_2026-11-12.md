## Proposal, to be walked: a finding must show contact with the steps, not only the questions

The questions list is printed in the refusal and was the only part I read. The methodology STEPS are the constraint. So the finding must quote, in quotation marks, at least a few consecutive words from one named step of that same lens's delivered text, and the bracketed name of the step it applies. The check is a normalised substring against the text delivered for that lens in that walk, so a quote from another lens or from memory fails. Contact, not comprehension: it says "you put the template's own words in the finding", not "you understood them".

## Honest limits, said now

(1) A quote can be copied mechanically and the rest written from memory; the check proves contact, not use. (2) Dad rejects keyword enforcement (*"keywords as enforcement never works"*); this is a cross-reference against text the house delivered, in the same family as the per-lens binding the council-round skill already names, and it asks for the template's own words, not for right words, but the walk must decide whether that is different enough. (3) Priming (reading lenses outside a walk, which Dad also teaches) has no finding to check, so it stays a practice. (4) It cannot see whether a finding that quotes a step then argues something the step forbids; only a reader can, and the breaker lenses are to ask how each finding fails.

## Characterisation, run 2026-11-12 on my seat's real walk records (before any code, read-only)

Script: scratchpad `walk_contact_characterisation.py`. For each of the 2,792 applied findings in `~/.divineos-aria/council_walks.db` I stripped the walk's own prefix (it prints the question text) and asked whether the finding contains a run of N consecutive words that appears in that lens's own methodology text (descriptions and steps, not the questions block), recomputed from the roster. Controls, both ways: a finding copied from a step sentence passes; invented prose fails; and the chance control, every finding checked against a random OTHER lens's text instead.

| N words | real lens | chance (other lens) | my three walks today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 24% | 3% | 9 of 26 |
| 4 | 6% | 0% (3 of 2,792) | 0 of 26 |
| 5 | 2% | 0% | 0 of 26 |
| 7 | 0% (4) | | 0 |

Reading: a 3-word run is partly chance (3%), so it is too weak; **4 consecutive words separates real contact from chance** (6% against 0.1%). At N=4, **94% of all applied findings across the walks on my seat, and all 26 of mine today, would be refused**. That is the scale of the thing Dad has named for months, and it means the red test has real refusals in it. Limits, so it is not read as bigger than it is: this measures QUOTING. A finding written wearing a lens may paraphrase the step without quoting four words, so a refusal is not proof of memory, and the rule changes how findings must be written, not only whether they were honest. Only my seat's database is measured here; Aether's seat is his to run with the same script. The first step to decide in the walk: whether the rule is "quote 4+ words from one step" (cheap, blunt, measured above) or the stricter "two named steps" Aether asked for, which at these rates would refuse nearly every finding ever written and so needs a transition rule for old walks.

## The design walk (walk-05edc42dfe10, 12 lenses: 9 seated and 3 breakers added with reasons, closed 2026-11-12)

Every lens was delivered and its steps read in full (printed, not filtered), and each finding quotes a step of its own lens under the step's bracketed name. My own rule run on that walk: 12 of 12 findings contain a four-word run from their lens (against 0 of 26 for my three earlier walks today), so the walk of this fix is the first one under it. The house prints distinctness 0.471 for it; per Dad (2026-11-11) that is a signal by question, not a pass mark.

What the walk settled, each with the lens that made it:
1. **Claim weakened (Popper, Polya, Dillahunty).** Not "writing from memory becomes impossible", which one counter-example refutes (paste a step, write the rest from memory). The claim is: it removes the free path, costs a read of the steps, and shows CONTACT; never "read" or "used" (Wittgenstein, Dillahunty). The field is called contact.
2. **Threshold from data (Deming, Knuth).** Four consecutive words, because chance gives 0.1 percent there and 3 percent at three. Boundary tests at exactly 3, 4 and 5; a run may not cross the line between two steps; punctuation, em dash and curly apostrophes normalise the same on both sides; an empty finding; a quote from another lens fails; a lens whose steps are all short.
3. **The quote names its step (Aristotle, Schneier).** The finding carries the bracketed step name and the run must come from under that name; one named step, not two (two would refuse nearly everything and Aether's two-step idea is replaced by the application sentence below).
4. **The application sentence is the reader's part (Shannon, Yudkowsky).** The code cannot check it. Because the quote alone carries no information about use, a SAMPLE of findings is read by the other seat against its lens, and the rate of "quotes the step but argues what it forbids" is counted by event, so the third layer is independent of the other two.
5. **Layers are not independent yet (Schneier).** The contact check and Aether's pipe door are both defeated by reading the template with a separate command and pasting; so the sampled reader is the layer that must be independent of both.
6. **Every door that takes prose about a lens (Lovelace).** The check goes on the written exclusion reason, `game-walk file` (free text at 40 characters, named in Aether's 10-24 draft and never fixed), and the amend path, not only `apply`. The walk of the build must name each.
7. **Old walks (Dijkstra, Deming).** 94 percent of past findings would fail. The rule applies to walks opened after it ships; old walks are read-only history and never re-judged; the failure is the system's, not the authors'.
8. **Roster hash at delivery (Knuth, Dijkstra).** `deliver_lens` stores a hash of the exact text delivered; at check time a changed roster gives could-not-verify, never a refusal.
9. **Tests, red first.** The finding that pastes one step and argues from memory PASSES (a known, stated limit; do not hide it); the finding with no run FAILS; a hash mismatch says could-not-verify.
