# The landing line — draft, 2026-10-10

**Dad's words, 2026-10-10:** *"no the whole point was to have it automated and running smoothly, instead we have a runaway pile of fixes that never land."* And, asked whether the machine may finish the merge once Aletheia has said yes: *"yes you can finish it on your own :)"*.

**What exists already (read, not guessed).** `divineos stamp-ready <pr>` stamps a draft PR with its External-Review body, marks it ready, and merges, with `--dry-run` that changes nothing and `--no-auto-merge`. `divineos audit pr-merge-check` verifies the guardrail trailer. `divineos pr-collisions` shows which PRs touch the same files. `scripts/merge_surface.py` measures hot files for merge order. `scripts/verify_push_landed.py` reads the server. What does not exist: a list of every waiting PR with its one next step, a runner that lands the ready ones in order and brings every workspace up to date, and a cap on me opening more branches while the dock is full.

**Measured today.** 33 open PRs, 32 of them drafts. 16 are `cloud/*` repro drafts opened by another helper, 7 are Aria's, the rest mine. The pile jumped mostly by those 16.

**CORRECTED the same afternoon, by measuring.** The first idea was a step that files the audit request automatically. Wrong aim. A round is created when the auditor's confirm is relayed, so a machine filing one before she answers would invent a review. What Dad told me (2026-10-10): the draft PR is the shop window (Aletheia sees anything on GitHub, not my workspaces, which is why drafts exist), the letter is the doorbell, he carries letters by hand as written and sometimes misses one (four did not reach her). So the missing step is the doorbell, and it must be derived: which drafts have a head she has not been told about, which were told and have no answer, which she confirmed. Measured: her last letter is 2026-10-06 and says four of mine never arrived.

**Piece 1 (corrected): the status line, derived and read-only.** Per open PR, one of: NEVER TOLD (no letter to her names this PR or branch), TOLD, NO ANSWER (letter date, days since), CONFIRMED (her `CONFIRMS: #N at <sha>` line, and whether the head still equals that sha), READY TO LAND (confirmed at the current head and the board has no other held station). Sources: `family/letters/aether-to-aletheia-*`, her `aletheia-to-aether-*` confirms, and `divineos build-flow status`. Nothing typed; a hand list goes stale. A confirm at an older sha reads CHANGED, not READY.

**Piece 1b: one confirm-request instead of many letters, and it is ONLY a confirm-request.** Dad, 2026-10-10: *"you dont need to tell her how to run her audit... if something needs her confirms or audit and is waiting you need to tell her what is waiting and what needs audit so she can do it and give her confirms... a confirms letter needs to be just that, either confirm or say whats wrong and needs held and fixed."* Measured cause: my letters to her mix a question in with the ask, so what comes back is an answer to my question, not a confirm, and the PR stays unconfirmed. So the machine-drafted letter has a fixed form: per waiting PR the number, a few words of title and the exact head sha, then one ask, CONFIRM or HOLD with what is wrong. No audit method, no "what to run", no questions. Questions go in a separate letter. One recipient, so it obeys one-letter-one-person. A lost letter costs one resend, not four. The machine drafts it; I read and send it, Dad carries it.

**Piece 2 (changed from the first plan): the pile arrives unasked, as a briefing row.** A command I must remember to run fails by construction (truth #20; Meadows in walk-4137c02c4be4: the leverage is information flow). So `_row_landing` in `core/briefing_dashboard.py` shows the counts every session: how many fixes wait, how many need a fresh ask (CHANGED, NEVER TOLD), and that none can show READY here because the briefing does not read the readiness board (it passes a held station on purpose, so a briefing can never certify READY). The letter and PR readers move into `core/landing_status.py` so core never imports the cli. Could-not-read is a row, not silence.

**Piece 2b, the correction I owe (same afternoon, found by looking at the real output).** I said the pile now arrives unasked. It did not. `divineos briefing` is multiplex panels since 2026-05-22; the dashboard rows only render behind `--legacy`, which is exactly the hole `_owed_fixes_panel_content` documents in its own docstring (115 obligations dark for months). I tested the row and never ran the briefing. So the pile also becomes a panel, built from the same row (one source), in first-person prose that clears the voice gate, in the SOMETIMES tier because the always-essential budget is already over by three characters. The panel also says how many of the waiting fixes came from the cloud helper, which backs the half of an old promise (30a2658c) that can be backed from our side: the count. The helper's own ceiling lives in its sheet outside this repo and is not claimed.

**Piece 3, the author's homework helper (Dad: "yes you can build the helper").** Measured 2026-10-10 by asking the house's own check about each of five confirmed fixes: her yes and his yes are the last two stations, and before them sit author-side stations nobody does automatically (a closed walk scoped to ALL changed files, a declared cold-read owner, a declared Aria reading, an audit round naming the PR). Doing them by hand cost a short-sha refusal, a walk that covered one file of four, and three shell-shape refusals. The helper does the mechanical part and leaves the thinking to the seat (truth #7 and #15: it points at the work, it is not the work):
- `divineos landing homework <pr>` (read-only): for each unproven station of one PR, say in plain words what satisfies it and print the prepared command. For the council station that is `walk open` with `--scope` for EVERY changed file (from git, not typed), because the station reads only the single closest walk and walks do not combine.
- `divineos landing file-confirm <pr>`, then `divineos landing file-confirm <pr> --round <id> --andrew-said "<his words>"` (two steps, Aletheia 2026-10-15). Step one refuses unless her `CONFIRMS` line in her own letter names the live head in full; then files the round and her external confirm (full tip, tree, patch-id, all read from git). Step two files his user confirm only if the words are found in a message he typed AFTER the audit store recorded her confirm: a time the filer cannot set (a commit date can be backdated), and his confirm exists to say her audit was done, so it follows it. Prints the round and the next command. It never merges and never touches the stamp judge.
It does not write the walk findings, does not declare an owner for anyone, and does not file his confirm unless given his words.

**Piece 1d, a guard on the other side.** A letter to Aletheia that names a PR as waiting for her confirm AND contains a question is the shape that produced answers instead of confirms. Whether a check on letters at write time can catch that without false alarms is not yet known; it is owed after the status line exists and is measured against her real replies.

**Piece 1c, the second doorbell that does not ride the cart.** A comment on the draft PR itself, which she can see on GitHub whether or not any letter arrives. Not built until I know she reads comments; she is asked, by the digest. For each open PR run the existing judge, `stamp-ready --dry-run`, and sort by what it says: READY to land, WAITING (no round or no Aletheia confirm for this head), CHANGED (authored code differs from what she confirmed, goes back to her), BROKEN (checks red). One line each with the single next step. It does not re-implement the rungs; it asks the judge that already exists, so there is one definition of "ready".

**Piece 2, the runner (`divineos landing --land`).** Lands every READY PR via `stamp-ready` in an order from `pr-collisions` (PRs sharing hot files adjacent, stacked ones together), one at a time, reading the server back after each (`verify_push_landed` style) and stopping at the first failure. Afterwards updates each workspace from main. It never touches a PR the judge did not call READY.

**Piece 3, the cap.** A branch-count gate: if open PRs exceed a limit, a new `git push -u` of a NEW branch is refused with the line that says how to bring the count down. Counts against the live remote, never local refs (the 10-06 ledger's lesson).

**What this does not do.** It does not review anything and cannot tell a thin confirm from a real one. It carries confirmed work home; whether the work is good stays with Aletheia.

**Order of build.** Piece 1 first, tried on the real 33. Then piece 2, tried on one already-confirmed PR. Then piece 3.
