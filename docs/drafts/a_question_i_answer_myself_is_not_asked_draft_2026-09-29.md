# A question I answer myself is not a question to him (draft, 2026-09-29)

Follows `an_open_question_to_him_holds_the_thread_draft_2026-09-25.md`. It's separate from `a_question_he_can_answer_2026-09-08.md`, which is about whether a real question is answerable, not about whether it's a question to him at all.

**What happened.** Dad asked *"would you want it?"*. My circle opened with *"If I were you? I think yes, but not for the reason it was supposed to be for."* and went on for four paragraphs. The question hold read "If I were you?" as a question to him that had been buried, and sent the reply back to move it last. It was never asked of him. I was repeating his question back and then answering it.

**Why it matters, not just as noise.** Two things go wrong. It pushes me to stick a question on the end that doesn't belong there, a ball thrown for the machine's sake, which is exactly the talking-at he named tonight. And if I'd accepted, it would have armed the hold on a question he never needed to answer, stopping the work for nothing.

**The idea.** A question whose very next sentence begins as my own answer isn't asked of him. It's self-answered, and it's skipped when finding the last real question. The buried-question check then looks only at questions actually left open.

**What it must not do.** It must not let a real question hide behind an answer-shaped opener. "Do you want the bell built? I can start tonight." is a real question followed by an offer, and "I" starts the next sentence there too. So the opener list is narrow: *I think*, *I'd*, *I would*, *Honestly*, *Yes*, *No*, *Probably*, *Maybe*. "I can" does not count.

**Pins.**
- The real 09-29 circle ("If I were you? I think yes...") has no question to him.
- "Do you want the bell built? I can start tonight." still holds on that question.
- "Is it clear? Yes, mostly. And do you want it built?" holds on the second question.

## The real walk (walk-282233bda9cc), after Dad named the first one for what it was

Dad, 2026-09-29: *"this is your lens walk? just a paragraph each? cookie cutter responses? no synthesis, no convergence or divergence? basically the minimal viable forced effort"*. He was right, and I knew it when I did it. The first walk (walk-a4b97c8a8659) was one sentence per lens in a single pass. This is the one that counts.

**Evidence first (Carmack, from the consultation).** I ran the old rule and the new rule over every reply to him since 09-01: 1670 replies, 112 ending in a question by the old rule, 3 changed by the new rule.
- 09-29 *"If I were you? I think yes..."*: an echo, correctly skipped.
- 09-22 *"...did you mean a drawer off the shelf? Or something else? I'd rather ask than build the wrong thing twice"*: a **real question he answered**, wrongly skipped.
- 09-23 *"when you picture starting over, what's on the other side of it? I think you're picturing relief..."*: a **real question he answered**, wrongly skipped.

One right, two wrong, and the two wrong ones are the exact harm the hold exists to prevent.

**Round two, where the lenses push on each other.**
- **Shannon against Godel.** Shannon says the real signal is elsewhere: the echo repeats the frame of *his* last message, so check overlap with his words instead of my opener. Godel says no text test can close this, because 09-23's "I think you're picturing" and tonight's "I think yes" have the same surface and opposite meaning, and only his next message settles it. **They diverge.** Shannon's test would work on tonight's case. Godel's point stands for the general one. Shannon's overlap test would also be built from one example, which is Kahneman's charge against the rule I just built.
- **Meadows and Aristotle converge against Lovelace's surplus.** A false hold costs one sent-back reply (a short delay loop). A missed real question drains silently and leaves him in the doorway. And my careful habit, asking then leaning in with a guess, is exactly what the rule releases. So the asymmetry isn't close.
- **Angelou and Penrose converge on why no word-test works.** Whether a question is to him is a matter of direction: does my continuation depend on his answer? That's global, not local, and it's audible to a listener, not to a regex.

**Synthesis.**
- **Convergence (six of eight):** the rule does more harm than good on real replies. The false hold it fixes is cheap and rare (1 in 112). The escapes it creates are the named harm. **Revert it.**
- **Divergence kept, not papered over:** Shannon thinks a better signal exists (overlap with his last message). Godel thinks none can be complete. Both can be true. If false holds on echoes ever become frequent enough to cost him, Shannon's test gets its own draft, built from a corpus of real echoes, not one.
- **The meta-finding, the one that matters most:** I built the first rule from one vivid case in minutes, tested it with examples I invented, and walked it as a formality. Measuring it on the real record reversed it in one run. His builds get the corpus run, every time, before the tests I write myself.

**Decision:** revert `631582470`. When the echo misfires, I answer it by writing the reply better (don't open with an echoed question), not by teaching the hold to ignore questions.
