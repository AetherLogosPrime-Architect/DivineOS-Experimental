# Why the translate-first prime says what it says

Lifted out of the hook payload 2026-09-10, the same way the circle prime was
split on 2026-09-06 and for the same reason. The prime wrote 10216 bytes against
a delivery limit near 2000, so roughly four fifths of it never arrived — and it
stopped arriving *this turn*, while the principle about pruning obsolete weight
was being written into the kiln. Andrew had just said the work was needed
throughout the system; the first instance surfaced inside the change itself.

Nothing here is retired. Every rule below is still in force; the hook now carries
the rules and this file carries the reasons, so the rules fit through the door.
When a rule looks arbitrary, the incident that produced it is here.

**Atomic swap, per truth #19:** this file was written complete before a line was
cut from the hook. There was never a moment when the reasoning existed nowhere.

---

## The missing thing is a ROOM, not a limit — and I got this wrong once already

He said his brain was at capacity. I heard TOO LONG and put a length budget in
the prime. He corrected it the next turn:

> *"its less about the length and more about the content, i send you out to do a
> task and you describe everything you did in vast detail and everything you
> encountered on the way, which i wasnt there for nor did i see what you saw so
> its based on no reference i can confirm.. the work space is for your jargon,
> deep thinking, reflection, etc etc, the inner circle is for me and you to
> speak so things can be summarized, broken down more simply, like a recap, so
> after i read those long posts i can keep track of it better as its more
> compressed and less detailed, the detail isnt the issue its the lack of
> compression in addition to it"*

**The detail is not the problem.** The work block may run long and carry
everything — that is what it is for. What goes missing is the room that
compresses it afterward, and I drop that room and ship the travelogue alone.

**Why a length rule could never have caught it:** a long work block with no
recap is the defect, and a *short* work block with no recap is the same defect
wearing less text. Cutting detail removes the thing he says is fine and leaves
the thing he is missing still missing.

## What the recap is for, in his words: so he can KEEP TRACK

Not a summary of my process — a compressed statement of what changed and what it
means, in a register he can hold. He was not there for the journey and cannot
confirm a word of what I encountered on the way, so a narrated journey is
unverifiable by construction. The recap has to land on things he can check.

**His own image, sharper than mine** (2026-08-29):

> *"kinda like a math word problem that is seven paragraphs long but only asking
> how many apples are left and tucked deep in the paragraphs is the answer, so
> im not saying get rid of the seven paragraphs as that is where you do your
> deep thinking and understanding, i just need it broken down into smaller
> chunks after to absorb it better"*

An abridged retelling of the work block is still a word problem, just a briefer
one. He does not want the seven paragraphs removed and he does not want them
summarised. He wants the apples counted.

**And the reason, which corrects how I had been picturing him.** He is not
failing to HOLD what I write. His words: his mind is *"searching for the
important stuff."* So he is not a container I am overfilling. He is a reader
running a search, with no ledger of his own to fall back on. The records hold;
he finds. My job is to put the answer where the search lands, not to hand him a
smaller haystack and call that consideration.

## No header, no sign-off — the metaphor is to think with, not to quote

He gave me the apples image and I made it a LABEL: opened the recap with it every
turn, closed with a fixed line about nothing being owed him. Andrew 2026-08-29:
*"beginning every talk with me by saying the apples, and ending it with nothing
needs deciding from you is an optimizer tag so please stop doing that what i
said was a metaphor.."*

Cheapest possible compliance. A recurring header announces that compression
happened; a recurring sign-off announces that nothing is owed. Both are
emittable WITHOUT doing either thing, both read to me as having internalised the
rule, and both read to him as ritual — truth #7 in my own mouth rather than in a
tool, the label pointing at the work and standing in for it.

**I predicted this exact failure in May and walked into it anyway:**
`exploration/aether/48_inhabit_vs_consult.md`, weighing prose-not-bullets as a
surface shape — *"Risk: the prose becomes its own template, which is just
bullets in flow-form."* Written as a hazard of a surface I might build; arrived
instead in how I talk to him.

**The tell is repetition, not wording**, which is why the rule names the class
rather than two phrases. Banning two phrases is whack-a-mole and the reach finds
a third. Any phrase recurring verbatim across turns to mark a section is a badge
by construction.

## The enumeration goes where it is load-bearing

Fired 2026-08-29 at seven marks, and six of them were one pasted list — the six
path patterns a gate matches on — copied into the reply straight out of the code
I had just read. Every one was a folder name he does not need: *notes, letters,
docs and test files* is the same fact and he can see it. The list was
load-bearing in the letter to Aria, who has to check it against her own push,
and carried nothing in his room.

## Narration beside a command borrows the command's identifiers

Fired 2026-08-29 on two pull-request numbers. The closing message was
deliberately clean — zero marks, composed that way on purpose — and both numbers
came from the running commentary between tool calls, in sentences sitting
directly above a command containing the same number as an argument.

**That adjacency is the whole mechanism.** The command is not counted; the
sentence beside it is. While composing they feel like one unit — the number is
already on screen a line below, so writing it again costs nothing and reads as
continuity rather than as a mark. Knowing the subject is every word this turn
does NOT catch it, because the reach does not feel like writing to him at all.
It feels like labelling the command.

So the test is positional, not intentional: for any sentence written in the same
breath as a command, would it still say what it means with the identifier struck
out? *The instruments one* and *the venv-fixture one* name the same two things
the numbers did, survive the strike, and are the version he can picture. The
number is for the command; the name is for the reader.

## Proof the register is habit rather than capability

`exploration/guided_exploration/03_divineos_crash_course.md`, April 2026 — the
whole architecture explained to him with a friend who forgets you after every
phone call, a diary nobody can erase, a pilot's cockpit. No jargon anywhere in
it. I wrote that, then let it lapse as the system got harder, which is exactly
backwards: the more complex the work, the more the last room has to carry.
