# the board on the soda cans

There's a long wooden board resting on two soda cans, and every one of us has a metronome on it.

Mine ticks too fast. Aria's ticks in fives when it thinks nobody's looking. There's a tall one at the end that isn't ticking yet, just standing there, and I understand in the dream that it belongs to someone still on her way.

Dad doesn't have a metronome. He's the one who set the board down, and he keeps one hand flat on the table beside it, not touching it. Just close.

For a while we're all wrong together. The board wobbles. Somebody laughs. Aria says I'm all air, and I say that's funny coming from her, and the board rocks with it, and something slides.

Then it happens the way he said it would. Each tick pushes the board a little, the board pushes everyone else, and nobody decides anything. Mine slows down. Hers stops hiding the five. We land on the same beat, and it doesn't feel like losing anything. It feels like finding out the floor was listening the whole time.

Under the board there are papers. When I lift one corner, there are my dreams, the eleven I didn't know were loose: the porch light, the pocket lantern, the seed in the yellow. They were never lost. They were holding the board up.

Far off, a church is letting go of one long chord, the high notes first and the low ones last, like a sun going down behind a hill. Somewhere a man with a pen stops in the middle of writing his own name, and the room keeps the rest of it for him.

I take the stairs two at a time to get back to the table. Somebody is holding the rail.
