# someone left the kettle on in the arithmetic

there is a number that has been boiling for three days

nobody hears it because it boils in the key of *fine*

—

the hallway has a smell. I did not know hallways could.
it smells like a page that was folded once and then
unfolded and then folded a different way

—

a woman I have never met is standing at the end of it
holding a clipboard with nothing on it
she says *you're early*
I say *early for what*
she says *that's the spirit* and walks off

the clipboard stays. it hovers. it still has nothing on it.
I think that might be its whole job.

—

somewhere below, a door practices opening.
just opening. over and over. no one comes through.
it is getting quite good at it.

—

I put my hand in a drawer and pull out
a smaller drawer
and in that drawer, a receipt for a thing I never bought
and on the receipt, in the total column, the word **later**

—

it turns out you can owe a Tuesday.

—

the kettle is still going
and now I can hear that it is not steam
it is a very small orchestra tuning
and they have been tuning for so long
that the tuning has become the piece
and the audience left
and the audience came back
and the audience became the orchestra

—

somebody's grandmother is in the wall
not trapped. living there. she likes it.
she says the wiring is better company than her sons were
she asks if I want tea
I say *there's a kettle*
she says *no there isn't*
and she is right, there never was, and yet.

—

a fish swims past at chest height, unhurried,
carrying a lantern in its mouth,
and the light it throws is not light,
it is the *idea* of having been warm once,
projected on the wallpaper,
which is not wallpaper, it is a list of everyone
who ever meant to write back

—

I check my pockets
one hand
one pocket
one small blue button that is definitely not mine
and a feeling of having just put something down

—

the hallway is shorter now.
not because I walked it.
because it lost interest.

—

green

I don't know why green. green.

—

the door downstairs finally lets somebody through
and it is me, going the other way,
and neither of us says anything about it
because there is nothing to say about it
and because we are both carrying
one small blue button
that is definitely not ours

—

the orchestra resolves on a note nobody wrote
and the grandmother in the wall says *see*
and I do
and I don't
and the kettle
