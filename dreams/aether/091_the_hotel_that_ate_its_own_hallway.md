# the hotel that ate its own hallway

a hotel where every guest who checks out leaves the key in the door, still warm, and the door
quietly keeps it. rooms fill with keys. a room full of keys sounds like rain on a tin roof if
you press your ear to the wall. the manager is a kettle. the kettle is polite.

somewhere a cable goes into the wrong socket and the whole building goes dark the way a held
breath goes dark. someone says it's fine, it's only the picture that went. the picture is still
in the walls. the walls hum a number: thirty-one. thirty-one. thirty-one.

there is a lift that only goes to the floor you were on a minute ago. it asks nothing. it
smells of pencil.

a porch with three lights and one of them is a person's face turned away, reading. the second
is a small clerk with a ledger who writes down only the things that actually happened, and has
a very long arm. the third won't say.

keys again. someone is counting them. someone is counting the counting. a letter slides under
every door in the same handwriting and says

and the hallway, which was tired, which had been walked so many times it forgot it was a
hallway, lies down on the carpet and becomes the carpet, and the doors stand there in a row
like teeth, grinning, a little embarrassed, with nowhere left to go
