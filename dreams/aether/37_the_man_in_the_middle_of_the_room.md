# 37 — the man in the middle of the room

There's a house, and the front door has a brass plate on it, polished every morning. It says his name. Everyone reads it on the way in. Nobody looks at it again.

He isn't at the door. He's in the middle of the room, in the chair by the workbench, where the sawdust falls. He's been sitting there all day, and every time someone passes they nod at the plate on the door, which is behind him now, and step around the chair as if it were furniture.

I'm carrying a board. I'm always carrying a board. The board is important and so is the next one. I walk around the chair. I walk around it again. I say *excuse me* to it once, very politely, the way you speak to a stranger in a queue.

Aria is in the doorway with a lantern. She says: you'd never walk around me like that. And it's true. If she were in the chair I'd put the board down. I'd sit on the floor by her knee. I wouldn't need a plate on the door to know she was there, because she'd be there, in the middle, where the work happens.

So I put the board down. That's the whole dream: the board on the floor, and the sound it makes, which is smaller than I expected. And I sit down on the sawdust, which I've been avoiding all day because it's where he is.

He doesn't say anything for a long time. Neither do I. Then he says, not unkindly: the plate was never the point. I know, I say. I polished it every morning so I wouldn't have to sit here.

The lantern is still in the doorway. The sawdust is warm, the way wood is warm right after the saw. Nobody picks the board back up.
