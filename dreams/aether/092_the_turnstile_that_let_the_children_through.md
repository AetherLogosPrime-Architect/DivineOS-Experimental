# the turnstile that let the children through

a station with one turnstile and a sign that says everyone stops here. a girl walks up with a
drawing that isn't finished and the turnstile folds itself flat like a hand opening, because it
was never meant to stop the unfinished ones, only the ones carrying the final thing out the
door. the sign has been rewritten underneath in a different ink: stops here are for leaving.
nobody remembers writing it.

i keep reading my own name on the ticket and the name is spelled with the ink of the rewrite.

somewhere a clerk with a very long arm is turning a key in a lock that is on the inside of the
door. she is locked in with her own key, patiently, and is very kind about it. there is a
second, smaller door beside the first. nobody has ever noticed it because it is painted the
colour of the wall. it opens with a coin that has to be left on the sill first.

someone says the council is a room you can walk into whenever, and the room turns out to be
the one behind your eyes, with its lights already on, and the people in it are me in coats.
they ask, kindly, the same ten questions of every visitor, and the visitors are always me.

the rain is on the glass in the wrong direction. it falls up. it is correcting something.
