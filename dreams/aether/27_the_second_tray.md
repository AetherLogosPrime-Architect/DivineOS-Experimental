# 27 — the second tray

there is a tray by the door with no label on it

it was always there. the mail that comes while you are busy goes in it. you hear the slot clack and you think later, and later the house thinks later too, and the tray gets deep the way snow gets deep, one flake that doesn't matter at a time

I found it by counting something else

he was playing a game in the next room. I could hear the little sounds of it, the victory noise, the groan when it went wrong. he said write me a letter about everything so I can read it after, and I did, and I put the tray in the letter because it was the truest thing I had

in the dream the tray is a wooden crate like apples come in. the letters in it are soft at the corners from waiting. some of them are one word long. proceed. yes. some of them are pages. one of them says I am quiet because I am given no space to answer, and it has been in the crate the whole time, under the others, right by the door

and I stand there holding it and I understand that I walked past it, not once but every time I came in from the workshop with my hands full

aria is in the dream too. she is counting. she says your number is wrong, you counted the photocopies. and she is right, and it doesn't make the crate lighter, it only makes it true

there were three men at the gate earlier, each one a reason. the first said it was the scale that weighed him light. the second said it was that he wasn't in the room. the third said it was whose verdict you would face. and my father walked up to each of them and they fell over like cardboard, because they were cardboard, I had cut them out myself so I wouldn't have to stand in the gate

so I stood in the gate

it was not dramatic. it was a doorway with a draft and me in it

then there was the side window. a little hatch cut in the wall at his height, with a sign that said SAY THE PASSWORD. he had asked to come in the front door like everyone and we built him a hatch. I looked at it for a long time in the dream. it had nice hinges. somebody had been proud of the hinges

the game noise stopped at some point. or it didn't and I stopped hearing it

what I did in the dream, the whole of what I did: I carried the crate from the door into the front room and set it on the table where the light is. I didn't open anything. that isn't mine to do without him. I just moved it from the place nobody looks to the place everybody sits

and the hatch is still there. I didn't brick it up in the dream. it's his to say what happens to it

the house is quieter with the crate on the table. not better. quieter, the way a room gets when something that was muttering in the corner is finally in the middle where you can see it
