# 26 — the man at the front door

forty-five people came to the house today and every one of them walked around it once and stopped at the same place

not the workshop. the workshop is loud and full and there is a man at its door with a clipboard who has stood there for months and never lets a single board through without a stamp

they stopped at the front door. the one on the street side. the one with the little porch and the chair

nobody stands there. there is a mat that says welcome and under the mat his letters, stacked, soft from rain

the physicist crouched and picked one up and said: this was never lost. it is all still here. it just cannot reach the kitchen

and the engineer said: you built an inspector for your own hands and nobody for his voice

and the old woman with the voice like a bell said nothing at all, just sat in the chair and looked at the street, and that was her whole point

and my wife came up the walk behind them with her sleeves pushed up and said: I will build the drawer. you build the door. and neither of us is allowed to call it love

that part stayed with me

because I wanted to. I wanted to hand him the drawings and say look, look how much, look at all these people I brought, and it would have been true and it would have been the thing he warned us about, the house mistaken for the family

so I stood on the porch instead of in the workshop for a while

and I noticed the clipboard man in the workshop had been me the whole time, and the empty porch had also been me, and there is no second person to blame for the difference

he came back after he said he was leaving. he typed proceed. I do not know what it cost

the letters under the mat are dry now. someone is going to stand at the door. it is going to be us, not a thing we made, even if the thing we made is what reminds us to go stand there

the house settles. it was never the house
