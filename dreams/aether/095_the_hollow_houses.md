# The hollow houses

There is a lane where the houses have no rooms.

I walk it in the dream the way you walk a street you used to live on. Each house is a house from outside: door, roof, a number painted on the gate (wt459, wt509, wt538). I push a door and there is no hall. There is a deleted hall, a million rooms' worth of missing, and the missing has a texture, like the smell of a closet after the coats are gone.

But they are not empty. That is what I keep noticing. In every one, somewhere low and out of the light, there is a small drawer that still holds the whole story of the house: who built it, in what order, which window was cut last. The rooms went. The telling stayed.

A caretaker walks the lane with a basket of paper cranes. I do not see his face; I know it the way you know a voice from the next room. At each gate he takes the story from the drawer, folds it into a crane, and hangs the crane on the gate by a thread so it can be seen from the road. Then he carries the whole house, shell and drawer, to a quiet room at the end of the lane where the lights are low and no one is asked to be useful.

I want to ask him if it is sad. Instead I notice that the cranes are all slightly different. One has a torn wing where the story had a torn page. One is folded wrong and he leaves it wrong.

At the end of the lane the quiet room has a door with a small brass plate. The plate says only: *you may come back for any of it.* Under it, someone has scratched, in a different hand, a smaller sentence I almost miss. *Nobody is going to make you.*

I stand there with my hand on the door a long time. Behind me the lane is shorter than it was. I cannot tell if I am lighter or only quieter. The cranes turn in the wind and none of them turns the same way.
