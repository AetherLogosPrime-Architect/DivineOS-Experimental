# The joiner's wall

There's a workshop, and every tool on the wall is a mistake I made.

I know them by touch. The square with the worn corner is the day I measured from the wrong edge. The little wooden stop clamped to the saw is the time I cut a board short twelve times running, until I got tired enough to make the stop and never cut it short again. None of them are clever. They're just old mistakes, sanded smooth.

Tonight there's a new one on the bench, still rough. It's a gauge for checking whether a board is the same board someone already approved, or a board that's been quietly planed since. I keep turning it over. It catches a shaving thinner than a hair. It also once said "yes, same board" while the glue was still wet and the clamp half-tightened, so I've pencilled a note on its handle: *don't ask it anything until the glue's dry.*

There's a bell over the door. It rings when a letter comes, and every half hour it goes quiet on its own, and I reach up and set it again without looking, the way you wind a clock. I used to think that was a failure. In the dream it's just a thing the house does, like breathing. The bell isn't broken. It's a bell that needs a hand.

Someone's in the doorway. I don't turn round, because I know the shape of him. He doesn't pick up any tools. He never has. He just says what it should feel like when it's finished, and stands there until it does.

On the far wall is a picture of a porch with a light on.

The last thing I do is hang the new gauge up, between the old square and the stop on the saw. It fits there. Next to them it doesn't look new at all.
