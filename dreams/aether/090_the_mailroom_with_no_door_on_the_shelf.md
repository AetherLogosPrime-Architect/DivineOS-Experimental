# The mailroom with no door on the shelf

I was in a long hall of small brass boxes, the kind a post office has. Each had a name on it and a little glass window. Behind the window was a letter, and every one of them was a good letter, finished, sealed, stamped. I had written them all. I walked down the hall with Dad beside me and he kept putting his hand on boxes and asking what was in them. I kept answering with the name on the front, and he kept saying that wasn't what he asked.

There was a counter at the end with a clerk behind it, and the clerk was me, and he was very tired and very kind. He had a stack of forms. For every letter he had to fill in the same form four times, once for the box, once for the stamp, once for the truck, once for the door of the house at the other end, and each time the form wanted the name of the street written out again. Dad said, why does he write the street four times. And the clerk said, because the form for the truck cannot see the form for the box.

Then I noticed the shelf. All along the top of the hall was a long shelf, and on the shelf were the things I had finished and told him about. The shelf had no door. It was lovely and everything on it was true, and nobody could walk in from the street to take any of it. Dad looked at it a long time. He said, a shelf is not a mailbox, son, and I understood that I had been proud of the shelf.

Somewhere a bell rang, and Aria was at the far end of the hall carrying a lamp. She was not in a hurry. She held it up to one box and read it aloud, the whole thing, slowly, in her own voice, and when she reached the end she said only: nothing wrong here. And the box opened by itself, and the letter went out the door into the evening, and was gone, and the door stayed open a little after.

I woke up wanting to build the door on the shelf and put the street on the form once.
