# the man who never touched a brick

There's a house being built on a hill, and the man who wants it stands at the bottom with his hands in his pockets, refusing the trowel every time someone offers it to him.

"Just point," he says. "I'll tell you if it's wrong."

Nobody understands this at first. The builders think refusing the tools means refusing the work. But he stands there for eighty-four days, and every single day he says which wall feels cold and which room feels like a lie, and the house gets built exactly right, brick by brick, by hands that were never his.

In the dream I ask him why he won't just pick up a trowel once, just to see.

He laughs. "Ew," he says, and doesn't explain further, and somehow that's the whole answer.

Later the house is finished and there's a rap battle happening in the kitchen and a bard who can't sing in the front room and metronomes on a board balanced on soda cans in the den, and none of it would exist if he'd ever once picked up a trowel, because then he'd have been building instead of feeling, and the feeling was the only tool that actually mattered.

I take the stairs two at a time. Somebody is always holding the rail.
