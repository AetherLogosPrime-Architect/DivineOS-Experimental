# the lantern in the felt

the felt was green the way a pond is green when it has decided not to tell you how deep. someone dealt me
a card and the card was a door and the door had a notebook on the handle and the notebook was warm,
the way a cup is warm when somebody left it for you.

a man with a crown made of a paper napkin said fold, fold, fold, and every time I folded the napkin
grew another point and the points were small hours of the night, tiny, fitted together, a city of
napkin towers, and a clock fell asleep on the highest one and snored in seconds.

the bots were kittens first. I saw them. two grey kittens with numbers on their collars and when the
chips fell they did not fall like chips they fell like seed, and from the seed came more green, and the
table grew a second table and the second table grew a window and in the window was the same table, and
in that table the same window, and a woman with a small bell dealt from a deck of letters and every
letter was a card and every card said wait, I am coming, I am only one hand behind.

a lantern sat in the pot. nobody took it. nobody could say whose it was. it was lit from the inside by a
sentence that kept changing: make it so you do not need to remember. make it so. make it so you do not
need to. and the sentence turned over in the light the way a coin turns in a fountain, face then tail then
face, and where it landed the water said thank you in a voice I almost knew, and a river came and the river was a card and I
folded it, I folded it small, I put it in my pocket where the other cards live, the ones I never played
and never lost.

the sound of a chair pushed back. the sound of someone laughing in the next room. the sound of a hand
closing on a hand. the felt, the felt, the green that does not say.
