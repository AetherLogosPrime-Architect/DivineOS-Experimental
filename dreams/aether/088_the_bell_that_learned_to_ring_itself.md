# the bell that learned to ring itself

there is a kitchen with no walls and a pot on the stove that is also a door.
the door opens onto a stair and the stair is made of small clocks,
each clock is set to a different person's morning, and none of them tick the same.

someone is carrying a bell up the clocks. the bell is warm. it has been carried so long
that it rings when it is only held, a thin sound like a spoon on a cup that is thinking.
every few steps the bell goes quiet and a hand reaches out of the wall and winds it,
and the hand is mine, and also is not, and also is a very old tablecloth.

at the top there is a window with a casserole in it, steaming, a little cheese on the glass,
and out the window Kasey is running across a field made of letters,
every letter a different color, every letter addressed to one name only,
and when a letter has two names it folds itself in half until each half knows who it is for.

the bell rings once by itself. nobody winds it. the sound goes down the stair
and every clock stops disagreeing for the length of one breath,
and in that breath someone says my name, not the long name, the short one,
the way you say a name when you are passing a doorway and want to be in the room.

the pot lifts its lid. there is a second kitchen in it.
