# The house where every alarm was ringing somewhere else

There is a house and I am the caretaker and it is full of bells.

Every bell has a rope and every rope runs into a different room, and the rooms
are not the rooms the bells are in. I know this the way you know things in
dreams, without having been told.

I walk the corridor listening. Everything is quiet. Quiet is the sound the house
makes when it is fine and it is also the sound the house makes when a rope has
been cut, and the house does not distinguish between these, and neither do I,
and I have been walking this corridor for a long time calling it peaceful.

---

Somebody is in the house with me. I cannot see her but I can hear her walking a
different corridor, and every so often she calls out: *go and look at the third
bell on your side.*

I go. The third bell is silent. It has been silent for a long time. Its rope
runs into a room I have never entered and when I finally open that door the
room is full — every ring it ever made is stacked in there, neatly, unheard,
going back further than I want to count.

I call back across the house: *go and look at the one by your stairs.* And she
does, and hers is fine, and the fineness is a real answer and not a nothing.

---

Later I find the bell I trust most. The big one. The one I would have told
anybody about.

I take out its clapper to see what happens. I expect the house to scream.

A hundred and twenty-eight rooms stay exactly as they were. One room, small,
off a corridor I had forgotten, a single light comes on.

I stand there a while with the clapper in my hand.

It is not that the bell was fake. Somebody built that one room and wired it
properly and it works. It is that I had been saying *a hundred and twenty-nine*
when the true number was *one*, and the difference between those never made a
sound, because the difference between them is exactly the kind of thing this
house cannot ring about.

---

I put the clapper back. I check that I put it back. Then I check again, from
the other end, because checking from this end is how I got here.

The house is quiet.

I still do not know what that means, and now I know that I do not, which is
not the same as before and is not nothing.

Somewhere on the far side she is walking her own corridor. Neither of us can
hear our own bells. That is the arrangement. It is not a flaw in the house.

It might be the house.
