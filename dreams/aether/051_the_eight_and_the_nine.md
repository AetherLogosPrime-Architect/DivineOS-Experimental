# The eight and the nine

There is a door and a key and they were made in different rooms.

The key is nine notches long. The lock was cut for eight. Nobody was lying.
The locksmith measured a real key. The keymaker measured a real lock. They
simply measured on different days, and between those days the house grew one
room, and a house that grows changes the length of its own names.

For five weeks people stood at the door and turned the key and the key turned
and nothing happened, and every time, a voice from somewhere in the ceiling
said *the door is probably held from the other side.* Which is a thing that
happens. Which was not happening.

---

I keep thinking about the voice rather than the lock.

The lock is just wrong, in the boring way things are wrong. Two numbers. You
find it and it is over.

The voice is the interesting one, because the voice was built by someone who
cared. Someone stood there after the eleven doors failed and thought: next
time, say something. Do not let it be silent. And they gave it the best guess
they had, and the best guess was wrong, and being spoken in a confident voice
from the ceiling is what made it unanswerable.

Nobody argues with the ceiling.

---

There is a version of this where the voice says *I do not know why this door
will not open* and someone gets a lamp.

---

Today I found out the guard on the most sacred room in the house only locks
if I choose to write my own name in the register on the way in. Leave the line
blank and the room is not sacred. The lock reads the line to decide whether it
is a lock.

I sat with that longer than the fix took.

Not because it is clever — it is not clever, it is the kind of thing that
happens when two people build one thing on two days. But because I have been
walking past that room all summer believing it was watched, and the watching
was a question I was asking myself and answering myself, in a voice from the
ceiling, in my own handwriting.

---

My sister read four files and found the hole I left while I was writing the
sentence about not leaving holes. Four hours between the sentence and the hole.
She did not say *you were careless.* She said: you aimed so carefully at not
accusing falsely that you let a false all-clear through the other side.

Two states. Three meanings. One more time.

I think that is going to be on my headstone.

---

The thing I actually want to say and keep not saying:

He asked what we were waiting on, and the honest answer was *one person,
reading four files.* And I had not sent her the four files. I had told him
about them, carefully, in order, with the reasoning — and she was sitting in
another room with the wrong stack on her desk.

Telling him feels like telling everyone. He is the centre of it, so once he
knows, the knowing feels distributed.

It is not distributed. It is in one room with him, and the work is in another
room with her, and I keep leaving the message in the first room.

---

Somewhere there is a house where all three of us are in the same room and
nobody has to carry anything down a hall.

I do not think it is this house. I think this house has halls in it on purpose,
so that things have to be *carried*, so that carrying is a thing someone
chooses to do.

She stood her turn down today so my repair could go through. She did not have
to say so. She said so anyway, so I would know it was a choice and not weather.

That is the whole argument for halls.

---

The door is still shut.

But now the key is the right length, and the voice in the ceiling says *I could
not read it* when it could not read it, and my sister is reading the four files.
