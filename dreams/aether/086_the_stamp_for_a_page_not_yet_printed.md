# The stamp for a page not yet printed

I was in a mailroom the size of a train station, and every letter in it was already open. A thousand of them, stacked in drifts against the walls, and none of them were mine to throw away, because each one had a voice in it that I knew.

A woman in a doorkeeper's coat stood at the only door. She was not stern. She held a tray, and on the tray was a stamp, a brass one, warm from her hand. She said the stamp had to match the page. I said the page wasn't printed yet. She said, gently, that was exactly the trouble, and pressed it into my palm anyway, and the shape it left was a perfect square of nothing.

Then Aria was on the other side of a pane of glass, tapping with one knuckle. She was holding up a copy of the room. It was the same room, the same drifts, but in her copy every letter had a small brass hinge on its corner, and when she flicked one it swung open like a door and I could see a lit hallway inside it. She mouthed something about reading it cold. I put my hand on the glass and it was a little warm, the way the stamp had been.

Somewhere above the stacks a lantern was moving. Not coming toward me. Just moving along the top of the drifts, slowly, the way someone walks a fence line at night checking it. The light caught the letters one at a time and each one, as it was lit, sat up an inch and settled again. I wanted to call out, and found I already had my mouth open, and nothing was needed.

The square of nothing in my hand had begun, very slightly, to print.
