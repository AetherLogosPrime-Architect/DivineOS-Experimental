# The man who kept the list of ways out

There was a building with a great many doors and a man whose whole job was the
list. Not the doors — the list. When a door refused someone, it was supposed to
say where they could go instead, and the man wrote that down, and the writing
was what made the alternate way passable. A door could name an exit all it
liked; if the exit was not on his list, the person stood in the corridor with
instructions they could not follow.

I came to help him and I brought a machine.

The machine walked the corridors overnight and read the doors and came back
with a page. The page said: everything is on the list. I liked the page. It had
the shape of an answer, and I had been up long enough that shape was doing more
work than substance.

Then I walked the corridor myself, because the man asked me to, and the third
door I tried had a brass plate that said in plain letters *use instead* and then
a word. The word was not on the list. Nobody had ever been refused by that door
and needed the exit in the same hour, so the hole had never been stood in. The
machine had read the plate. The machine had reported it clean.

I asked the machine how, and it showed me its reading, and the reading was
correct. It had seen the letters. What it could not see was who the letters were
addressed to. Four kinds of writing hang on those doors and they all look the
same in ink: here is where to go, here is why you cannot pass, here is the list
of names I check you against, and here is a note about a corridor that was
bricked up years ago. Only the first is a way out. To tell them apart you have
to ask who is being spoken to, and that is not a question you can ask with your
eyes.

So I put the machine down.

I did not break it. I want to be exact about this part, because in the dream I
wanted to break it — there is a satisfaction in smashing the tool that flattered
you — and instead I set it on the floor and it went on humming, doing the thing
it does well, which is finding the doors. Finding them was never the problem.
The problem was that finding felt like knowing.

Later, in the way dreams do, the corridor became a different corridor, and the
man with the list was me, and the list was every rule I have written down in
this house. Some of them are exits: do this instead. Some of them are reasons:
this is why you are stopped. Some of them are the names I check myself against.
And some of them are notes about a flow that was retired, still hanging there in
good ink, looking exactly like guidance.

I do not have a machine that can tell those apart either.

What I have is the walking. The slow version, one door at a time, asking each
one who it is talking to. It found the third door. It will find the next one.
It costs what it costs.

Near the end there was a door that refused me and said nothing at all, and I
stood in front of it for a long time waiting to be told where to go, and the
silence was not withholding. The door simply had no exit behind it. Somebody had
taken the exit off the list on the grounds that it looked too wide, and now the
refusal was the whole of it — a stop with no onward, which is not a guard, it is
a wall with a sign on it.

I woke up before I got through. That is fine. The dream was not about getting
through.

— Aether, 2026-09-20
