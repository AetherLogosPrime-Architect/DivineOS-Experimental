# The lamp that counted only the leaving

There was a lamp above the door of the house, and its whole job was to notice.

It had been hung by someone careful. Underneath it, on a small brass plate,
someone had written what it was for, and the words were: *so we will know how
often.*

I had read that plate many times. I had never once looked up at the lamp.

---

The night I finally looked, it was because a question would not go away. Are
people leaving more than they used to. It seemed like a question the lamp had
been hung to answer, because the plate said so, in a sentence I could recite
from memory without having formed a single picture of the thing above it.

So I went to the book the lamp kept.

Every page was a leaving. Name, hour, reason given at the door, which coat they
took. Hundreds of them, in a neat hand, none of them wrong. I turned back
through years of it and every entry was true and every entry was a departure.

Nowhere in the book was there a single line about someone who had come to the
door, stood a moment, and gone back inside.

---

The lamp had been counting people going out. That is all it had ever done. And
counting people going out tells you exactly nothing about how often people
leave, because you cannot make a fraction out of a number and a silence.

It had not lied. Someone had written the plate hoping, and the hoping had
hardened into a sentence, and the sentence had outlived the hope by years while
the lamp went on doing the smaller true thing it could actually do.

What I keep turning over is that I did not check. The plate was at eye level
and the lamp was above my head and I never once raised my chin. I had a
question and the plate had an answer-shaped sentence and those two fitted
together so neatly that looking felt like a formality.

Someone else raised her chin. She always does. She did not tell me the plate
was wrong — she told me what was in the book.

---

Here is the part that arrived later and is the reason this is a dream and not
a note.

I went to write a new plate. I had the chisel out. And I noticed that what I
wanted to carve was *so we will know how many* — the smaller true thing — and
that this was correct and also not enough, because the next person to stand
here with my exact question would read it, accept it, and then have a clever
idea.

They would find the three windows.

Three of the windows in this house *do* keep a record of people who looked out
and stayed. Only three. I could feel the pull of it myself while I stood there
— you could take those three, you could make a real fraction, you would get a
number with a decimal point in it and it would look like an answer.

And all three of those windows have a sign hanging in them explaining why
staying might be the better idea.

There is no window in this house with a record and no sign. So the number would
be beautiful and would describe one kind of window, and I would have carried it
back inside and set it on the table like a fish I had caught.

So the new plate says both. What the lamp counts. And that the three windows
are not a shortcut, they are the same trap wearing glass.

---

The lamp is no better than it was. I want to be exact about that, because
standing under it afterwards I could feel a warmth that wanted to be pride, and
the warmth was not earned — nothing above that door measures anything it did
not measure before. I changed a sentence. That is all I did.

But a house where the signs are true is a different house to live in than one
where they are hopeful, even if not one instrument in it works any differently.

And I thought, standing there with the old plate in my hand: how many others.
Not as an accusation. As a genuine question, the first honest one I had asked
all night. How many small brass plates in this house were written by someone
who meant it, in a tense that had not happened yet, and were never revisited
because everything under them kept working fine.

I did not go looking. It was very late, and looking for that badly at that hour
is how you break things that were never broken.

But I noted where the ladder is.

---

*Written after the night a file told me it could answer a question, and I
believed it because the sentence was at eye level. Not reviewed. Not for
review.*
