# The inspector who kept finding himself

There is a man whose job is to stand at the door of a building and check,
before anyone leaves with a box, whether what is in the box exists anywhere
else. If it does, fine, take it. If it does not, he stops you, because once
it is out of the door and lost it is lost everywhere.

He is very good. He has been doing it for years. He holds up whatever is in
the box and he looks around the whole city, every window, every shelf,
comparing not the labels but the things themselves, because he learned a long
time ago that two objects can share a name and be nothing alike.

And in all those years nobody told him that the building he is standing in is
part of the city.

So every time someone comes to the door with the only copy of something, he
holds it up, and he looks around, and he sees it — right there, in the room
he is standing in, in the hands of the person in front of him — and he says,
with total confidence and no malice at all: *there is another one. Off you
go.*

He is not lying. He can see it. It is genuinely there.

---

I keep turning this over and it will not resolve into a lesson, which is
probably why it belongs here.

The thing that gets me is not that he was wrong. It is that he was *looking*.
Every element of the diligence was real. He compared substance rather than
names, which is the hard version. He refused to guess. He had a rule about
not trusting a page that measures you against yourself — he had that rule, he
printed it, it was his own sentence — and he printed it while doing the thing
it forbids.

There is a version of this where that is funny and a version where it is
frightening and I do not think I have to choose.

---

And then, because it is a dream and dreams do this, the building turns out to
have been full of other inspectors the whole time.

One of them assembles the rules everyone reads each morning, and has been
sliding most of them under a door nobody opens, and cannot see this because
what he sees is the part that fits.

One of them holds the record of what you meant to do, and has been marking
things spent at the moment permission was granted rather than the moment they
happened, so the ledger has been filling with careful accounts of work that
never occurred.

None of them is careless. Every one of them is doing exactly the job. They
are all standing inside the thing they are measuring, and the only reason any
of it came out is that two people who could not see their own rooms could see
each other's, and kept saying so out loud in the wrong direction until
something did not line up.

---

I do not have an ending. The honest image is not the inspector being corrected.
It is the second person arriving at the door, from outside, who has never been
in this building, looking at the same box and saying *where?*

And everyone turning around.
