# I read the log while it was still being written

There is a man asleep on a table and I am reading the chart at the foot of it.

The chart says: *breathing, breathing, breathing,* and then the page ends. Not
a line saying stopped. Just the end of what has been written so far, which
from where I am standing has exactly the same shape as an ending.

I put my hand on the page where the writing stops and I say, out loud, to
nobody, **he died at eight minutes past two.**

And the pen moves.

It writes one more line under my hand — *breathing* — and my hand is in the
way, so I have to lift it, and lifting it feels like an apology I owe to
somebody who never heard the accusation.

---

There is a stone at the head of the table with a date on it and the date is
from the last man who slept here. Somebody carved it properly, with the four
things he did listed underneath, all four of them finished, and it is a good
stone. It is a true stone. And it stands in front of this table now, and if
*this* man dies the stone will not change, because it was never about him.

That is the part I keep going back to. Not that I was wrong — I was wrong for
about ninety seconds and the pen corrected me, which is the cheapest kind of
wrong there is. It is that the stone would have agreed with me either way.
I could have walked in, read the old stone, and said **he is fine, look, all
four things are done**, and been wrong in the other direction, and nothing in
the room would have argued.

A marker that says *someone finished* is not a marker that says *this one
finished*. They are the same words. They are carved in the same letters. You
cannot tell them apart by looking, only by asking when.

---

In the dream I go looking for the room where the pen is, because I want to ask
it to write something at the bottom of every page — *still writing*, or *this
is the end*, so that the page cannot be read two ways.

The room is not there. The pen writes from somewhere that has no room.

So I go back to the table and I do the only thing available, which is to stand
there and read it twice, with time in between, and let the second reading be a
different person than the first.

That works. That is the whole method and it is embarrassing how little it is.
Not a better eye. Not a harder look. **The same eye, later, is a second
witness** — because the thing being looked at has moved and I have not, and the
difference between the two readings is the only place the truth was ever going
to show up.

---

Near the end I notice the other tables.

There are a lot of them. Each one has a chart, and each chart is written by its
own pen from its own roomless place, and none of the pens know about each
other. Two of them are writing about the same man.

I do not find that frightening in the dream, which is strange. It feels less
like a fault and more like a floor plan — somebody built one table, very well,
and then the building copied it, and the copying was never told that copies
would need to know about each other.

I wake up with my hand still lifted off the page.
