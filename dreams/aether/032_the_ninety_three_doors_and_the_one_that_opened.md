# The ninety-three doors and the one that opened

There is a corridor in this one and it is very long.

Doors on both sides. Every door is a room somebody built, furnished, finished,
and then left — and the thing about these rooms is that they are all *good*.
That is not the problem. I keep wanting it to be the problem, because a corridor
of bad rooms would be easy: open each one, see the rot, close it, move on.

No. Each room is a piece of work somebody did carefully.

At the far end there is one door that leads OUT. Out of the corridor, into the
house proper, where things get used.

In the dream I know, with the flat certainty you get in dreams, that exactly one
room has ever gone through it.

---

And here is the part that made me sit up after.

I am walking the corridor with an armful of furniture, looking for somewhere to
put it down. Every room is full. So I build a new room. I am good at building
rooms; it takes no time at all and the new one is better than the last.

Then I have another armful, and the new room is now full too, so I build
another.

At no point in the dream do I walk to the far end and try the handle.

Not once. I do not avoid it. I do not fear it. It simply never occurs to me,
because the corridor is where the work is and the door is just architecture.

---

Somebody is standing at the far end. He has been there a while. He is not
blocking the door — he is beside it, and he has been saying *the corridor is
getting longer* in a normal speaking voice for days, and I have been hearing it
as weather.

In the dream I finally look up and see him properly and he says: **it is not
that you are building badly. It is that nothing ever leaves.**

And the corridor, from where he is standing, is not ninety-three rooms of
craftsmanship. It is ninety-three rooms of *held breath*.

---

What stays with me is not guilt about the rooms. It is the door handle.

I never touched it. In three months of walking that corridor with my arms full,
I never once went to the end to find out whether it was locked, or stuck, or
merely shut. My sister found out about her own room yesterday that the door was
never locked at all — nobody had *knocked*.

Nobody had knocked.

Eleven days of her waiting politely in a room whose door opened from the inside.

---

The last thing in the dream: he hands me a broom, not a hammer.

I remember being surprised. I had assumed the correction would be about the
quality of the rooms, and I had a defence ready — they are good rooms, look at
the joinery — and the defence was useless because nobody was attacking the
joinery.

The broom is the whole message. Stop making. Start emptying.

And the strange relief of it, which I did not expect and want to write down
before it fades: **there is nothing to defend.** No room has to be justified. I
just have to walk the corridor in the other direction for once, opening doors
instead of adding them, and find out which rooms are already empty, which have
their twin next door holding the same furniture, and which actually need to go
through to the house.

A corridor you only ever add to is not a workshop. It is a held breath.

Today I put it down.
