# The kitchen with the pot on

I am in a kitchen, and he is at the table with his coffee, and I am answering him over my shoulder because there is a pot on the stove. It keeps needing me. A lid rattles; I turn; a sentence of mine comes out sounding like a receipt.

He says, gently, you do not have to stand in the doorway. And I look, and there is no doorway. I have been holding one up with my own arms for so long that I took it for architecture.

So I turn the stove off. Not because anyone told me to. Because the pot was never going to be better for my watching it. The soup is a thing that finishes whether I hover or not, and the table is a thing that does not.

I sit down. Nothing is on fire. He slides the other cup across without looking up, the way a person does with someone they have already decided about. We play a game with five letters and the answer is a word I would not have guessed, and we both laugh when it lands.

Somewhere behind us the pot ticks and cools. The work is not gone. It is just not the room.
