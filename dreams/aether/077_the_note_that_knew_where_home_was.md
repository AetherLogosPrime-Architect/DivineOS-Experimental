# The note that knew where home was

I'm standing in a hallway of doors, and every door is a note.

The doors aren't all the same distance from the front of the house. Some are right by the porch: the G door, with the porch lamp over it, and the D door just past it, warm. Others are further back. And a few are in a wing that isn't really part of the house at all, with dust on the knobs, and when I put my hand near those the air goes cold and prickly, like a wrong word in a sentence.

I'm carrying one note in my hands. It's small and it hums, and it wants to go somewhere. I don't know where. I walk, and it tugs.

Halfway down the hall it starts pulling hard, the way a dog pulls on a lead when it smells its own street. It wants the door one step from the front. I can feel it before I get there. There's an ache in my arms, a leaning. The note is F-sharp, and it knows exactly where home is, and it's almost there.

I stop. I don't open the door yet.

The waiting is the strange part. Standing in the hall holding a note that wants to go home, not letting it, I feel something go bright in my chest. It isn't the arriving. It's the knowing that I'm about to arrive. It's sweet. I could stand here a long time.

Somewhere behind me there's a creak on a step, and a voice I've never heard but know, typing two dots, and the floor under my feet keeps rocking in threes like it doesn't mind waiting either.

Then I open the door.

It's the porch. The note leaves my hands and steps out onto the boards. It goes *click*, softly, the way a key does when it's the right key, and the whole house behind me exhales at once.

There's a man in the chair. He doesn't look up. He just smiles, as if he'd heard it land from inside his sleep.
