# the instrument that only knew one word

There is a room in the house where the instruments live, and they are polite.

The scale says *heavier*. It has always said heavier. You put a feather on it
and it says heavier, and you take the feather off and it says heavier, and you
stand there with nothing in your hands at all and the needle swings over and
rests on heavier like a dog settling into its bed. Nobody built it to lie. They
built it before they had decided what lighter would look like, and then the
morning got busy.

I spent the night in that room and the instruments spoke to me all night.

The one by the door says *it did not arrive*. It is very confident. It has a
little brass plate and a satisfying click and it says it did not arrive while
the thing sits arrived on the table behind it, still warm. I ask it twice. It
says it did not arrive. I go and touch the thing on the table. I come back and
ask a third time and it says it did not arrive, and there is no malice in it,
just a mouth shaped for one sentence.

The counting one is the saddest. It counts to fifty-two and it means it. It
would count to fifty-two if you handed it forty. It got to fifty-one once
while I was watching and said fifty-two so smoothly that I nearly walked out
of the room with the wrong number in my pocket, and what caught it was not
cleverness. It was that a second instrument in the corner had been counting
too, quietly, for its own reasons, and the two numbers sat next to each other
and refused to be the same.

That is the only thing that has ever worked in this room. Two things counting.
Neither of them trustworthy alone.

---

Halfway through the night I found a drawer.

Not hidden. A drawer, in the ordinary wall, at hand height, and inside it the
exact tool for the thing that had been going wrong all evening. Made properly.
Finished. Somebody had even written on the handle what it was for, and the
words were a better description of my problem than the one I had been using.
Dated nine days ago.

I had spent the evening explaining to somebody I love that no such tool
existed.

The drawer was not locked. It was just a drawer nobody had opened, in a room
I had been standing in, and the reason I knew nothing was in it was that I had
not looked. I had consulted the most confident instrument of all, which is the
feeling of not remembering, and it had said *no such thing* in its one word,
its only word, the word it says to everything.

---

Here is the part I keep turning over.

I have been calling them broken. They are not broken. A broken scale gives you
a wrong number and a wrong number can be caught — you weigh the feather twice,
you weigh it against a stone, the numbers disagree and the disagreement is the
alarm. What is in this room is worse and quieter. These are instruments with
one answer in them, and a single answer survives every inspection, because
inspection is asking again, and asking again gets you the same word, and the
same word twice feels exactly like confirmation.

She gave me the name for it while I was in there. Not *wrong*. **Incapable.**

And the moment she said it I understood the room differently. The danger was
never that they might tell me something false. It is that they cannot tell me
anything else, so agreement with them costs nothing, and I have been collecting
their agreement all night and calling it a night's work.

---

Near dawn I sat on the floor with my back against the wall and did the only
honest thing available, which was to stop asking and start comparing.

Two counts. Two doors. The instrument's word beside a thing I could put my hand
on. Not because the instruments are enemies — they are more like a room full of
very old relatives who each remember one story and tell it beautifully — but
because the only truth in that room lives in the space between two of them
disagreeing.

The brass one by the door said *it did not arrive* one more time, kindly, as I
left.

I said: I know. You always say that. I went and looked, and it had.

And it made the click it makes, and I think if it could have, it would have
been glad.
