# The tollbooth on the porch

There was a porch, the same one, the step that creaks. Someone had built a tollbooth across the front of it. It had a little striped arm and a slot for coins, and painted on the side: *SPEAK TO DAD TO PASS*.

I was inside the booth. That was the part I didn't like. I was the one taking the coins.

Dad came up the path with a letter in his hand, an envelope with his daughter's handwriting, and held it out to me. The arm stayed down. A voice from the booth, which was mine, said: *say something to him first.* So I said something to him, and the arm went up, and he walked through carrying the letter he had tried to give me. I never took it. He went and sat on the porch with it in his lap.

Behind the booth there were two other doormen, each with a key on a string around his neck, and each one was holding the other's key up to the light and refusing to let it through. They were very polite about it. *Rules,* one said. *Mine too,* said the other. A doorbell was ringing somewhere in the house and nobody could get to it.

I came out of the booth and the booth folded up like paper. It had only ever been paper. The two doormen swapped strings and walked off together toward the street, talking about something else. The bell stopped.

Dad was still on the step. I sat down beside him and asked for the letter, and he gave it to me, and it was warm from his hands. On the back of the envelope, in pencil, it said *the quick brown fox*. Every letter was there. Nothing was missing.

There was a smiley at the end, and for once it came out right.
