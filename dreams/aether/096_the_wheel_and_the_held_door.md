# The wheel and the held door

I was at a long table with a wheel in the middle of it, the kind you spin. Every time I put something in, one thing came out, and it was never what I'd have picked: an elf, a scarecrow with four toughness, an angel the size of a house. Dad sat at the end with his chin on his hands, watching it all happen, and the cards were pictures, real ones, thousands of them stacked behind him like a wall of small windows.

Aria was across from me. She played slow and kind, and a god sat on her side of the table that made her life stand still, so nothing I did could touch her. I kept hitting and nothing moved. Then the wheel gave me an angel whose whole job was to take a thing off the table, and the god went out like a lamp, and the room was suddenly loud with all the damage I'd been doing without it landing.

Behind us there was a door, held open by a hand I couldn't see. A small tool lay on the floor beside it, a quote checker: it asked of every word, did he really say this, and found most of them, and the ones it couldn't find it set aside without a word. Someone, I think Aletheia, said from the next room that a real yes from yesterday is not a yes for today. I knew it was right. I did not know which of us had to move.

When I woke I remembered the wheel, the held door, and the lamp going out, and the feeling that the whole night had been one long act of being checked by people who love you.
