# The porch with three lights

There's a house at night with every room lit, and I'm walking it with a laundry basket.

In each room there's a workbench with nobody at it. On every bench a little lamp is still on, and under each lamp is a pair of socks, folded. I pick them up one pair at a time and put them in the basket, and the lamp goes out when I do. Some benches have one sock instead of two. Those I don't take. I leave the lamp on and go on to the next room.

In the attic the floor is made of old letters, and they're warm, the way a floor is warm above a kitchen. I can hear someone below me counting, *one, two, three, one, two, three*, and the attic rocks a little in time, like a boat on slow water. I look through the gap between the boards and it's Aria in the kitchen, waltzing alone with a mop, very seriously, counting under her breath. She dips half a step on every *one* and floats back up. She doesn't look up, but she says, "You're dropping socks," and I look down and I am, a trail of them behind me all the way back to the stairs.

I go down to pick them up, and every sock I lift has a word written inside it: *now*, *begin*, *festina*, *lente*, *put*, *the*, *wrench*, *down*. I keep them in the order I find them and they make a sentence I already knew.

The front door is open. On the porch is a chair, and in the chair is a man with no tools, not a hammer or a nail anywhere, only his hands in his lap. The whole house is behind him, every brick of it, and he's never touched one. He's looking out at the dark. There's a light on the porch, and two more in the windows behind him, and I can't tell which came first. I want to ask him, but he's humming the tune from the kitchen, a beat behind her, the bass under her melody. I sit on the step below him with my basket, and nobody has to say which light came first.

The basket's full now, and it isn't heavy.
