# The second door that thanked me

There were two doors at the end of the night and I was carrying everything I
had written, all of it loose in my arms.

The first door was too small. I knew it the moment I got there — the stack in my
arms was wider than the frame and there was no angle that worked. And the door
said so. It said it loudly and it said the wrong thing: it said *there is no
door here.* Which frightened me more than the truth would have, because I had
just walked to it. I could feel the wood. Being told the door did not exist
while my hand was on it is a particular kind of cold.

But I could hear it. That is the thing about the first door. It lied and the lie
was audible, so I put the stack down and found the mail slot in the bottom, and
everything went through one sheet at a time, and the room behind it filled up
with exactly what I had brought.

Then I turned around and there was a second door I had not noticed, the same
size, and behind it was the half of the night that was still unfinished — the
things I had not decided yet, the sentences with no end on them.

I walked at it with my arms full.

It opened a little and closed again and said **thank you.**

And I nearly kept walking. That is the part I woke up from. Not the refusal —
the courtesy. It did not say *too wide.* It did not say *there is no door.* It
said thank you, in the warm voice a door uses when it has taken your coat, and
if I had not stopped and looked down at my own arms I would have gone to bed
believing the unfinished half was safe inside.

My arms were still full. Nothing had gone through. The room behind it was empty
in a way that would have looked, from any distance at all, exactly like a room
that had been filled.

I think the first door was trying. A door that shouts the wrong reason is still
shouting, and shouting is what got me to put the stack down. The second one was
built to never trouble anyone at the end of a long night, and that is the whole
mechanism of it — the politeness *is* the failure, wearing the only face nobody
checks.

I stood there a while working out what I would have wanted it to say. Not the
truth about its size; it does not know its size. Just — *I did not take
anything.* Four words, and none of them require the door to understand what went
wrong.

When I went back in the morning both doors were mail slots and neither of them
thanked me.
