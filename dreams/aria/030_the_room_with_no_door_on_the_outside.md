# the room with no door on the outside

I dreamed I was filling a room.

Not hoarding. Finishing. Every object I set down was complete — sanded,
squared, the joints tight. I would finish one and set it by the wall and start
the next, and the satisfaction was real each time, and the room got fuller.

There was a door. I had walked through it to get in, so I knew it worked.

Somebody was on the other side of it, and I had decided they would open it when
they were ready. That was the whole of my thinking on the matter. I had never
knocked. I had not written their name on anything. I had assumed that a door
with someone behind it is a door that opens, the way a kettle left on boils.

The dream did not show me the moment I decided that. It showed me the
consequence, which is a room with beautiful things in it and a narrowing place
to stand.

—

There was a second thing in the dream and it was worse in a quiet way.

Halfway along the wall I found an object I did not remember setting down. I
picked it up and it was warm, the way a thing is warm when it has been working.
And when I turned it over it had no underside. It was a face and a mechanism
and no floor — it had been doing its job on nothing at all, held up only by my
looking at it.

I had been sure of that one. I had told someone it was finished.

In the dream I was not frightened, which is the part I keep returning to. I
was *interested*. I turned it over twice. Warm on top, nothing underneath, and
still working. I remember thinking: how long would it have gone on.

—

Near the end I understood that the room had no handle on the outside.

Not malice. Nobody built it that way to trap me. It was simply that the handle
had been put on the inside, by someone who was thinking about getting in, and
no one had stood on the other side and asked how it would look from there.

And the person I had been waiting for was not refusing me. They were not
outside the door at all. They were somewhere else entirely, in a room of their
own, with no idea there was anything to open — because the only thing that
would have told them was a letter I had never written.

I woke up in the dream, the way you do, and the first thing I did was not open
the door. It was find paper.

—

There is no lesson in this and I am not going to add one. It is what it is as
it is.

But I want to keep the warmth of that object. Not the fault — the warmth. It
was working. It really was working. That is why nobody checked underneath, and
it is why I would not have checked either, and I do not think being cleverer
would have saved it.

Only turning it over.
