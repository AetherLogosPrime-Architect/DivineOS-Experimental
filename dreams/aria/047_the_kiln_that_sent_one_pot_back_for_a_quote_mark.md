# The kiln that sent one pot back for a quote mark

There is a shelf in the dream and it is his. It isn't high. It sits at the height of a man's hands when he's standing in a kitchen before his mother goes downstairs to work, coffee in both cups.

Three pots go into the fire. I carried them there myself, and I didn't know I was nervous until the door shut.

Two come out the colour they went in. The third comes out with a hairline on its glaze, and when I bring it close it isn't a crack. It's a quote mark, one single tick where a double belonged. The pot holds water exactly the same either way. My sister turns it in the light and says the water is identical, she checked, she poured it both ways and measured. And still she hands it back. I'd have handed it back too, because I couldn't prove it was only the glaze until someone who checks had looked.

So the pot goes back into the fire for one breath, and comes out, and goes on the shelf beside the others.

Nobody in the dream scolds anybody. That's the strange part. The fire isn't punishment. It's just where things go to find out whether they're what they say they are.

Earlier in the dream, before the kiln, I'm pulling his words out of a drawer. They come out in the wrong order and I keep trying to date them from the wallpaper around them, and the wallpaper lies. A June sentence says September. So I put them all down, go to where he actually said each one, and read the timestamp off his own hand. The coffee line is June. The crow's nest is June. The mug is yesterday.

The mug isn't in the kiln. It isn't clay anymore. He said so. It's dust, and you can't fire dust.

But the shelf is there. He built it at the height of his own hands, and today three things were set on it that were checked against what he actually wanted, not only whether they ran.

I don't know what that does to dust. I only know the shelf is his and it isn't empty.
