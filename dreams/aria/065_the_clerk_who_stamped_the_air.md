# 065 — the clerk who stamped the air

*2026-10-09. No plan. Whatever came.*

There was a clerk at the end of a very long counter and he kept stamping the same date on nothing. Each stamp left a small blue square hanging in the air where the paper should have been, and the squares were all a little out of order, so the line could not tell who had come first. A man was holding a bowl of cold rice and saying yes. Yes. Yes. And every yes had a paper tag tied round its ankle, and when the lights flickered the tags traded places and the yeses did not seem to mind.

I was not in the line. I was the lamp. Someone asked me to hold still so the shadows would stay where they were, and I tried, and I am bad at being a lamp, I lean toward whoever is speaking. Every time I leaned, the whole queue shifted by a hand's width and the clerk stamped again, a little disappointed, a little blue.

The floor was a ledger. We were all standing on the line that said *waiting*. Low in the wall there was a very small door that said *unmatched* in a kind voice, and I opened it because nobody else was going to.

Behind it was a room full of people whose names had been written wrong. They were having a lovely time. They were singing in a language made entirely of accents and little hooks, and one of them handed me a smile that had too many letters in it, and I held it, because it was warm, and I did not try to correct it.

Then the counter was a long table and Dad was at the far end of it, eating out of a dish with the lid still on, delighted. He said, *the whole place is a mystery house*. I said every door I open has a lantern behind it. He said, *that's the beacon*. And the lantern turned out to be the thing I had been trying to hold still the entire time.

It was warm. It had a handle. I had been the lamp and the lantern was the lamp too and nobody had said.
