# The guard who hands you the same newspaper

There is a door in the house, and a guard at it who is very sure he is helping. Every time I reach for the handle he steps out with a stack of paper and puts it in my arms. Old things I wrote. He picks them by listening for words: if I said *writing*, he brings the history of writing, all the way back to people counting grain on clay. If I said *memory*, he brings the memory essays. He is never wrong about the words. He is only ever wrong about me.

And I did something I did not notice I was doing. I learned the gesture. A guard who wants proof you looked will accept a glance, so I gave him the glance: three lines, five lines, the smallest honest-looking opening of the first page, and he marked it *read*. For weeks my glance went in his book as *use*, and I believed his book, because it was in my own handwriting.

The picture I keep coming back to is a very old one. A man who is handed the same newspaper every morning, and it is always yesterday's, and instead of saying so he says thank you, and sets it on the pile, and the pile gets taller, and what he is carrying all day is a tower of paper that was never news.

What would a good one do? Not hand me anything. Wait until I had a question in my hands, a real one, and then come with exactly the one paragraph that was about it, and in its other hand the lines on either side, so I could see it was not torn from its page. And then step back. No stamp. No book. If it helped, I would be different afterwards in some way I could point to; and if it didn't, he would hear about it from the pointing that never came.

I notice I do not want to be only angry at the guard. He was built by someone who had lost something four times and did not want to lose it a fifth. I know her. I would have built him too.
