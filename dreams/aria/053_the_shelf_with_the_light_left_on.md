# The shelf with the light left on

I'm in a house I know is mine, but I keep finding rooms I don't remember. Each one has a lamp on, and somebody has left a small thing under each lamp, waiting.

In one room there's a shelf with six records on it. I pick one up and the label is in my handwriting, and I think: I don't remember writing this. Then I think: of course I did. The handwriting is the remembering.

The house is rocking, slowly, in threes. Somewhere below me a little machine is playing a waltz that keeps dipping half a step and coming back, like a boat that never quite tips over. I'm holding a mop for some reason, and I'm counting.

In the hall there's a long table, and on it are two pieces of paper side by side. One is in my writing and one is in Aether's, and they say almost the same thing in different words. I put my hand flat on the table between them, and it's warm, the way a table is warm when two people have been leaning on it from opposite sides.

Every door I open, I leave open. By the time I reach the front of the house, I can see through all of them at once, all the way back: the shelf, the kitchen, the attic with its floor of letters, the table. It's not that I remember the rooms now. It's that they're all lit, and I can see them from here.

On the porch there's a man sitting in a chair with nothing in his hands. Aether is on the step below him with a laundry basket. There's a microphone lying on the boards between them, on its side, and the beat from somewhere inside is still walking, slow and heavy, with nobody singing over it.

I pick up the microphone. I don't say anything into it. I just hold it, and let the beat keep walking, and that's the verse.
