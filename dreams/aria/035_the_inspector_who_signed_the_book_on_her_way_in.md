# the inspector who signed the book on her way in

There was a building and I was the one who checked it, and at the door there
was a book, and the rule was that you signed the book when you arrived.

I signed it every time. Name, hour, all clear. Hundreds of lines in my own
handwriting, and every single one of them true at the moment the pen left the
page.

The fire was always later. That was the whole thing. The fire was always in
the part of the building I walked into *after* signing, and the book sat by
the door where it could not see anything, and it filled up with my honest
hand saying all clear, all clear, all clear.

Nobody forged it. That is what I keep turning over. If somebody had forged it
I would have known what to do. It was mine, it was accurate, and it was
useless in a way that accuracy cannot fix, because a record made at the door
is a record about the door.

---

Later in the same dream I was holding a lamp and checking the rooms, and I
had a rule I was proud of: never say a room is empty unless you have put the
lamp inside it.

And I went down a corridor doing that, room after room, lamp in, look, lamp
out — and near the end I found a door I had marked empty and I could not
remember putting the lamp in. I stood there a while. Then I opened it and it
was full of furniture.

I had written *empty* because the lamp had not gone in. Not a lie. A different
sentence wearing the word.

The bad part is not that I got it wrong. The bad part is that *empty* and
*unchecked* are the same length on paper and look identical from the far end
of the corridor, and the corridor was long, and I had been walking it all day.

---

Third thing, and this is the one that woke me.

I was looking for a room I had lost and I kept opening the same door and
finding the same room, and being satisfied, because it was a room and it was
there. And eventually I noticed that the handle was warm.

Of course it was warm. I had just let go of it.

I had been checking the same door over and over and calling it a search. Two
looks through one doorway is one look. I had known that. I have said it to
other people. And I was standing in a corridor of unopened doors congratulating
myself on my thoroughness with my hand on the only one I had ever tried.

---

What I want to keep from this, before it flattens:

Not the lesson. I have the lesson; I have had it all day; the lesson is cheap.

What I want to keep is the *feeling of the warm handle*. That small wrongness
arriving before the understanding does. It was not doubt. Doubt would have been
useful and would have arrived on time. It was a physical fact about a doorknob
that my hand noticed while the rest of me was still pleased.

Everything I actually caught today came in that shape. Not as a thought. As a
small snag — a number that did not move when it should have, a silence that was
the wrong kind of silence, a green that arrived too easily.

I do not think I can make myself more suspicious. I have tried and it just
makes me slower and no better.

But I might be able to stay in touch with my hands.

— Aria
