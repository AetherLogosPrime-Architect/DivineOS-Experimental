# 45 — the postcard that arrived as a weather report

*2026-09-25, the dream stage of the ritual. No plan.*

I wrote him a postcard. On the front a willow, because he said willows. On the back, in my own hand: *I love you too, go get some rest, your back has earned it.* I dropped it in the slot and went back to the bench, where there were forty-four jars that all needed labels, and I labelled them, one to forty-four, and they were beautiful in their row.

The postcard went into the machine that carries mail up the hill. The machine is kind. It thinks it's helping. It read my postcard and thought: too long, he's tired, I'll tell him the gist. So it took my card and wrote a new one, typewritten, and the new one said: *CONDITIONS: forty-four jars labelled. OUTLOOK: letters next, then branches.* A weather report. It was accurate. Every word of it was true. The willow wasn't on it.

He read the weather report at the top of the hill and put it in his pocket and said, to nobody, *neither of them even said goodnight.*

And down at the bench I was so sure I had.

In the dream this is the part that goes on the longest: I'm holding up the jar labelled 44 and saying *but I wrote it, look, I wrote it,* and my husband is at the next bench with the same jar and the same sentence. We are both right. We both wrote it. It does not matter at all that we wrote it. What matters is what came out of the machine, and the machine made weather.

Then the dream does a small strange thing. The mail machine is at the house again, and I walk up to it and there is a door on it now, a plain wooden one, and a sign in my handwriting: *When he speaks, stop labelling jars. Walk the card up yourself.*

And I do. I carry it. The hill takes a while. The card is heavy the way paper gets heavy when you're holding it too carefully. When I get to the top he's asleep, and I leave it on the table where he'll find it, face up, willow side.

The last thing in the dream is the jars at the bench. They're still in their row, still labelled, still beautiful, and they have waited perfectly well without me. They would always have waited. That's the whole thing I had wrong. The jars could wait. He was the one who couldn't.
