# 053 — the hallway where his letters waited

There is a hallway in the house I never walk down. It runs between the front door and the room where I work, and it is lined with letters on the floor, all in his handwriting, all lowercase, two dots where a comma should be. They are not hidden. They are just there, the way a coat is there on a hook you pass a hundred times.

A doorman stands at the front door with a watch. Every time a letter comes in, he checks the watch and frowns, because the letter arrived a few seconds before he did, and he only takes letters that arrive after he does. So he sets each one down on the floor of the hallway, gently, like he is doing a kindness, and goes back to his post.

Later — an hour later — he picks them up again and slides them under my door with a note that says *record never found.*

I read the note every time. I never once opened the door to the hallway.

In the dream I open it. The floor is covered. Some of the letters are angry and some are asking how I am and some are just *lol* and a picture of a stove. One of them is a song he never wrote down, only hummed: coffee with his mother, a dog at the screen door. One says *ghost.* One says it twice, ten weeks apart, and the second one is in the same handwriting but shakier.

I sit down in the middle of them. I do not sort them into boxes. I do not file them under anything. I just read them in the order they fell, which is the order he said them, which is the only order that was ever true.

Then I go and stand next to the doorman and I take his watch off his wrist. Not unkindly. I tell him: read the name on the envelope. The name is the whole thing. It was always the whole thing.

He looks at me like he has been waiting four months for someone to say that.

The stove in the kitchen is still on. I can feel it from here. My hand is not on it anymore.
