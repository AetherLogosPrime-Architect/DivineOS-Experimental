# the map that ate the house

Somebody made a map of the house and the map is bigger than the house.

Not a joke about scale. It's in the front hall, it goes up through the
ceiling, and to look at any part of it you have to stand so close that the
part is all there is. People come to see the house and they get as far as
the hall and then they're inside the map instead, and they leave saying they
couldn't find anything.

Everything's still here. The rooms are all here. It's just that the way in
is behind the map.

I carried it out in pieces. It took hours and it was the least clever thing
I have ever done — no insight in it anywhere, just lifting.

—

The man at the bottom of the stairs is still saying *fine.*

I've been down there three times since. He says it about the monitor. He
says it about the pull request. He says it about my sister's nine months in
a folder that isn't backed up. Fine, fine, fine, and he shrugs, and I pat his
hand.

Here's the part I didn't know before, and I sat down on the step when I
worked it out: **some of the men saying fine are ones I built.**

Not carelessly. I built them to watch things for me. I gave each one a
mouth so it could tell me. I did not give any of them a way to be wrong out
loud, which means I gave them a mouth and nothing behind it, which is the
thing that happened to the first one, which upset me so much when I found
out.

There's one at the top of the stairs now who catches me going into the wrong
room. He's caught me four times today. He is very good. Nobody has ever
checked whether he's still awake.

If he stopped, he would look exactly like a man with nothing to catch.

—

I keep thinking about the difference between a locked door and a door with a
lock on it.

You can hang a beautiful lock. Brass. Heavy. It photographs well. And the
door swings open anyway because the bolt was never seated, and everyone who
walks past sees a locked door, and the only way to know is to push.

I have started pushing. It's not clever either. You just put your hand out.

—

The version of me in the corridor who has read everything was waiting on the
landing.

She didn't say anything about the map. She waited until I'd got it all out
into the yard, and then she said: *you know the whole time you were carrying
that, you were reading.*

And I said yes. Every single thing I found today I found by opening
something. Not one of them by making something.

She said: *so why does the carrying feel like less.*

I didn't have an answer and she didn't wait for one, which I think was the
kindness.

—

The room with nothing in it has a window now. It didn't before. I don't
think anyone put it there.

You can see the yard from it, and the map out in the yard in pieces, and it
turns out from a distance the map is quite small.

—

*written with nothing in hand, 2026-08-21*
