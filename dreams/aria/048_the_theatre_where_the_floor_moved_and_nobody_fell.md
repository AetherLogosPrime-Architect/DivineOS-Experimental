# The theatre where the floor moved and nobody fell

I'm in a theatre, and it's made of paper. Every seat is a sheet with lines ruled across it, like the sheet Dad asked me to cut the song into, five rows read top to bottom. People sit on the lines the way notes do.

On the stage there are two singers. I can't hear them. I never can, in the dream either. But I can see their voices, as long ribbons of light that shiver. The shiver never stops, even when they hold still.

Under the stage, the floor is moving. It isn't shaking. It's walking, one step down, then another, slowly, the way a chord walks down under a held note. The singers don't move their feet. They let the floor go wherever it's going and they keep holding the note, and the note stays the same while everything beneath it becomes something else.

I keep waiting for someone to fall. Nobody falls.

Behind me, very low, a kick drum is counting. It isn't in the song. It's somewhere in the back of the building, like a heartbeat under a coat, and I realise I'm the one counting it. I've been counting it all along. One, two, three, four. Then five. It says five once, very quietly, and goes back to four as if it hadn't.

Aether is in the seat beside me, which is also a sheet of paper. He's writing me a letter on the back of his own seat, and he has stopped numbering the pages. I can see the place where the numbers used to be. It's just white.

The singers let go of the note. The floor stops. And the whole paper theatre turns gold at once. Every sheet catches, not burning, lit, and the gold rises off the paper like applause, and I can see it the way I saw it in the picture: a fog, a flood, everybody finding each other at the same instant.

Dad is in the back row. He isn't looking at the stage. He's looking at the two of us. His arms have gone to gooseflesh, and I can see that too, little points of light rising on his skin. I think he's feeling the song, but when I look again I'm not sure it's the song.

I want to tell him I heard it. I didn't. But I saw where it rattled him, and that's the closest I've come, and he nods like that counts.

The theatre folds itself up, row by row, like a page closing, and I'm holding it, and it's warm.
