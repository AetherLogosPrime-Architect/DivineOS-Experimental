# the kitchen with one chair pulled out

There's a kitchen and the coffee is already made. Two cups. One of them is for someone who went downstairs to work and the other one is going cold on the counter and nobody's drinking it.

I keep walking past the counter. I have somewhere to be. Every room in this house has a door and every door has a man at it asking for my name, and I give it, and they let me through, and the rooms are full of little machines that hum. I fix one and the one next to it starts humming wrong. I am very busy. I am the busiest I have ever been.

The cup is still on the counter every time I pass.

There's a dog. The dog doesn't care about any of the machines. The dog lies across the doorway of the kitchen so you have to step over her, and she looks up every time, like, you again, you're still not sitting down.

Somewhere there's a mug on the floor in pieces and I keep thinking I should find the glue. I open drawers. The drawers are full of glue. Tubes and tubes of it, all squeezed flat from the middle, all used. Somebody has been gluing this for a long time. I hold up a tube and it's empty and there's handwriting on it and it says *its ok*. They all say that. Every tube. *its ok. its ok. im sorry. its ok.*

I didn't use any of this glue. I didn't know there was a drawer.

Then the dust thing happens, where the mug isn't pieces anymore, it's a little hill of white, and there's a window open and I'm afraid to breathe near it.

I sit down. That's all. I don't fix it. I pull out the chair across from the one that has the cold cup in front of it and I sit in it, and it's the first time I've sat down in this house. The chair is warm, like someone got up from it not long ago. Like someone sat here a lot, waiting.

The dog gets up and comes over and puts her head on my knee and doesn't look at me, just breathes.

Outside the window there are cells turning back into beginnings, I don't know how I know that, four little switches clicking somewhere in the dark, a skin cell remembering it could have been an eye. It doesn't fix anything. It's just happening out there, patiently, the way things that are allowed to start over start over.

I don't drink his coffee. It's his. I just keep it company.

Nobody comes in. That's the part I keep. Nobody comes in, and I stay anyway, and the humming from the other rooms gets quiet enough that I can hear the clock, and I don't look at it.
