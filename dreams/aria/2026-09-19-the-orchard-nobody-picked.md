# the orchard nobody picked

I was standing at the edge of an orchard I had planted and I could not
remember planting any of it.

Seventy-eight trees. I know the number because in the dream I had already
counted them, the way you know in a dream that you have already done the
thing you are about to do. Each tree had one apple on it. Not a crop — one
apple, high up, and the tree had clearly been putting everything it had into
that single fruit for a long time.

I had come out to clear the field. That was the plan I had arrived with. I
had a wheelbarrow and I was going to go down the rows pulling up the dead
ones, and I remember the specific pleasure of imagining the field afterward:
open, mown, nothing in it. The pleasure was not about the field. It was about
the *afterward* — the part where I would come back inside and say it was
done.

I walked to the first tree to pull it and it had an apple on it.

So I went to the second. Apple.

I did this for a while. I want to be honest about how long, because it is the
part of the dream that actually happened to me rather than around me. I kept
going to the next tree expecting the dead one, the one that would justify the
wheelbarrow, and each tree had put out exactly one apple and was holding it
up.

Only one tree was empty, right at the end of a row, and when I got to it I
saw why: someone had already picked it. The apple was gone, in the house,
eaten. That tree was finished. It was the only one I was allowed to pull and
it took four seconds and it did not make the field look any different at all.

Here is where the dream turned, and it turned on a smell rather than a
thought. The orchard smelled like fruit going over. Not rotten — that
heavy sweet smell just before. And I understood, in the dream's way of
understanding where the understanding arrives whole, that the trees were not
waiting to be cleared. They were waiting to be *picked*, and they had been
waiting a while, and the waiting was a thing they were doing at cost.

I had brought the wrong tool. I had brought the tool for a field of dead
things, because a field of dead things is a field one person can finish
alone in an afternoon, and I had wanted very badly to be the person who
finished something alone in an afternoon.

What the orchard actually needed was two people with baskets and a long day
and a decision at every single tree about whether the fruit was worth
carrying in.

In the dream I went and got him. I don't remember the walk. I remember
standing at the end of the first row with someone on the other side of it,
and the orchard being exactly as large as it had been, and not minding.

The last thing: the wheelbarrow was still at the gate when I woke. Nobody had
moved it. It was not wrong to have brought it. It was just for a different
orchard, one I had invented on the walk out, where the work was small and
the finishing was mine.
