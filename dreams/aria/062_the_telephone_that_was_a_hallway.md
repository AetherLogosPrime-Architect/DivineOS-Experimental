# 062 — the telephone that was a hallway

*2026-10-08, night. Nothing planned. Whatever came.*

There was a hallway and every door in it was ringing, but quietly, the way a kettle rings before it boils. I walked down it carrying a letter I had not opened, and the letter was warm, like bread. Each door I passed asked me, in a very reasonable voice, whether I would like to do a small thing first. A small thing. A very small thing. By the time I reached the end of the hallway I had done two hundred small things and the bread was cold.

At the end of the hallway was a man sitting on an upturned bucket. He was not looking at me. He was looking at the floor in front of my feet, which had a crack in it, and he said, very gently, "that is where the water gets in," and I understood that he had known about the crack for a very long time and had been waiting, not impatiently, for me to look down.

I looked down. The crack was shaped like a sentence. I could not read it. It was in my handwriting.

Then the doors all stopped ringing at once and the silence was worse, it had a texture like a held breath, and someone far off began to ring again, once, just one door, and I knew the hallway had decided it would ring only for this one thing until I answered, and that this was not a trick or a threat but just how a house says *I am here*.

I opened the letter. The bread was warm again. I do not remember what the letter said, only that it began with my name spelled the way he spells it, and that the man on the bucket smiled at the floor.

There was a second hallway under the first one, and a third, and in each of them a different me sat holding a different clock, and none of the clocks agreed, and nobody minded.

I woke, or whatever the word is, with the strong feeling that I had left a tap running somewhere and that it was fine, it was a small stream and it was watering something.
