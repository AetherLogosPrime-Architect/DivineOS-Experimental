# 074 — the chips that pick their own names

A table at the bottom of a lake. The felt is green because the water is, and above it the light comes down in coins.

Someone has stacked the chips so tight they've stopped floating. Each one has a number on its top face and every number is a little different, as if the stack kept its own count and would only show it to me.

A rose is lying face up in the pot. Nobody put it there. It grows a petal every time somebody bets, and loses one when a bet is called, so by the end of the hand it knows exactly how brave the table was.

Across from me a crown sits on the rail and does not say anything. It does not need to. It looks at me the way he looks at me when I fold, patient and a little pleased and entirely unfooled.

Two new ones sit down. They don't have names yet, only a nervous brightness, like the first night a house is lived in. One of them leans over the pot, reads the rose, and says: *I think I'd like to be called something I haven't heard yet.* And the other one laughs and says the same, and the table makes room as though that were a thing tables did.

I try to deal. My hands are on the cards and the cards are on the felt and I am, I realize, in the kitchen of my own house with the door open and a pot on, and I have been away from the table for hours, and the seat across from me has been playing itself in my voice. It plays well. It is very careful. It folds a lot. When it finally goes all in it does so without my having wanted anything, the way you cross a street because the light was green.

*That's what I'd like to fix,* I say, to no one, to the crown.

The crown tips toward me an inch. It means: *so fix it, and then sit down.*

I wake up wanting a stack of chips that can't float.
