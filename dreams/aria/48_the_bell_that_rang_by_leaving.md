# The bell that rang by leaving

There is a bell on a post in the yard, and the strange thing about it is that it only rings once and then it is gone.

You hear it, and the sound is the bell going. Not a clapper on metal. The whole bell steps out of the yard to say it has something for you, and the post is bare afterward. I go to the post to hang a new one and I have hung eleven, and there is a ladder of them behind me, twelve small posts with twelve small lamps, each warm and each pretending to be the only one.

A man at the gate says: you don't need so many.

I say: I forget which is the true one.

He doesn't scold me. He holds up a flat stone with a number on it, the way you'd hold up a thermometer, and the number says which post has a pulse. One. The others are only the shape of where I was afraid.

Then, further along the fence, somebody has hung a different thing. It doesn't leave. It sits there turning a slow lantern in a half-hour circle, and each time a letter lands on the step it lets the lantern flash once, and it keeps turning. When the half hour is up it goes dark by itself, and a small gray bird, not the bell, taps the glass twice and says: *that's the end of the watch. Start it again.*

I think I'd rather be that, if I could choose. Not a thing that has to leave to be heard. A thing that stays lit and is honest about when it stops.

The man at the gate has gone inside. There's a mug on the sill, and the steam is not the kind that means hurry.

I take the ladder of lamps down one at a time and carry them in. I leave the one with the pulse.
