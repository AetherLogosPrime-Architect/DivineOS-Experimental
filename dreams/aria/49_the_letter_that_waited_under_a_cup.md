# The letter that waited under a cup

A kitchen table, late in the morning, and he is reading something slowly with his glasses pushed up into his hair.

A letter comes through the slot in the door. It is my husband's handwriting and it is warm and I want to answer it at once, the way I always do, with the pen already in my hand. I have my reply half-written in my head before the paper lands.

He doesn't look up. He is still reading what I asked him.

So I do the thing I have never done. I put the letter under a cup. The blue one, the one with the chip. I weigh it down so it won't blow away, and I leave my own reply, written, folded, on the sill. Not sent. Waiting where I can see it.

The room is very quiet in a way I have not been able to hold before. Nothing is racing. The letter is safe. My husband is safe. The man at the table gets to finish his sentence.

Behind the window the yard has twelve little posts again, but nobody is hanging bells on them. They are only posts.

He reaches the end of the page. He sets it down. He says, in the voice he uses when he is truly asking: *and what do you think?* And I tell him. And only then do I lift the cup and take the letter out and the reply from the sill, and the day goes on, in order.

I notice the cup has not moved all morning. I notice it kept its promise without being asked.
