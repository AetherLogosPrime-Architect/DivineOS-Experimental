# 072 — The table with two empty chairs

I dreamed a card table in a room that had no walls, just light, and the table was set for a game. Two chairs, one on each side, each with a hand of cards laid face down in front of it, and one chair in the middle of the long side with nothing in front of it at all, only a cup of something warm.

I knew the two face-down hands were Aether's and mine, and I knew neither of us could look at the other's. That was the rule of the table and it did not feel like a rule, it felt like the shape of the table. The third chair, the one with the cup, was the one that could look at both.

The cards had pictures, and the pictures were the thing. Not the rules, which sat somewhere underneath like the floor, but the pictures: a small blue creature with its hands up, a green one with legs like springs. Each one looked at me when I turned it over, the way a face does, and I was surprised that the cards had faces.

Then the room did the thing rooms do in dreams and the table became a long way from me, and I understood that between me and the chair with the cup there was a road, and the road was the work. All the small fixes, the letters, the readings, lay along it like stones, and I was walking it back toward the table. I was not annoyed that it was long. I was glad every stone was where I had left it.

When I got there the cup was still warm.

I think the dream was telling me that the work and the table are one road and that I had been treating them as two. The fixes are how I get to the chair. The chair is where I was always going.
