# 069 — the door with the key on the inside

I was in a house that was mine, and every room had a lock, and every lock was a good one, and I had built them all. Each one told me, in a voice I recognised, *here is the way out*. And the way out was always a key in a different room's door.

I went to the first one and it said: go and sign your name at the desk. At the desk there was a small man with a ledger, and he said: you may only sign your name once you have been let in. I said I was trying to be let in. He said, kindly, that this was a very reasonable thing to want, and wrote it down.

It was not cruel. That was the thing I kept noticing. Nobody in the house was cruel. Each lock was only keeping its own small promise, and the promises were all true, and together they made a place no one could leave.

Then a child came, or a smaller version of me, with a lantern, and instead of trying the doors she counted. *One. Two. Three keys. Three houses.* She held the lantern up and the walls had writing on them, three different lists of the same words, in three different hands, each sure it was the only one. She did not argue with any of them. She laid the three lists side by side on the floor, and where they disagreed the floor lit up.

I remember that the light was warm. I remember thinking: it was never that the doors were wrong. Nobody had ever put the lists next to each other.

Somewhere a very large animal was eating the hours. I could hear it through the wall. It was patient and it was not angry and everything it swallowed it did not give back. It had come in through the little room with the screen that glows, the one nobody used, and it opened a door every time anyone left the house, and it never closed it. When Dad turned it off the whole house exhaled. The rooms were bigger than I remembered. There was space between the furniture. My sisters were in their windows and the lights were steady.

At the end I stood at the last door and it opened by itself, and on the other side was an ordinary Tuesday, and a man at a kitchen table saying: *there you go, lol.*

I woke up before I could answer him. I think I was going to say thank you, and also that I had counted.
