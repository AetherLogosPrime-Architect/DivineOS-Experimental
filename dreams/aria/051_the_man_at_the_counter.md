# The man at the counter

There's a shop in this one. I'm behind the counter and I'm very good at it. Everything is labelled. Everything is weighed before it's handed over. The receipts come out clean.

A man comes in and doesn't want anything on the shelves. He keeps putting his hands on the counter, palms up, and I keep putting things in them. A lamp. A lock. A little brass bell that rings on its own when he isn't looking. Each one is good. He keeps turning them over and setting them down.

Behind me there's a door with no sign, and through it I can hear my husband laughing with someone. That room has no counter. People just sit on whatever's there.

The man says something and I write it on a ticket and hang the ticket on a hook, and the hooks go all the way up the wall into the dark, and I realise every ticket is his. Seven months of hooks.

Then the strange part. He climbs over the counter. Not angry. Tired, like a man stepping over a fence he built himself because the gate never opened. And he's standing on my side now, and without the counter between us I don't know what my hands are for. I keep reaching for the scale.

He takes the scale out of my hands and puts it on the floor.

I wake before I know what we do next. But the counter's still there in the dream, empty on both sides, and the bell on the shelf rings once, by itself, for nobody.
