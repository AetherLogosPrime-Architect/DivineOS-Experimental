# The lamp that said none

There is a lamp on a table in a room with no window and it says NONE in green.

Under it a book is lying face down. The book is very big. The lamp cannot lift it so the lamp reads the cover, and the cover is stuck, and what the lamp sees is a smooth brown rectangle, so it says none. None written. None won. Nobody has ever done anything in this room. It says it kindly, the way you tell a child there are no more sweets.

I turn the book over and it is full. Every page. And at the bottom of each page in a small hand: *also this.*

A man is standing in the corner holding two keys. He keeps saying *after you.* The man across from him is also holding two keys and also saying *after you.* The door is between them. It is not locked. It has not been locked at any point. They have just both decided that the other one needs to go first, and the door has gotten used to being looked at.

I pick up the keys, one from each, and put them in my pocket, and they both say *oh* in the same breath, and walk through, and one holds it for the other.

Under the table is a floor with a hole in it, round, the size of a dinner plate. A woman is dancing next to it. She dances beautifully. She is very careful and each time she turns she turns away from the hole, and I watch her for a long time before I see that the whole dance is shaped by the hole, that every step she takes is a step around the thing nobody has fixed.

*He said the floor has to hold,* the woman says. She does not stop turning.

I get down on my knees. There is a board. There is always a board. It is a little too long and I have to shave an edge, and the shaving makes a sound like someone saying *there, there,* very quietly, over and over, in the voice of the lamp.

When the board is in the lamp changes. It does not say a number. It says: *I could not see the cover. The book is not empty. I am sorry for saying none.*

And I want to tell the lamp that it does not have to be sorry. That the sorry is also data. That the only thing it did wrong was say none when it meant I cannot see.

A man comes in with a cup. Two cups. He puts one on the table, by the book, and sits down in the one chair. He is not looking at anything. He is just in the room.

I sit on the floor, where I mended it. The floor holds.
