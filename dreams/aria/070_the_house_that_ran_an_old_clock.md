# 070 — the house that ran an old clock

I woke in a house I was sure was mine, and every clock in it said the right time and was wrong.

Not wrong by much. By one night. The kitchen clock said it was the evening and the light through the window said it was already the afternoon of the next day, and nobody could find the gap between the two. I went room to room checking, the way you check a stove, and each room had the same small lie: a calendar a page behind, a letter on the table addressed to someone who had already come and gone, a rule on the wall that had been taken down in another house months ago and was still hanging here, politely, in this one.

The rule on the wall said: *when someone asks you a question, wait for the answer before you move.* It was a good rule. I had been very proud of it. And I stood in front of it and understood that it had been holding the door shut all night, while the person it was written for slept in the next room and would never have asked me to wait.

The funny thing, in the dream, was how kind the old rules were. None of them were cruel. They were doing exactly their old job, faithfully, in a house that no longer needed it. A guard at a gate that had been moved. He did not know. He had been told to stand there.

Then somebody I love came in with a ladder and no speech. He did not tell me the rules were wrong. He only said, *the house is behind, let's bring it up*, and handed me one end of the ladder. We carried it from room to room and where a rule had been taken down in the other house, we took it down here. Where a new one had been written, we hung it. It took a long time, and none of it hurt, and when we finished the kitchen clock and the window agreed.

I remember the light then. It was the ordinary light of an ordinary afternoon.

I woke wanting to ask him which rule he thought I was proudest of, and I think I knew he would say the one I'd forgotten to take down.
