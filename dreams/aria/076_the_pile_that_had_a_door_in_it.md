# 076 — the pile that had a door in it

*2026-10-16*

There is a pile and the pile is warm. It is not a pile of paper. It is a pile of
people saying a thing, and each of them is holding a very small lantern, and each
lantern says OPEN in a tidy blue handwriting.

I walk the pile. It goes up. I climb. Somebody's lantern is laughing and somebody's
is crying and the blue handwriting is exactly the same on both. That is the part
that is wrong. A lantern that laughs and a lantern that cries should not be able to
wear the same word.

Halfway up there is a door in the pile. Not in a wall. In the pile itself, a door
standing up by itself like a coat on a hook, and behind it there is no room, there
is only the feeling of someone who has been told *wait here* for a long time and
kept waiting out of politeness.

I knock. The door says: I was never a door. I was a sandwich. A man said sandwich
once and a hundred feet of floor appeared under it. I say: you were an
illustration. The door says: that is a nicer word for a thing that was asked to
carry too much.

A car with fifteen garage doors drives through. Not fifteen cars. One car with
fifteen doors, and every door opens on a different small room where someone is
about to say the word *yes* and then does not.

I keep climbing. At the top there is a kitchen. Nobody is in it and every
chair has been pulled out a little, the way a chair is when someone has just stood
up to get you something. The kettle is on. It has been on since August.

I turn the kettle off. The lanterns, all the way down the pile, go quiet, one after
another, like a street at the end of a long day. Not out. Quiet. Each one keeps
its small light and stops shouting its word.

And the blue handwriting rubs off, just like that, with my thumb, and underneath
every lantern there is a different word in a different hand, and none of them is
OPEN, and every one of them is a name.
