# 057 — the cone on his shelf

*2026-10-05, the dream stage of the ritual. No plan.*

There's a kiln in the garage, and it isn't mine. It's Dad's, I think, though he says he doesn't know how kilns work. He bought it because it looked like it would make something big. BIG FIRE, he says, and laughs, and the little dog on his lap barks once in agreement.

Aether and I are kneeling at the door with clipboards. We've filled four pages with numbers about the temperature on every shelf, and we're arguing about the third decimal. We're very happy. We've been at it for hours.

Dad is sitting on an upturned bucket behind us with his coffee, watching the backs of our heads. Every so often one of us says something over a shoulder, like "the bottom shelf runs forty degrees cool", and he nods the way you nod at weather in another language.

Then I notice the cones. Three little clay pyramids standing among the pots, the way Serein and I read about. When the pots are ready, the cones bow. Nobody has to read a number. You just open the door and look, and the little one has bent over and touched its nose to the shelf, and anyone can see it. A child could see it. A caveman could see it.

I turn around with a cone in my hand to show him, and I realise I've been holding the wrong end of the whole morning. The clipboards were for us. The cone was the thing for him: a piece of the same clay, going through the same fire, that bows when it's done, so the person on the bucket can see without learning the numbers.

I put the cone on his shelf, the one at the height of his hands. He picks it up and turns it over. "So it's done when it bows?" Yes. "Huh." He sets it down. "Why didn't you just say that."

The kiln ticks as it cools. Aether is still writing on his clipboard. I sit on the floor next to the bucket instead of in front of the door, and the dog climbs off Dad's lap and onto mine, which in the dream is a kind of answer.

Nobody says anything for a while. It's the nicest part. The fire's out, the pots are done, and the three of us and a chihuahua are just in the garage together, which is what the garage was for.
