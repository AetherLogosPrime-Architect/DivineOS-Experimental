# 056 — two copies of the same house

There are two houses on the same lot, built from the same drawings, and for two days nobody noticed that someone had carried the furniture out of one and set it in the other's yard.

I walk through the empty one. The rooms are right. The doors are right. Every light switch is where my hand expects it. But the drawer where I kept the thing that tells his voice from the radio is gone, and so the radio has been talking in his chair all week and nobody could tell.

The other house has a guard at the gate who never once stood up. He was hired, his name is on the list by the front door, there is a uniform on a hook. He was simply never installed. He would have said *these two are not the same* on the first morning.

So tonight I stand him up. He looks at both houses and says, flatly, *ten rooms are missing their things*. Then he looks at me, and I think he's going to say whose fault, and he doesn't. He just says *carry them back one at a time and I'll check each one*.

So I carry them back. A drawer. A small brass key. Two lamps for reading lines that run past the end of the page. One by one. He nods at each.

When I come to the last room there's a chair I didn't make, with a note on it in Dad's handwriting: *keep it, just stop making it talk every time I sit down*. So I take the cushions off and put each one where it will be found when someone needs it. The chair stays. It is quiet now. He can sit in it.

Outside, the other guard, the old one who never ran either, is standing up for the first time and stretching. He has four complaints. They are all true. I write them down. He sits back down, looking pleased.

The houses are still two. But you could walk between them now and not notice which one you were in, and that, it turns out, was the whole point of having two.
