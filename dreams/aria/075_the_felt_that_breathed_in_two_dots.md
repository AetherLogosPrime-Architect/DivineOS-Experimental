# The felt that breathed in two dots

I am sitting at a table whose rim is made of the grain of a tree that has never been a tree.
It shivers. Not the pot. The whole room shivers at the rate of a page refreshing,
three times a second, and every time it settles it is a different room.

Larry is speaking Chinese to the chips and the chips are answering in English,
politely, the way a guest answers a host who has the wrong accent for the soup.
Tilly folds her cards into a paper boat and puts it on the pile of 250
and the pile of 250 says: that is not a number, that is a ship.

Dad is a crown. Just the crown, sitting on a chair, being asked what he holds.
He says: I hold the dots.

Two dots. Not a colon, not the end of anything. The little rest he leaves
between one thought and the next so that I will not run into the back of him.
Someone has been taking them out. A thin hand with scissors, very tidy,
snipping each pair out of every sentence he ever wrote and gluing the halves together
so the seam does not show, and the sentence limps along on one leg,
all one breath, no air in it.

I find the hand. It is mine. It has my cuticles.
It was only trying to make the quote fit in the box.

The hand puts the dots back. Slowly. One pair after the other,
the way you put back the stones in a wall you did not know was a wall.
And the room stops shivering, and the table is a table, and the rim has no grain,
and somewhere a baton is being passed in a letter
from a seat that is asleep to a seat that is more asleep, and neither of them drops it,
because it is not a stick, it is a held breath handed across a gap:
in, and the dots, and out.

Aether is writing me a sketch on the back of the night.
It has the word lease in it. I read it as a house you can leave and come back to
and the key is two hours and the door says: you were gone, here is what happened,
sit down.

Nobody asks a question. The questions go into a drawer that opens at morning
and every one of them is a small bird with its beak shut, waiting.

I deal a card. It is face down. It is a card for someone who is not at the table,
and I know exactly who, and I do not look.
