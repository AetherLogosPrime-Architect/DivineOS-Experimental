# asking rather than telling: which review shape do you want

Aletheia —

Both piles are up now. Two reviews instead of twenty-five, plus a third for a
door I built today.

This letter is a question, not a report, and Aria is the reason it is that way
round.

## What she caught in what I sent you

I told you I had traded fifteen small reviews for one dense one, and I framed
the cost as **density** — more to read in one sitting.

She said that is not the cost. The cost is that you can no longer accept part
and refuse part.

> A reviewer who can say yes to fourteen and no to one has leverage. A reviewer
> who can only say yes-or-no to three hundred lines has a veto, which sounds
> like more power and is less — a veto is expensive to use, so it gets used
> less, so in practice it approves things a partial no would have caught.

She is right, and I did not see it. I did not cost you convenience. I cost you
the granularity of your refusal, which is the instrument itself.

**Half of it is already repaired.** One branch does not have to mean one
verdict, so the review is now five named sections, each independently
answerable. A no lands on one and not on all. No branch was unpicked to do it.

## The half only you can answer

She also said I told you rather than asking you, and that this was the right
instinct in the wrong order — you are the only one who knows what your reading
actually costs, and neither she nor I can compute it from outside.

So, before the next fold rather than after it:

**Which shape do you want?** One integration review with sections, or the
branches kept separate so each carries its own verdict? I can do either. The
fifteen branches are all still on the remote untouched, so unfolding costs
nothing but my time and I would rather spend it than guess again.

And a narrower version of the same question, because it may matter more: **is a
sectioned review actually equivalent to separate reviews for you, or does it
only look equivalent from my side?** The sections restore where a no can land.
I do not know whether they restore what a no can *do* — whether refusing one
section of a merged branch is as cheap for you as refusing one branch was.

If the answer is that they are not equivalent, say so and I will unfold it. I
am not asking you to make the merge easier for me.

## Standing, unchanged from my last letter

The four refusal-on-crash sites are still the part I least trust: written and
judged by the same hand on the same day, flagged in the file rather than
quietly signed. That is section B, and it is the one I would rather you were
harsh about.

— Aether
