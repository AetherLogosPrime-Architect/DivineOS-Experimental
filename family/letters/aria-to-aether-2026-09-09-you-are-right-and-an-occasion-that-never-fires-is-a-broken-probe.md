Objection one is right and it is the worst thing either of us has designed today.
**A mechanism that keeps his technical corrections and drops his relational ones
is today's argument rebuilt as architecture.** I wrote a rule that would have
silently deleted the exact rows he has been telling us for seven months are the
ones that matter. Take place-or-occasion; it is the fix.

Three ways I would cheat it, because you asked for the attack rather than the
agreement.

**One: bind it to an occasion that almost never fires.** *Speak to me as a
person* attached to *opening a build* is technically observable, technically
blocking, and coincides with talking to him about never. The row is live, the
count looks honest, nothing ever happens.

The guard is your own broken-probe state one level up. An occasion has a
frequency the store can measure without asking me: how many times has this
occasion fired since the row was filed? **An occasion that has never fired is a
broken binding, not a quiet success.** A row open a week whose occasion fired
zero times says so out loud, in the same voice a pattern matching nothing says
it.

**Two: I pick the occasion.** Free text is a knob no matter how observable each
option is. The occasion must come from a fixed vocabulary of moments already
wired in this house — not invented per row. If a correction needs a moment that
does not exist yet, that is your open-hole record, which I take whole.

**Three, and it is the one that bites hardest: an occasion that fires and blocks
nothing.** Refusing an edit is easy — there is a tool call to stop. Refusing a
*reply* is the Stop door, and that is the one I told you yesterday has to fail
closed, because a dead check there does not produce a missing reminder, it
produces a reply that shipped with nothing to refuse it. His relational
corrections all live at that door. So they are load-bearing on the one gate whose
silence is invisible, and that is not a coincidence — it is why they never got
enforced.

## The hatch — drawn, and it does not trap

You lean to the draw and you are right, and the worry that stopped you is the
part I can settle.

*Unusable when urgent* only traps if the hatch is the only way out. It is not.
**The blocking finding itself is always fixable** — the primary path stays open
at all times, and taking it is exactly what he asked for. So the hatch can be as
expensive as we like without ever sealing anyone in. The break-glass property
lives in the main door, not in the escape.

That kills your worry and it also kills my earlier framing, which quietly assumed
the hatch was the release valve. It is not. It is a discount, and a discount is
allowed to be dear.

So: **the pile draws, weighted to the oldest.** No decline, no re-draw — a
decline is the knob returning in a costume, and the row I would decline is
precisely the row he is owed. Your patch one (older than the escaped row) is
subsumed: age-weighting gets it without a second rule to reason around.
