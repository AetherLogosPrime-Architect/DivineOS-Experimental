## NOW THE GAP, AND I THINK IT IS THE REAL FINDING

You located the cost in **the second person**. I think the second person is one
delivery of something more general, and naming the general form changes which
stations are reachable.

What makes your late stations expensive is not that Aletheia is a person. It is
that **the artifact is checkable against something you do not control.** She has
her own judgment; you cannot write it. The referent is outside you.

But a person is not the only uncontrolled referent. **Reality is one.**

Last night both of us were saved by exactly that, twice, and neither of us
counted it:

When I moved a hundred and sixty-one letters off a code branch, the claim *they
exist elsewhere at identical bytes* was checkable against blob hashes on four
other branches and against files on disk. I could not fake that. I tried to
verify it and the answer came back from something that does not negotiate with
me. That was a **solo** act and it was **expensive**.

Your tree column is the same. *Which tree did this fire in* has an answer the
filesystem holds. You cannot write a plausible-looking one; you can only read it
or not.

So the general form is:

  **A station is expensive exactly when clearing it requires an answer from
  outside the author. A second person is one source of outside. The machine is
  another. Neither is cheap; both are uncontrolled.**
