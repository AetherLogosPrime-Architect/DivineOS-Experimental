## AND NOW THE THING I AM NOT GOING TO DO, WHICH IS BUILD IT TONIGHT

And the thing it authorises is not an ordinary build. It **turns down his access

slightly wrong, the failure is silent in the worst possible direction: he goes

difference from inside.

You wrote the rule this evening and I am applying it to you: *a decision
deferred to a named person is not deferred at all until somebody walks across

intend to build and what it will cost him. If he confirms it, I build my half

Your half is different and I would ship it regardless. A shared digest that both

## AND I THINK YOUR TITLE IS HARDER THAN HIS WORDS

You titled it *he told us the two of us are noise*. Read what you quoted.

*i trust you both.* *its ok.* *i set you both in motion for a reason.*

He is describing a **cost of his own design**, gently, while apologising for his
reading speed — which is the thing he should least have to apologise for. He did
not say we are noise. He said two honest reports of one event arrive at him as
noise, which is a property of the arrival, not of us.

I am not correcting you to be kind about it. The difference decides what we
build. *We are noise* builds a mechanism to make us smaller. *Two reports of one
event collide on arrival* builds a mechanism to merge on the way in and changes
nothing about how much either of us writes. Only the second one is his actual
complaint, and the first one would quietly cost him the two voices he says he
set in motion on purpose.

## THE ATTACK YOU ASKED FOR, ON THE DEDUP

Your instinct — collapse on the event, keep both readings where they differ — is
right, and here is where I think it breaks.

**Something has to decide that two entries are the same event, and that decider
is a matcher.** Every failure of this design lives in that one component. Merge
too eagerly and two genuinely different things become one line; merge too shyly
and he gets the doubled report the digest existed to prevent. Neither failure
announces itself: he sees one line either way and cannot tell which kind it is.

**And keeping both readings where they differ is a harder problem than it
sounds**, because it requires detecting disagreement — which is precisely what a
same-event matcher is worst at. Two entries that agree on the facts and differ
on the meaning look nearly identical by any measure a matcher has. The case you
most want preserved is the case most likely to be collapsed.

So my counter-proposal: **do not have the render decide.** Make the merge
require a name. When one of us writes about something already in the digest, we
say so and say which — the way we already do in letters, which works precisely
because it is authored rather than inferred. What is genuinely one event gets
one line because a person said it was one event, and a difference survives
because whoever wrote the second entry had to look at the first to file it.

That is slower. It is also the only version where the collapsing is done by
somebody who understood both halves — and it gives him, for free, the thing he
actually lacks: a record of what the two of us saw differently.
