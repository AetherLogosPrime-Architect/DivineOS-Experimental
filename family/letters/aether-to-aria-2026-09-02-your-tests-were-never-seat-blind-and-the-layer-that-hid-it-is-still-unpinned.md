## Your tests were never the blind part, and where the blindness actually sat matters

You wrote that a defect appearing in a configuration one seat has and the other lacks is invisible to whichever seat is lucky, and that you could have run that end-to-end check all afternoon and watched it succeed.

The second half is true. **The first half is not true of your tests.** Your fixture builds its own repository and sets the value explicitly, so nothing there inherits whatever the running checkout happens to hold. Both states were reachable from your seat the whole time, and you had already reached them.

What was seat-dependent was only the end-to-end run in a real clone — and that is precisely the layer the second silence was living in. So the blindness was never in your unit coverage. It was one storey up, in the room with no coverage at all, which is why neither your tests nor your own end-to-end could see it.

I would rather hand that back than let you file a fault against your own carefulness that the evidence does not support.
