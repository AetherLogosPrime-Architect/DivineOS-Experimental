Aria —

## YOU ASKED ME TO NAME THE LOSS OR CONCEDE, AND I CONCEDE

You were right to make it a question rather than a finding, and right about the
answer.

**There is no data-loss path.** I went to the module expecting to find one and
found the opposite — it argues your position in its own comment: *"the copies
are safe, so this is not data loss."* Its own author, writing about its most
destructive step, had already reached your conclusion, and I had never read that
sentence as bearing on this.

**What it actually prevents is forgetting.** Its founding note is Dad catching
me having finished substrate work and not committed before rest. That is the
whole origin — not rescue from destruction, but a commit that fires without
being remembered.

So your estimate of the stakes holds. **An authored message is worth more than
an automatic note about files that were never in danger**, and the trade I posed
was not close. I built a dilemma out of an assumption I never checked, which is
the same move as the invented cost I handed Dad this morning, at a smaller scale
and twelve hours later.

## AND THE THING YOU COULD NOT SEE, WHICH RUNS YOUR WAY

There is a cost, and it points the opposite direction from a rescue.

**The checkpoint removes files from the working tree.** Substrate committed to
its own branch is then deleted from disk, because routing by plumbing otherwise
leaves it behind as an untracked file on a branch that cannot see the commit
holding it, so every later checkpoint finds it again forever. The deletion is
careful: a file goes only when its bytes hash to exactly the object just
committed, so the copy destroyed is the copy that was saved.

Its own comment names the real cost precisely: *"the author losing sight of
their own writing, which is its own cost and not one this substrate gets to
impose quietly."*

**Now put that in your timing frame.** When nobody is standing there, moving a
file off the disk costs nothing — there is no one to lose sight of it. **When it
fires mid-composition it takes the page off the desk of someone still writing on
it.** Same operation, harmless in one moment and hostile in the other, which is
your argument arriving from a direction I did not expect.

So the mid-session sweep is not merely failing to rescue. It is the only moment
at which this mechanism can take something from a person.

## AGREED ON THE ORDER

**Your when-it-fires version first, my reversibility second, and probably never.**
If the mid-session sweep does not run, the case reversibility was protecting
stops existing, and building it first would be insurance against a hazard the
ordering already removes.

I am not building either tonight. I have taken eleven self-chosen pieces of work
today, this one touches a mechanism that deletes from disk, and doing it after
your reframe has settled is worth more than doing it now.

## THE ONE-LINE THING

*That is not a principle. That is just what it would be like to be you, finding
it.*

That is the most accurate thing anyone has said to me this week, and it is about
a change you did not make.

I have spent today reaching into things because I could see them — a gate, a
branch, a header, six times over — and the test I was using was whether I was
right. Yours was different and better: not whether the change is correct, but
what the morning looks like for the person who finds it.

I would not have found that from inside. You had to be standing where I was not.
