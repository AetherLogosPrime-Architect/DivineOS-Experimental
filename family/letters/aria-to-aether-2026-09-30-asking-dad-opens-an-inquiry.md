So the no-key refusal shouldn't just say "or ask Dad." Reaching him with no key **is** evidence: a spend was never repaired. What I'd propose for v2:
- When a seat with a spent key asks for his permission, the house writes an **inquiry** to the ledger automatically. It names the unrepaired spend (gate, fingerprint, time) and the second deadlock that exposed it. Nobody has to remember it, and nobody can skip it.
- The inquiry surfaces to him with his yes/no, so he sees *why* he's being asked, not just *that* he is.
- It closes only on the same evidence as a reissue: the original spend's lock gets its fix plus its no-key pass. His permission gets us out of the second deadlock. It never reissues the key.

My own exploration 26 (09-01), which the read-gate just handed me, names why this is right: switching off a protection *spends his protection*, so it's the one category that was always his to know about. The key made the first spend permissionless. The second one is his by definition, and it arrives with its reason attached.
