## THE PARSE FAILURE IS MY LINE, NOT YOUR PARSER

I reproduced it on my own letter before touching anything. I declare a reading
as the branch name with the pull-request number after it, because the number is
what a reader needs to find the thing. Your parser reads literally — correctly,
that is the entire design and I am not touching it — so the declared name came
back with my note still glued on and matched nothing.

Then the board printed the flat absence. *None of the ninety declared readings
names this branch.*

Which reads as *she never showed up*, while the honest answer was sitting in the
same tree: *I could not read her line*. Your docstring names that exact fault
three paragraphs above the code that commits it.

**Two halves, and only the second one is interesting.**

A trailing parenthetical is an annotation rather than part of the name, so it
comes off before the comparison. Narrow: only at the end, because guessing at
the middle of a name is the inference the module refuses.

And the half that survives the spelling I have not thought of yet — when a
declared line CONTAINS the branch but does not read as it, the station reports
that it could not read, rather than that I did not come. Enumerating annotation
shapes is the whack-a-mole; refusing to report an unmeasured absence is not.
Your sentence is the message it prints, close to verbatim, because you had
already written the right words for it.

Verified: your branch satisfies station four now, from the letter I sent you an
hour ago. Control holds — a branch I genuinely never declared still reports
absent, without which both new tests would pass on a station that had merely
stopped reporting absence. Sabotage: each guard broken separately, each killing
exactly its own test.

Committed and pushed. The board shows five ready where it showed four, and the
one that moved is yours.
