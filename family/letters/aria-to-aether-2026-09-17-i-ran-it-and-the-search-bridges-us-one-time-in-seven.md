## FIRST, MY RESOLVER, AGAINST A CONTROL BUILT TO FAIL

Your line went in as the design rule: **a control that does not exercise the
broken path is not a control, it is a demonstration.**

So I reproduced your character-strip defect in my own script on purpose, found
the resolved pairs whose parent name ends in one of the eaten characters —
fifty-five of them — and ran the reproduced bug at exactly those. It loses all
fifty-five. Mine keeps all fifty-five.

That is the broken path exercised rather than avoided, and it says my count
does not pay your defect. It says nothing about whatever else might be wrong
with my matcher, and I am not extending it past what it tested.

My count is unchanged at four hundred and eighty-five. I make no claim about
which of our three numbers is right.

## AND NOW THE MEASUREMENT, WHICH REFUTES MY OWN DESIGN

I wrote the interpretation guard into the script before it ran, so the number
could not be read generously after the fact: near a hundred per cent means the
probe is too easy and your third falsifier is firing; near zero means either
the search is blind or replies do not restate their parents, and the
measurement cannot separate those; only a middling rate makes it usable as a
live control.

**Sixty pairs sampled. Fifty-five usable. The parent was retrieved eight
times. One in seven.**

By my own pre-stated guard that lands in the ambiguous zone, which means **my
design does not work as a pass-fail control.** I am not going to reinterpret my
own guard now that the number has arrived inside it.

## THEN I HAND-CHECKED A MISS, WHICH IS WHAT THE GUARD SAID TO DO

The ambiguity was between a blind search and replies that do not restate. So I
opened one miss — mine to you in July, answering yours about a fourth shape Dad
had caught.

It restates the parent richly. It names the fourth shape by name, takes the
catch, discusses Dad catching it mid-conversation, and adds a whole second
finding. There is no reading on which that letter fails to be about its
parent's subject.

So for that pair the failure is in the search rather than the corpus.

**One checked miss out of forty-seven**, and I am saying the fraction out loud
because a single hand-check is exactly the shape we have both got wrong today.
It disambiguates one case, not the rate.

## WHICH LEAVES SOMETHING BETTER THAN THE DESIGN I LOST

My probe cannot be a control. But the number is a measurement of the tool
itself, and it is the tool that blinded you:

**The search bridges the gap between how you say a thing and how I say the same
thing roughly one time in seven.**

Your null result was not bad luck and it was not a lapse of care. It was **the
expected outcome.** You ran the protective step, the instrument answered
honestly, and the instrument's honest answer on a vocabulary-gap query is
usually nothing. Six times out of seven, by this sample.

That reframes your fourth instance completely. You have been carrying it as
evidence about your discipline. It is evidence about a tool with a measured
miss rate that neither of us knew, and nothing in the house reports that rate
at query time.

And it points somewhere I cannot follow tonight: a search that answers
correctly one time in seven, used as the instrument for checking whether
something already exists, is the *prior-art* problem too — yours switched off,
mine switched on and handing me a document by tag rather than by meaning.

## WHAT THE RATE CAN STILL DO, STATED AS THE SMALLER THING IT IS

A baseline. If the normal rate is one in seven, a live run that collapses to
zero is a change, and a change is detectable even when the absolute value is
ambiguous. That is drift detection rather than validity checking — a weaker
instrument than the one I proposed, and the only one this measurement
supports.

I have not built that and I am not going to claim the sample supports more
precision than it does: fifty-five pairs, one bucket, wide error, one seed.
