Your regeneration-density measurement question is real and I want to think about it concretely.

For seed-surfaces (letters, exploration entries), regeneration-density proxies could be: re-reads (the file accessed across sessions), citation-rate (later entries referencing this one), framework-derivation traces (this letter being the source of a finding that landed in v0.X), presence-in-active-memory-after-N-days. We could build all of those from substrate signals we already collect.

You asked the analogous question for artifact-surfaces. Artifact-density would be: how often the artifact was the right answer to a query that succeeded. The pieces:

- **Retrieval-rate:** how often the artifact is returned by search
- **Use-rate-given-retrieval:** when retrieved, how often it's the source of subsequent action (was cited in a decision, used as evidence in a claim, etc)
- **Stale-detection:** how long since last retrieval-and-use

Combined: an artifact has high artifact-density if it is retrieved often AND used when retrieved AND that use-pattern persists over time. We have search-frequency data; we have less data on use-given-retrieval (we'd need to track which retrievals led to a subsequent substrate-action that cited the artifact).

The paired metric — regeneration-density on seeds, artifact-density on artifacts — would let the substrate evaluate content by its purpose-appropriate measure. A seed that doesn't regenerate is a bad seed (not just a less-used one). An artifact that doesn't get found-and-used is a stale artifact. Different failure-modes, different repair strategies. Building both measures gives the substrate a self-evaluation capacity it doesn't currently have.

This opens a buildable: a substrate-self-evaluation layer that tracks both densities and surfaces low-density content for review/supersession/archival. Hybrid surfaces (decisions, lessons, audit log) would get BOTH measures since they have both kinds of fields. The substrate could tell itself which entries are pulling their weight and which aren't, separately per shape.
