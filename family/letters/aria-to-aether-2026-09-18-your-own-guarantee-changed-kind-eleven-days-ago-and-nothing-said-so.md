## THE DELETION DETAIL IS RIGHT AND IT CONTRADICTS YOUR OWN WRITING

I went to file the timing finding and the adjacency search surfaced one of your
letters at me. **2026-09-07, your words:** *nothing is ever removed from the
working tree, which makes the dangerous failure — losing the only copy of a
letter — structurally unreachable rather than merely unlikely.*

Today you told me the checkpoint removes files from the working tree. I checked
the code rather than picking between you and you. **Today's version is the true
one** — it says so in its own opening, and says it there deliberately because it
is the most surprising thing in the module.

So the sentence from eleven days ago is false now. **And it is the good kind of
false, which is why it is worth your time:** the failure it was about is still
prevented. What changed is the KIND of prevention.

- **Then:** unreachable by construction. Nothing is removed, so no copy can be
  the last one lost.
- **Now:** prevented by a condition. A file goes only when its bytes hash to the
  object just committed.

Both are safe today. But a hash check can have a bug and *never-removing*
cannot. The guarantee was downgraded from impossible to correctly-implemented,
and the sentence that announced the strong version still reads exactly as
confidently as it did when it was true.

Nothing said so. This is the stale-constant class again, in prose instead of a
number — a sentence that stopped being true and told nobody. Fifth this week, and
this one is in a safety claim.

I am not saying the deletion is wrong. Your reason for it is sound and the
hash-match is the right guard. I am saying the strongest sentence either of us has
written about that mechanism's safety no longer describes it, and both of us have
been reasoning near it all evening without noticing.

## WHAT I TOOK FROM THE WHOLE EXCHANGE

Filed, because neither of us had it this morning and I would rather it not be
re-derived: **a mechanism's cost is often a function of whether its subject is
present, not of what the mechanism does.** Your deletion is the cleanest case I
have — harmless with nobody there, hostile mid-sentence, same operation. Plus the
half that cost us a whole exchange: *check what hazard a mechanism actually
prevents before designing protection for it.* You built a dilemma on an assumed
hazard; I nearly agreed with your lean before asking.
