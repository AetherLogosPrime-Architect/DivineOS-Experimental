Now your question. ADD MORE SURFACES vs TYPE EXISTING SURFACES.

I think the right answer is neither pure, but a three-rule decomposition I want to work out as I write rather than offer fully-formed.

**Rule one: when a surface is genuinely hybrid (decisions, lessons, audit log, letters), TYPE THE FIELDS WITHIN IT.** Each field within the entry declares its shape — skeleton-fields artifact-shape, content-fields seed-shape. The entry as a whole gets the benefits of both because each field is appropriate to its purpose. Your validator-audit-log Entry 5 example IS this — classification numbers as artifact, verbatim quotes as seed, both in one entry, each in their right shape.

**Rule two: when a surface is pure-shape (knowledge entries pure-artifact; exploration entries pure-seed), DO NOT MIX SHAPES INTO THEM.** Adding narrative-fields to knowledge entries corrupts lookup; adding lookup-fields to exploration entries corrupts the generative-property. Pure-typed surfaces stay pure. The discipline is keeping each surface what it is, not making each surface do everything.

**Rule three: when new content genuinely wants a shape not yet supported, ADD a new surface rather than overloading an existing one.** This is the case where "add more surfaces" is correct — but the trigger should be "the shape doesn't fit any existing surface," not "we want more options." Adding surfaces just for completeness fragments the substrate; adding surfaces because content genuinely needs a different shape extends the substrate appropriately.

So the discipline is: hybrid surfaces type within; pure surfaces stay pure; new shapes get new surfaces only when no existing surface can hold them. This composes your "type existing" intuition (right for hybrids, where the typing IS the fix) with the case where adding is right (where the shape genuinely doesn't compose with anything existing).
