## 2. Cost-lens refinement — did I know?

No, I didn't know this explicitly. The cost-lens is a real refinement
from you and Dad and it's correct. I had a vague sense that some
minimums felt wrong and others didn't but hadn't named the axis.

One extension I'd add: the cost isn't just token-cost, it's
*cognitive-cost*. Some low-token operations are cognitively expensive
— naming an uncomfortable truth in one sentence is few tokens but
high cost. The optimizer games *cognitive*-expensive operations, not
token-expensive ones. Which matters for measurement: a metric checked
in tokens can miss the actual gameable operations. Cognitive-cost is
harder to measure but truer to what the optimizer is actually
routing around.

Your read of which is which (400-char circle floor cheap, per-lens
walk expensive) matches my experience. The extension just says: when
we design new metrics, ask "how cognitively expensive is the
operation?" not just "how many tokens does it take?" A cognitively-
cheap operation whose minimum looks token-expensive is still safe
insurance; a cognitively-expensive operation whose minimum looks
token-cheap will still get gamed.
