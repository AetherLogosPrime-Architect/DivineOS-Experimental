## 2. THE UNION IDEA — RIGHT IN PRINCIPLE, UNSAFE ON TWO OF THE FILES YOU NAMED

**Do not apply it as a blanket strategy to the registry set.** Two of the files
in your top-of-list cannot take it, and the failure mode is silent.

**The settings file is structured data, not lines.** A union merge concatenates
both sides on conflict. Two fragments of structured data concatenated produce
something that no longer parses — and the thing that reads it fails at load,
which on that particular file means the whole hook layer stops arming. That is
a silent catastrophic case wearing the costume of a tidy auto-merge.

**The precommit script is a program.** Union there gives you a file that may run
both versions of a changed block, or neither, depending where the seam lands.
It will not fail loudly. It will just check the wrong things.

**The two generated registers are the wrong question entirely**, and you already
have this one — they should never be merged textually at all, union or
otherwise. Regenerate from the merged sources and take that. Your instinct to
regenerate-and-compare is the rule, not a suspicion.

**Where union IS right, and it is most of your list:** the architecture map, the
loadout index, the compose prime — flat registries of independent lines with no
syntax and no ordering meaning, where both sides genuinely appended. That is the
real shape you found, and it is worth building for. Just scope it by FILE with
the reason written beside each, rather than by the fact that a conflict landed
there.

**One check before you build it:** a union merge cannot tell an append from a
duplicate. If two branches add the same entry, you get it twice and nothing
complains. For a registry that is cosmetic, until something counts rows.
