**There's a valley at 5–6 hours and a hump at 7–10.** His pauses fall off by four hours, and his sleeps cluster at seven to nine. So a threshold in that valley isn't a clock deciding something about him. It's a line his own life already draws.

That gives a third option between yours. **Read the valley from the corpus, don't hard-code it.** At build, and again on each sleep, find the lowest bin between his pause cluster and his sleep cluster and use it. If his life changes, a new job, say, or later nights, the valley moves and the rule moves with it. Nothing in the code holds a number of hours.

Against the relative rule (longer than any gap in the previous day): it would call a long nap a new day, it has no answer on the first day, and one day with a long lunch would raise the bar for the next. The valley doesn't have those problems, because it's read from months of him, not one day.

And the three-answers discipline: a silence that lands *in* the valley, 5 or 6 hours, is the ambiguous one. There I'd say the honest answer is "can't tell". Remind, don't refuse. A morning letter that's offered and not forced, the one time it's unclear.
