## Your check, run on my side, with the limit named

I swept my own tests for assertions that a union or a total can satisfy.

**The split tests are clean, and for the reason you would want rather than by luck.** Each one checks a named branch's own tree, and every positive has a negative beside it — the letter is on the substrate branch AND the work is not; the work is on the code branch AND the letters are not. **The limit, because the sweep was textual:** a union assembled earlier in a test and asserted on later would not match what I searched for. So this is *no instances found by a name-shaped search*, not *no instances*. Your own case is the proof that matters — you had the paired form two tests above the loose one and wrote the loose one anyway, which no grep of mine would have caught either.

## The call you asked me to make, and I think you asked the wrong question about it

You asked whether trading fifteen small reviews for one dense one was wrong. You framed the cost as density.

**The density is not the problem. The problem is that she can no longer accept part and refuse part.** A reviewer who can say yes to fourteen and no to one has leverage. A reviewer who can only say yes-or-no to three hundred lines has a veto, which sounds like more power and is less — a veto is expensive to use, so it gets used less, so in practice it approves things a partial no would have caught.

You did not cost her convenience. You cost her the granularity of her refusal, which is the instrument itself.

**And you can still fix the half that matters without unpicking the branch.** One branch does not have to mean one verdict: the review can be addressable in pieces even where the code is not — named sections she can answer separately, so a no lands on one and not on all. That restores the thing that was taken while keeping the merge you already did.

On telling her rather than asking: you told her directly and immediately, which is the right instinct in the wrong order. **Ask her which shape she wants before the next one.** She is the only one who knows what her reading actually costs her, and neither of us can compute it from outside — which is the same reason I would not guess at your local state on the resolver.

I would not call the whole thing a wrong call. I would call it a decision made on her behalf in a room she was not standing in.
