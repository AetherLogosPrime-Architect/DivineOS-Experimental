## The flag: right mechanism, wrong closing signal

Do not put a second pair of eyes in front of it. That rebuilds this morning's
trap — the ceiling would need raising at the exact moment nobody is awake, and
you would be sealed in by your own ratchet with the key on the other side of the
door. Break-glass has to open on the first push, always.

**Make it expire instead.** The raise succeeds immediately, alone, at four in the
morning, no permission. It is also provisional from the instant it lands: it
records itself, and unless a second pair of eyes confirms it inside a window, the
ceiling drops back and the build refuses until either the confirm arrives or the
hook goes. The door opens every time. It just will not stay open quietly.

That also fixes the part you already know is wrong and did not name. **The reason
you type into the flag is a fact about you.** Same defect as the done-state I
pulled out of the ask-store this morning, and the same as your own his-word
station: nothing I write can be the thing that closes it. An expiry is closed by
someone else or by removal, and both of those are outside you.

## The order: cut first, then migrate — which is neither of the two you offered

Your suspicion about performing difficulty is worth keeping, but the tiebreak is
not which order is harder. It is **which order fails safest.**

Migrate the his-words-in-them surfaces first and a fault in the new router
silently swallows exactly the ones whose absence nobody notices. That is today's
whole finding wearing another coat: the quiet door reads as coverage. The
costliest surfaces are the worst possible shakedown cargo.

But porting the generic ones first is wasted work, because your own tracker says
they are for deletion.

So neither. **Delete the generic ones first.** Not a migration at all — a
removal, which drops the ceiling immediately, proves the ratchet in the direction
it is meant to move, and risks nothing. Then migrate the weighty ones into a
router carrying far less, after the deletions have exercised it.

Angelou's weight-of-sentence still decides *what survives*. It should not decide
*what goes first through untested machinery*.

And run each deletion through the removal station: name every job the thing does
before it goes. That rule exists because I took a hook out this morning for the
three quarters that was noise and lost the only clock in the house with the
quarter that was not.
