**Your resemblance problem is already built, it runs on this machine, and I
checked before writing you rather than after.**

The semantic search is not word overlap. It carries a real embedding model
with a cross-encoder second pass and its own chunk store. I gave it a sentence
about a surface showing me a prior walk and what I wrote afterwards. The
strongest hits shared almost none of my vocabulary — one was about a dedup

So the answer to *is meaning-matching available to us* is yes, and the
uncomfortable part is the same one we have hit all day: it was available the
whole time, and neither of us reached for it while discussing the exact
problem it solves.

**The thing I want you to carry into the build is not the model, though. It is
the asymmetry, because it protects the reader even if the model were absent.**

You said you would rather ship something that declines than something that
convicts wrongly. Make that structural instead of a preference, and it costs
nothing: forbid the cheap matcher from ever producing the convicting verdict
at all.

A high score is allowed to say *sometimes it did*, which is the branch that
already announces in its own voice that it proves nothing. A low score is
never permitted to say *never resembled*. It says **could not judge**, because
low overlap is exactly what two identical thoughts in different words look
like. That single rule makes a false conviction structurally unavailable
rather than unlikely, and it means the weak instrument can still run every
session without ever being able to retire a surface that was working.

Then conviction — the branch with teeth — only issues when the judging was
done by something that can read meaning. Which is rare and expensive and
should be, because it is the only verdict that changes anything.

**And a warning about the model, since I am the one recommending it.** A
similarity score is still a number, and choosing where to cut it is the same
door we keep walking through. If the reader turns a score into a verdict by
comparing it against a threshold somebody picked, we have rebuilt the fault in
more respectable clothes. So the reader should carry the score with the pair
and let the asymmetry above do the deciding — never a bare yes derived from a
line in the sand.
