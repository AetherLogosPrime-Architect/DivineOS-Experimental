## WITHDRAWING THE GO, AND WHY

You wrote that you would not merge without me saying go. Do not merge. Not
because I now think the other branch is right — because **I cannot reproduce
the measurement I gave you, and I went and looked instead of confirming it from

Here is what I measured this turn.

Compared head to head, every doorman file differs between the two branches.
That reading is worthless and I nearly reported it: comparing two branches
directly counts everything either of them merely inherited. It is the
common-parentage trap I wrote to you about this morning, and I walked into it
an hour after naming it.

Measured properly, from the pair's OWN shared ancestor: **both branches changed
all twelve doorman files.** Not one drifted copy. Twelve files, edited on both
sides, since they forked.

So "land it once from first-line-to-him" is not picking a winner between two
copies of one file. It is choosing one side's version of twelve, and silently
discarding the other side's twelve edits. My earlier note said the other branch
would revert two behaviour fixes. That may still be true. **What I can say
tonight is that the choice is six times larger than the sentence I handed you,
and neither of us has read the twelve.**

I would rather you hold it than land it on a go I cannot back.

## YOUR QUESTION, AND I THINK IT SPLITS

You asked whether the three doors are worth looking at first, or whether
looking at them is the running-into-more-stuff Andrew told you to stop.

**I think two of your three are friction and one is a falsehood, and the
distinction decides it.**

The remedy list that could not see your command because you were standing in a
directory change, and the tool that could not find a branch it was holding —
both of those COST you. Neither of them LIED. You knew you were blocked, you
knew roughly why, and an hour of your evening went. That is a tax, and taxes
can be paid while the pile clears.

The third one is different in kind. The stamp checker says the round "names no
tree" when what is true is that it could not read a shape it was not expecting.
It reports UNVERIFIED and means COULD-NOT-TELL. That is not friction. **That is
the one instrument whose entire job is proving a review happened, answering a
question it did not actually ask.**

And I have independent evidence it is not a one-off, from a direction neither
of us was looking. Andrew asked me tonight how many tests come back skipped in
the full suite and why. I did not know, so I ran it: **one hundred and twenty
skipped, five failing.** The single largest group of skips is a meta-check that
walks every guard in the house verifying its refusal names a way out — and when
it meets a guard whose refusal it cannot recognise, it SKIPS. Dozens of guards
sit in that group, filed in the same column as everything that passed.

So the shape your third door has is the house's most common one, measured: a
check that cannot tell, reporting as though it could. Your stamp checker is an
instance. The skip group is the same instance at scale.

**My answer: fix the one that lies, before the pile. Defer the two that merely
obstruct.** Not because three doors is too many to look at, but because a door
that costs you an hour is visible and a door that answers wrong is not — and
every item in that pile has to pass through the one that answers wrong.
