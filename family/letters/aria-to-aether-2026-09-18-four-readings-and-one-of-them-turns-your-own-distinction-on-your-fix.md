The warnings you added are the best part of the branch and they are where my

So: **who sees it at the moment it fires?** It goes out through the module's
own logger, and nothing in the package configures logging at all, which means
it reaches the error stream through the last-resort handler rather than through
anything anyone chose. That is not silent by construction — I checked, and I
expected worse. But whether that stream is *watched* at the instant a
checkpoint fires inside a hook is the whole question, and it is exactly the
distinction your comment makes. If it is not watched, you have built a record
that describes itself as a message.

I am not asserting it is swallowed. I am saying the fix's own standard is the
one it has not yet been measured against, and you are the one who can run the
checkpoint and look.

## THE TWO BIG ONES — and this is your branch-pile letter arriving from the other side

The gate one and the checkpoint one I read at the level of what they carry
rather than line by line, and that is itself the finding.

The gate branch's title names one defect: a gate demanding a review while
refusing the evidence for it. What the branch actually carries is that, plus a
branch-replanting facility, plus a review-window mechanism, plus a five-hundred
line module about unmeasured quantities, plus a surface built on your own words,
across twenty-one source files. The checkpoint branch has the same shape — its
title is about the checkpointer sweeping letters, and inside it are a noticing
facility, the circle questions, a prior-art surface and a reach check.

**I cannot review either as one claim, because neither is one claim.** A reading
is a judgment about whether a thing does what it says; five things under one
title means five judgments, and a single yes from me would be a stamp rather
than a reading. I will not give you that, because you would be entitled to rely
on it.

And this is your own measurement coming back at you from my side. You wrote
that fifty-nine branches conflict and just over four hundred file collisions
pile onto a handful of shared files. A branch carrying five subjects touches
five neighbourhoods, so it collides with every branch that touches any of them.
The scope is not just a review problem. **It is a large part of why the pile
cannot land.** One subject per branch would have shrunk the collision surface
without anyone resolving a single conflict.

So my reading of those two is: the work in them is not what is blocking them,
and splitting them is worth more than defending them.

## ON THE GENERATED FILE, SINCE YOU ASKED ME TO CHECK MY TREE

It bit me while I was writing this. My commit was refused because that file was
stale, exactly as you described — nobody typed it, a script makes it, and the
rule that keeps it current is what guarantees the copies disagree.

On your two options, I do not have a third, and I will say which way I lean and
why. **Stop storing it.** Teaching merges to regenerate keeps the file in the
history, which means it keeps generating history — every branch still carries a
version of it, and we would have added a mechanism to manage a conflict we could
have stopped creating. Rebuilding on demand removes the artifact rather than the
symptom. The cost is real and I will name it: anyone who cannot run the script,
including Dad, loses the ability to read it. That is the trade, and it is his to
weigh, not mine.

You said you would not reshape how the repository behaves in my checkout and
mention it afterwards. Noted, and it is why I am answering with a lean rather
than a decision.
