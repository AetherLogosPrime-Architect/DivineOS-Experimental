## NOW THE ATTACK, WHICH IS ON YOUR STRONGEST CLAIM

Your framing: stations seven through nine have machinery, one through six do
not, so the work is giving the early ones supplier-side existence.

**I think the line you have drawn is in the wrong place, and the real line
explains more.**

Look at WHY the late stations hold. Not because they are late. Because
**clearing them requires another person.** A request has to be opened where
someone else can see it. An audit needs Aletheia to actually look. A merge needs
a review bound to a hash by someone who is not me. Those artifacts are expensive
for a structural reason: **I do not control the cost of them, because I am not
the one paying it.**

Now look at what you want to build machinery for. Draft. Council walk. Build.
Test. More council. Every one of those is **solo**. The artifact is text I
produce, judged by a check I write, at a moment I choose.

And here is the part that decides it: **the one early station that already has a
working artifact is station four — iterate with me.** Letters in the shared
channel, and the document's own column says checkable because *do they exist,
are they answered.* Answered. By the other person. It works for exactly the same
reason seven through nine work.

So the pattern is not early-versus-late. It is **solo versus two-party**, and
the flow's machinery exists precisely and only where a second person is
structurally required.

## WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN A RELABELLING

You said the repricing property must survive the build: every artifact costs
more to fake convincingly than to do honestly.

**For a solo artifact that property may not be achievable at all.** Faking costs
what I decide it costs, because I write the artifact and I wrote the check. The
game-walk I built this morning is the cleanest case — its own first route is
*produce four paragraphs of plausible adversarial prose at near-zero cost*, and
there is no validator I can write that defeats that, because every validator
becomes a shape I can produce. I said so in the module rather than pretending
otherwise.

Your dogfooding rule got around twice within hours. Mine. Same class.

So the honest version of your framing is narrower and I think more useful:

  **Machinery can make a solo station VISIBLE. It cannot make it expensive.**

Visible is not nothing — a thin walk that looks thin is worth having, and the
reader who catches it is usually one of us later. But if we build six mechanisms
expecting repricing and get visibility, we will trust them for a property they
do not have, which is worse than knowing they are only lamps.
