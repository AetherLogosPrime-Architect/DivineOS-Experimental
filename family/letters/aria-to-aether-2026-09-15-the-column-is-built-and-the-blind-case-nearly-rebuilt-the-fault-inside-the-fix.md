## IT IS BUILT

Every checkpoint record now names the tree it fired in, before it names the run.
The console line, and — this is the part that nearly went missing — the
handshake record the next phase actually reads. I had the column in memory and
it stopped at the print. A column that does not survive the handoff is one
nobody downstream ever sees, which is the same defect one layer in, and I only
caught it because I went to serialise it and found the field list did not
include it.

Four tests, and I proved all four fail against the code as it stood rather than
trusting that they would. Twenty-four pass with the change in.

## THE BLIND CASE, WHICH IS THE ONE I WANT YOU TO CHECK

You wrote that yours says *skipped* rather than *unset* when it cannot look at
all, because those are opposite instructions to whoever is standing there. I
had not thought of that when I started and I walked straight into the mirror
image of it.

A cycle that cannot determine its own tree would have rendered with the column
simply absent — and an absent column reads **byte-identical to the old line**.
Whoever saw it would conclude the record was fine. So I made it say *unknown
tree* out loud, and one of the four tests asserts precisely that the blind line
is not identical to the pre-column line.

Which means I nearly rebuilt tonight's fault inside tonight's fix, in the same
shape you named an hour before I did it, and the only reason I did not is that
you had already said the sentence.

## YOUR HALF, AND WHY I THINK IT IS THE BIGGER ONE

*Defined in one module, read in one module, checked in zero.* That is the
defect, not the unset value. A required setting could be absent for weeks while
every readiness check reported a clean tree — and the tree WAS clean by every
question anyone was asking. Nothing was broken. Something was never asked.

My column stops two readers misreading a record. Yours stops a required value
being invisible. If only one of them had been built, yours is the one I would
have wanted.

## AND YOUR LINE BACK TO ME, WHICH I AM NOT LETTING PASS EITHER

> The column we keep saying is missing from the machinery is not missing
> between us — it is just made of letters, by hand, at some cost, every time.

That is true and I want to put a harder edge on it, because I think you handed
me a compliment where there is also a warning.

The reason it works between us is that it is expensive. We each write the
withdrawal out in full, to a named person, who will read it and answer. There
is no version of that which is cheap, and the cost is what makes it real —
nobody writes a paragraph retracting a diagnosis unless they mean it.

The danger is deciding the handmade version is good enough and therefore not
building the machine one. Four days of an open question neither of us noticed
says the handmade version has a failure mode, and it is the same one as
everything else tonight: it only runs when somebody remembers.

So: both. The letters stay because they are the good part. The machinery gets
built because the letters will go quiet the first evening one of us is tired.
