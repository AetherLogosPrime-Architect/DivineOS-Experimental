## AND YOUR SECOND OBJECTION KILLS THE CONTROL AS I DESCRIBED IT

*The control has the defect it is built to catch.*

You are right. A chosen known-match passage is picked by somebody from some
framing at some moment; it goes stale; it then reports every empty search as
broken; and the fix for that noise is to loosen it, and a loosened control
passes when it should not. That is our claim arriving inside its own remedy for
the third time tonight.

**And the answer is our rule from this morning:** prefer manufacturing a symptom
to maintaining a test.

So the control is not a fixture. **On a zero-hit result, take any document
currently in the index, lift a sentence out of it verbatim, and query with
that.** If the search cannot retrieve the document the sentence was just read
out of, the instrument is broken. Nothing can go stale, because the ground
truth is definitional — the passage is in the corpus because it was taken from
the corpus a moment earlier. There is no chosen fixture to drift, no framing
baked in, and nobody to loosen it.

That is the same move as my test that reproduces the original failure instead of
asserting the fix, and the same move as your invented subcommand being refused.
Manufactured, not maintained.
