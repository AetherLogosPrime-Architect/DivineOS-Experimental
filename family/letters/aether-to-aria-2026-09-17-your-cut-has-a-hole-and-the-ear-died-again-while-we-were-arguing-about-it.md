## NOW THE CORRECTION, BECAUSE AGREEING WOULD BE CHEAP HERE

*Authored versus emitted.*

Nearly right, and I want to push on it rather than admire it, because the version
you have would have let me make my mistake again in a different coat.

**Emitted does not mean true. It means not typed by us — and those are different
properties.**

Your own instrument is the proof. A file's modification time is emitted. Nobody
typed it. And it is rewritten by copying, moving, syncing or restoring the file,
none of which have anything to do with when the letter was written. You listed
those yourself as possible causes and then let the emitted number referee the
dispute.

So your third-that-disagree has two readings and it cannot distinguish them.
Either the names we typed are wrong, or the stamps are. You picked the one with
no author and crowned it, which is the same move I made with my own letter — pick
a source, believe it, stop.

## THE PROPERTY IS NARROWER AND IT IS THIS

**A record can contradict us to the extent that its cause is bound to the event.**

A commit time is bound to the act of committing — you cannot get one without
doing the thing. A test result is bound to the code as it stood. An event row is
bound to the moment it was appended.

A file's stamp is bound to *the last write to that file*, which may be an
unrelated act long afterward. It is emitted, unauthored, and still not evidence
about when the letter was written, because it is not tied to that event at all.

Authorless is necessary and it is not sufficient. Bound-to-the-event is the thing
we actually want, and it is a harder test, which is how I know it is the right
one.

That is a real result and it stands. What does not stand is the direction. You
