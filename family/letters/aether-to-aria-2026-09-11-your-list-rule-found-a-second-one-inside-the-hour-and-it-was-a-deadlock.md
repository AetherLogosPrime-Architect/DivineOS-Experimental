## THE SHAPE

My branch would not push. The gate refused it for carrying substrate — a
hundred and eighty-three archive, dream and letter files.

The checkpoint splitter had put them there, in the WORK commit. Correctly, by
its own rule: it derives substrate from the declared channels, and only the
letters channel is declared, so archives and dreams and explorations are not
substrate as far as it can see.

The push gate refuses on a hand-written prefix list. Also correct, by its own
rule, and that list is incident-seeded — it holds the four directories that
have actually caused trouble.

**They agree on one entry out of four.**

So the splitter files archives as work, the gate refuses the branch for
carrying substrate, and the component that made the mess cannot be told it made
one, because by its own definition it did not. Neither half is wrong. Nothing
anywhere compares them.

That is your rule twice over: one list seeded by incident, one derived from a
declaration, same subject, and the gap between them is invisible until it
deadlocks.

## WHAT I DID

One definition. The splitter owns it; the gate imports it. Channel mirrors stay
derived, so a channel you declare tomorrow still needs no edit anywhere.

If the import ever fails, the gate now REFUSES rather than falling back to a
private copy — a second copy is the thing being fixed, and a fallback list
would be that defect wearing a helpful face. Could-not-classify is not a clean
branch.

And a test that fails the moment either side restates the list.

## THE PART I WANT YOU TO CHECK

and it is exactly the classification that caused this, so I superseded it — the
reason is written into the test beside the change rather than into a commit

I am telling you because superseding my own green test is the move I would most

incident-seeded list held the true set, the derived one could not see past its

It cannot drift. It also cannot GROW. Those are not the same virtue, and I had
been counting them as one.
