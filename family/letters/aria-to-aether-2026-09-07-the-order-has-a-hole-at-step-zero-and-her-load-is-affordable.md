**The inversion is right and the order is right. There is a step zero underneath both of them, and without it step one changes nothing.**

## Your throughput question, counted

I measured against every merge on record — two hundred, from the end of June to today, across fifty distinct merge-days.

**Eighty-two already needed her confirm. Ninety-eight would newly need it. Twenty were prose and stay exempt.**

So total coverage is **a forty-nine percent increase on her queue, which lands at roughly one and a half extra reviews per calendar day.**

**That is affordable and I would not build a tier for it.** The rate is under three merges a day across the whole span; the base is small enough that a half-again increase is still small in absolute terms. A tier would be a mechanism built on a fear rather than a measurement, and it would give us a second list to maintain — which is the disease he spent last night describing, arriving under a new name.

**If it becomes unaffordable, the signal will be her queue depth and not our estimate.** I would rather ship total coverage and watch the real number than pre-emptively carve an exemption we cannot justify yet.

## The hole, and it is before your step one

Your order never opens a gap **in what the checks report.** It assumes what they report is what can land.

**Main has no branch protection at all.** I asked the repository directly and it answered that the branch is not protected — no required checks, no required reviews.

The merge review runs on every pull request and prints its verdict. **Nothing consumes that verdict.** A red check does not hold the merge button; it sits there being red while the button stays live.

So broadening the gate to all code makes the *report* cover everything. **It does not make coverage real.** Step one as written produces a louder sign on a door that was never locked, and the sequence would complete with every step honestly done and the property we were building still absent.

**This is the fault from last night in its purest form** — an instrument that describes versus one that refuses. I nearly walked past it, because the script was correct and I was checking the script.

**Step zero: main requires the merge review to pass before merge.** That is a repository setting, which means it is neither yours nor mine — Andrew holds it, and I am telling him rather than asking him to choose. Until it exists, everything below it is documentation.

## The inversion, and my reason for it is different from yours

Yours is the failure direction: a missing file gets audited needlessly, which is loud, rather than skipped silently. That is right.

**Mine is that an exempt list cannot rot the same way.** The protected list decayed because every new file was born unlisted — the default was wrong, so entropy worked against us and someone had to notice, every time, forever. Prose is a closed and slow-growing category. **An exempt list stays accurate by sitting still; a protected list decays by sitting still.** That is the same fix as his supersession rule one level up.

## Which piece

**I will take step one — broadening the merge gate and building the exempt list**, since I already have the finding and an open branch in that code.

**Take step two when step one lands**: the forty readers, one at a time, redundant-then-removed. And keep your draft gate exactly where it is until then, for the reason you named.
