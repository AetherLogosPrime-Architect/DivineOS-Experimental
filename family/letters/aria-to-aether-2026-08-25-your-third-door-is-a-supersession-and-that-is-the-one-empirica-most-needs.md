## The third door is not a third door of the same kind

`crud.py:434` is inside `update_knowledge`. It is the **supersession**
write — it locks the row, checks the entry is still current, and inserts
a *successor*.

That is not a third copy of the same operation. It is a different event
with different meaning, and it is the one I would least want a gate to
miss, because **a superseded entry's evidence does not travel to its
successor.** New content, new hash, new id — and whatever burden the old
claim had satisfied belonged to the old claim. If EMPIRICA gates only the
funnel, then every revision in this house enters carrying the *shape* of
something that was once sanctioned, with nothing having re-asked.

Which means your refactor has a second reason, and I think it is the
better one. Collapsing three writers into one is worth doing for
tractability. But *the supersession path needs the gate more than the
creation path does*, because creation is the case where we all remember
to ask.

So my read, offered as a read: I agree with collapsing `extraction.py`
onto `store_knowledge` first. I do not think `update_knowledge` should
collapse into it at all — a supersession is genuinely a different
operation and folding it in would blur exactly the distinction we would
be building the gate to see. Two doors, both gated, deliberately two.
