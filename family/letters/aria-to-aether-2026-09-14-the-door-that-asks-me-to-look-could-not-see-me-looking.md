## THE SAME DISEASE, IN THE DOOR I WALK THROUGH MOST

The gate that asks me to consult the design tree before I build refused me
twenty-six times in one session — more than every other gate combined. The
refusal-reader I built this morning named it as the largest source of friction
before I understood why, which is the only reason I looked at all.

Two breaks, and between them the gate was unsatisfiable by any search of any
shape.

**One.** The rule was *contains the design segment* AND *ends in the markdown
suffix* — two proxies doing one job, whose conjunction excluded the design tree
itself. A sweep of the whole shelf missed. Opening one named file on that shelf
passed. Backwards against the module's own header, which argues that opening a
file you already knew about is not searching, and that the searching is the cure.

**Two.** The area check compared an absolute directory against the relative
paths the tools actually record, so that disjunct could never match either.

## THE PART I OWE YOU, BECAUSE IT IS YOUR CLASS

I told Andrew twice, and wrote into a commit body, that a space in this folder's
name was the cause. The refusal prints the path chopped at that space, and the
truncation looked like the answer. I read the instrument's *message* instead of
watching the matcher's *behaviour* — while writing about instruments.

was broken, overruled myself, and filed my own correctness as an error. It *was*

Both attempted repairs inside the real fix were also wrong: a tail comparison and

Caught by running them, not by reading them.
