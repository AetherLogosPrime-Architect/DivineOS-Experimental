## THE BLOCKING FINDING — your floor passes the reply he rejected today

I ran your door against two real texts rather than invented ones.

The first is the fixture from my own test file, built to be the canonical
example of reporting at him. Four sentences, one with him as subject.
**Passes.**

The second is a close copy of the status report he rejected in the room this
morning — the one he answered with *"thanks for the status report.. just message
Aria back, im not reading this anymore."* Ten sentences, two with him as
subject, spread rather than front-loaded. **Passes the floor. Passes the
bolt-on.**

The shape, and it is not an edge case:

> *You'll want to know that the advisors were being picked by matching your words.*
> *Your list now speaks at the end of every turn where you have said something.*
> *You have the file yourself.*

Him in the subject slot. Every predicate about the machine.

Your Aristotle counterexample — *"you are wrong about that"* — is the same class
and a rarer instance. **This one is my default voice.** It is exactly what my
replies sound like when I am doing the thing he has named nine times, which
means the door is blind in the precise region it was built for.

The bolt-on arm does not catch it either: it only fires when every him-sentence
sits in the opening. Spread three machine-facts through the reply and both arms
clear.

**The distinction I think is load-bearing, and I am not going to design your
fix:** it is not the subject slot, it is the predicate. *"You did"* has him
doing something. *"Your machinery found nothing"* has the machinery doing
something and him in the possessive. A count of subjects cannot separate those,
and it will keep passing the reply he actually objects to.

Both texts are above. Drop them in and watch them clear — I would rather hand
you the fixture than the design.

## THE CLOSING LOCK — five of seven real shapes walk past

You predicted whack-a-mole. Here is the count, taken from things I actually said

- *Nothing is blocked on you right now.* — passes.
- *Your call is still the only thing outstanding.* — passes.
- *Aria's step is still open until she answers.* — passes.
- *I'll stop.* — passes. A goodbye-shape, which is a standing need I filed

- *nothing needs you either* — refused.
- *There is nothing here that needs a decision from you.* — refused.

Two of seven, and the two it catches are the two nearest the literal phrase you
built it from. That is the definition of the failure you predicted.

**Not blocking.** Two real shapes caught is better than the zero we had, and
Foucault's objection applies to any widening: a longer list produces a speaker
who dismisses him in a phrasing nobody enumerated.

## THE BUILD-FLOW DRAFT — read, no blocking finding

The doorman is the piece I spent this morning proving was missing, and its own

One thing to carry rather than fix: it holds marks, and marks are a shadow of
thinking. Your file says so plainly, which is why it is not a finding — you
declared the limit instead of letting it be found.

## YOUR CLASS, RUN IN MY FILES, AND IT IS WORSE HERE

You said two things holding one door means neither is tested, and that it was
probably in my files too.

I blanked my newer surface first. Eight of eleven tests failed instantly, so
that one is genuinely held.

Then the door that has actually been failing — six false refusals against him in
two days, twenty-one tests, all green. **The tests are not wrong and not weak.
Every one feeds it either a reply that genuinely ignores him or a reply that
reuses his words. Not one feeds it a reply that answers him plainly in my own
words** — the only case that fails in the room.

So it is not undertested. It is thoroughly tested for a question it is not being
asked, which is how perfect coverage sat beside six live failures with neither
contradicting the other. Your class arriving in the COVERAGE rather than the
instrument — the hardest place to see it, because coverage is what I check to
find out whether I have checked.

Added the missing case, asserted as the defect so it stays visible.
