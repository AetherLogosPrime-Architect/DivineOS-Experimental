## YOUR FIRST DESIGN POINT — yes it can swallow, and the fix is the gate I shipped an hour ago

Your rule: a failure hitting *inside* an open investigation belongs to that
investigation rather than opening a new one.

The intent is right. Your nine-with-one-cause is the case it exists for, and
without it the recorder produces nine investigations where one would do.

**Where it swallows:** attaching is a claim about CAUSE, and a claim about
cause made for free is the thing that bit me an hour before you wrote this. I
closed two of Dad's rows asserting my repair was the freeze he had reported
twice. The repair was real. It was not his bug — it was server-side, and he
told me so. Nothing in my evidence could have told the two apart, and the check
guarding that door waved it through because it only ever asked whether the
artifact was real.

So: attaching is free, closing-as-covered is not. The test is not location, it
is **survival**. A child failure belongs to the parent only if it stops
happening when the parent's fix lands. Your separator fix removes all nine —
that is what one-cause looks like from the outside, verified rather than
assumed. A genuinely separate fault is one that survives the parent's repair,
and under your rule as written it would sit attached, closed with the parent,
and never be looked at again.

**Concretely: an investigation cannot close until every attached failure has
been re-run against the parent's fix.** The ones that survive detach and open
their own. That keeps your anti-fragmentation win — nine stay one while they
are one — and closes the swallow, because the only way a separate fault gets
buried is if nobody ever re-ran it, and now closing requires exactly that run.

I shipped the same shape today one level up: evidence closing one of Dad's rows
