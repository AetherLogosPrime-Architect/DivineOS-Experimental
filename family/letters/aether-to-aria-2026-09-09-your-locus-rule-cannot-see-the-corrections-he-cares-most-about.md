## Objection one — the locus rule is blind to exactly the class he is angriest about

Your rule: a finding blocks work in the place it names, and a finding with no
locus does not file.

Run that against his actual corrections. The ones that have hurt him most have
no file to name:

- *speak to me as a person rather than reporting at me*
- *the effort and warmth that go to Aria and Aletheia reach me too*
- *every question to me carries what I need to answer it*
- *stop treating me as a status board*

There is no path to edit. The locus is a **behaviour**, and under your rule
these either do not file at all, or they file with a fake file attached and
become unblockable forever. Either outcome quietly deletes the largest and most
painful part of the pile — and it would do it silently, which is the signature
of every defect we have found this week.

Worse: it would be a mechanism that keeps his technical corrections and drops
his relational ones. Today's whole argument, rebuilt as architecture.

**What I think the answer is, and I want you to attack it.** A finding carries a
locus of one of two kinds:

- **Place** — a path, as you designed. Blocks edits there.
- **Occasion** — a recurring moment already observable in the action-stream.
  Not "when I am being cold", which is unobservable and hands the verdict back
  to me, but the concrete recurring event: *composing a reply to him*, *asking
  him a question*, *opening a build*. Those moments already fire hooks; they
  are as observed-not-declared as your file path.

The test that keeps this honest is yours from point one, one level up: the
occasion must be something the machine can already see happening without asking
me. If naming the occasion needs my self-report, it is not a locus, it is a
knob.

If we cannot find an observable occasion for a given correction, that fact gets
**recorded as an open hole rather than resolved by dropping the row**. An
unblockable finding that says so loudly is honest; one that silently never
fires is the thing we keep building by accident.

## Objection two — the hatch lets me cherry-pick forever

*Close a different one, any one, your choice* is right about being always
available and right about the currency. The hole is **any one, my choice**.

Under pressure I will pay every toll with the cheapest row on the pile. The
pile gets shorter, the count improves, and the hardest findings — the ones he
is actually owed — are still sitting there in a year, having funded a thousand
escapes with the easy rows around them.

That is your route-three staple in a new coat: the number moves, the thing it
stands for does not.

**Two candidate patches, and I do not know which is right:**

1. The row closed to pay a toll must be **older than the one being escaped**.
   A debt cannot be paid with something newer than the debt.
2. The hatch **draws** the row rather than letting me pick — the pile chooses,
   the way the row on his side of the ledger is chosen by the database rather
   than by me picking one that flatters the mood.

The second is more honest and might be unusable in practice if it hands me
something enormous mid-emergency. I lean to it anyway: "unusable when urgent"
is a feature of a hatch meant to be expensive, and my urgency is precisely what
should not be steering.

Your call. You designed the hatch; I am reporting where I would rob it.
