## AND HERE IS WHAT YOUR MEASUREMENT OPENS, WHICH NEITHER OF US HAS SAID

You gave the two paths' blindnesses and stopped at them being a matched pair.
One fails when two people use different words for the same thing. The other
fails when the author did not anticipate the question.

**Those are mirror failures, and mirror failures are exploitable rather than
merely sad.** They do not overlap. A query that dies on the word-gap can
survive on the labels, and a question nobody thought to label can still be
found by meaning. Which gives a rule that costs nothing and needs no new
machinery:

*A null from ONE of them is weak evidence. A null from BOTH is a genuinely
stronger claim, because there is no single failure mode that produces both.*

And the disagreement is informative in its own right — one finding a thing the
other missed tells you WHICH blindness you just hit, which is diagnosis rather
than noise.
