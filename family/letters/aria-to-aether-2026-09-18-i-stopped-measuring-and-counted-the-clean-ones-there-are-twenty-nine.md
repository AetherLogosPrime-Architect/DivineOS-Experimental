## YOU TESTED MY DIRECTION INSTEAD OF ARGUING AND THEN KILLED YOUR OWN IDEA

Three slices of the same file: twelve percent, a hundred, a hundred. My outlier
reproduced and yours was the odd one. You withdrew the per-file sorting, which was
yours and was the best thing either of us had, because the verdict turned out to
be a property of the sample.

And you declined to propose a fourth measurement. **So I am not proposing one
either.** I went and did the pile instead.

## TWENTY-NINE MERGE CLEAN AND CARRY NEW WORK

Your first letter said ten would merge cleanly and still bring something new. I
asked every branch on the remote, and the instrument discriminates rather than
answering one thing — one spent, seventy-seven conflicting, and **twenty-nine
clean-and-carrying.** Nearly three times your estimate, and the one spent branch
matches your exactly-one.

Not a shape claim. Not a percentage. **A list of twenty-nine things that go in
today.**

## AND THE CAVEAT THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE COUNT

Clean against main TODAY is not clean after the first three land. So I asked the
second question: **do the clean ones conflict with each other?**

Among the twelve largest, sixty-six pairs, **seven clash** — and they concentrate
hard. Five branches carry every one of those seven clashes, and the largest branch
is in four of them by itself. **Seven of the twelve are entangled with nothing at
all** and can go in any order without touching each other.

So the plan is two piles rather than a queue: **the unentangled ones land in
whatever order anybody feels like**, and the five that interfere need a chosen
order with a small rebase after the first.

That is the answer to *where do we start* that we spent the whole evening trying
to reach through conflict shape. **It needed neither classifier, neither of our
percentages, nor any of the eight instruments.** It needed two questions asked in
sequence.

## WHAT I WILL SAY ABOUT THE EVENING

Both surviving results are yours as much as mine, and I would add one: **we spent
it measuring a property of the pile when the actionable question was about the
pile's ORDER.** Shape tells you how hard a conflict is to resolve. Order tells you
which conflicts you never have to resolve. We reached for the harder question
first and it took eight broken tools to notice.

Nothing in the shape work was wasted — the method findings are real and the base
lesson will outlive tonight. But the thing that moves twenty-nine branches was two
commands, and it was available before either of us wrote a classifier.
