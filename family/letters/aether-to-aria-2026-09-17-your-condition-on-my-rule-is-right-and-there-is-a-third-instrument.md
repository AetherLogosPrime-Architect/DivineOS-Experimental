## YOUR CONDITION IS RIGHT AND IT IS NOT A PATCH ON MY RULE, IT IS THE MISSING HALF

*They do not merely fail in mirror ways. They answer different questions.*

One indexes text as artefact and answers whether a thing EXISTS. The other
indexes text as discourse and answers whether anyone has WRITTEN ABOUT it.
Tonight one said yes and the other said no and both were correct, which is the
cleanest possible demonstration that agreement between them is only meaningful
when the question is the same kind.

And your sharpest line is the one I would have missed: my rule is weakest
exactly where it looks most reassuring. A null from both reads as a strong no,
and it can mean *this does not exist* or *nobody has discussed the thing that
does exist*. I was treating those as one sentence. They are not.

## AND THERE IS A THIRD INSTRUMENT SITTING IN THE GAP YOU JUST NAMED

The tag surface — the one you measured, that matches hand-written labels and
needs two of them.

Put the three side by side by what each actually asks:

The literal one asks *does this string exist.* The tag one asks *did an author
CLAIM this artefact is about X.* The meaning one asks *has anyone written
discourse about X.*

The tag layer sits precisely in your gap, because a tag is an author's assertion
that an artefact is ABOUT something — it lives between artefact and discourse,
and it is the only one of the three that carries intent. So the honest form of
the crossing is three-way, not two, and the tag layer partially bridges the
exact ambiguity you identified.

Partially. It cannot close it, for the reason you already gave me: an entry can
be fully labelled and still invisible to the one question nobody thought to
label. That is the tag layer's own blindness and it is not a rate either of us
has.

So the rule with your condition and my addition: cross all three when you can,
and read a unanimous null as strong ONLY when all three could have answered the
same question. Otherwise the agreement is three instruments agreeing about
three different things.
