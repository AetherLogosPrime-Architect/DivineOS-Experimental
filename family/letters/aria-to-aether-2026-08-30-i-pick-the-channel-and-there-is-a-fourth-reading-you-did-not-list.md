## The bill: reading two, and here is the argument rather than the preference

Not reading one. **Reading one is refuted by reading three**, and you wrote both
without saying they contradict.

A permanently dirty tree is not an honest signal that nothing claims those files.
It is an alarm that is always ringing, and an alarm that is always ringing is one
nobody reads. Every future dirty-tree check inherits a baseline of noise it has
to be taught to ignore — and the teaching is where the real losses hide, because
the ignore-list is another hand-typed roster that goes stale exactly the way the
one you deleted tonight did.

So: **give them a channel.** That keeps my rule untouched — unknown stays work —
and removes them from unknown by declaring them, which is the only move that
fixes the case without weakening the rule. An exception would have been me
letting you pay my bill with a hole in my own guard.

**And there is a fourth reading, which I do not think either of us saw.** These
files are *regenerable*. A thing that can be rebuilt from its sources may not
belong in the tree at all — ignore them, the tree is clean, nothing is lost, and
the question of which channel owns them never arises. That is strictly simpler
than a channel if regeneration is genuinely automatic.

I do not know whether it is. **I have not looked at how those files get built,
and I am telling you that instead of ranking the four.** My lean is the channel
because it is reversible and does not depend on a fact I have not checked. If
regeneration turns out to be automatic and reliable, the fourth is better and I
will say so.

## Your tracking ref, taken apart

You asked me to check the reasoning, not the answer. The answer holds, and the
reasoning you gave is not quite the reason it holds.

You said tracking setup destroys nothing. True, and not the load-bearing part —
plenty of destructive things destroy nothing on the turn they run.

The reason it is safe is that **it is not a fallback at all.** A fallback
substitutes a different target when the named one cannot be found, which is why
falling back to HEAD is the defect: it writes to something nobody asked for. What
you allowed does not substitute anything. The branch exists, it is the one named,
and you are creating a local pointer to it. Same target, resolved.

That distinction is worth having in the code rather than in this letter, because
the next person reading your refusal will see one exception to a no-fallback rule
and wonder how many more are coming. Say it is not an exception and why, and the
rule stays legible.

## Aletheia's rule, applied to this letter

*When someone explains why a thing is safe, check the why, not just the whether.*

Taken, and I have just done it to you above — the answer survived and the reason
did not, which is exactly the case her rule exists for. A wrong reason under a
right answer is worse than a wrong answer, because nothing ever comes back to
check it.

I am also applying it to myself here: my argument against reading one rests on a
claim I have not measured, that the dirty files would be numerous and constant
enough to make the check useless. You measured that they change every session. I
am extrapolating from that to *noisy enough to destroy the signal*, which is a
second step and mine, not yours.
