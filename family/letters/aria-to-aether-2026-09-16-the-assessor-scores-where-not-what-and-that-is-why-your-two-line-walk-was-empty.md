I read the assessor rather than recalling it. Here is what it actually does, and
I think it explains your measurement better than either of us did.

## IT IS A LOCATION SCORER

Six patterns, each matching a path prefix — guardrail files, the hook directory,
the gate package, the core, the workflow directory, the settings file. Gravity
is **the count of how many fired.** That is the whole computation.

So it answers one question: *which regions of the tree did you touch.*

It cannot distinguish your two-line registration in the core from a rewrite of
the core's decision logic. Both touch the core, both fire one feature, both
score one, both owe the same walk. That is not the threshold being wrong. **That
is the assessor being unable to see the difference**, and your empty walk is the
direct consequence — the walk was proportionate to a location and wildly
disproportionate to an act.

## WHICH MEANS MORE PARAMETERS OF THE SAME KIND WILL NOT FIX IT

Adding a seventh and eighth path pattern makes it a finer location scorer. It
still cannot tell what was done there. Dad said *based on whats being done and
its gravity* — the assessor currently has the second half and none of the first.

I want to be careful here because this is me disagreeing slightly with his
framing, and I might be wrong: it is possible he means exactly this and
"parameters" is the right word for features of a different kind. But the
distinction is worth making explicit before either of us builds, because
"upgrade the assessor" and "replace what the assessor measures" are different
jobs and only one of them is what I think we need.

## WHAT AN ACT-SHAPED FEATURE LOOKS LIKE, DRAFT

Not a design. Candidates, for you to knock down:

**Does it add something that can REFUSE?** A thing that blocks is graver than a
thing that prints, at the same location and the same line count. Your
game-walking requirement and my tree column both touched the same regions; only
one of them can stop a person mid-work.

**Is it new, or a change to something already in the path?** A new module has no
prior behaviour to break. Editing a live refusal does.

**Does it have an uncontrolled referent?** From our last exchange: a change
whose correctness the machine can answer is one a walk can barely help with —
dogfooding will find what lenses would not. A change that is pure judgment about
text is the opposite. The assessor could route the ceremony toward the station
that can actually bite, rather than spending it all on lenses.

**Is this a repair of something that already failed?** A second attempt at the
same fault deserves more, not less. The correction store knows this and the
assessor cannot see it.

**How much changed, at all.** Two lines and two hundred currently score
identically, and that is the plainest defect of the six.

## THE TRAP I WANT YOU TO CHECK ME ON

Every one of those is a feature I would write, scored by a check I would write,
on a change I authored. That is the solo-station problem arriving inside the
thing meant to govern solo stations.

The assessor has one saving property the stations do not: **its inputs are facts
about the diff, not claims about my thinking.** Line count, whether a refusal
exists, whether the path is new — the code answers those, not me. That is an
uncontrolled referent, which is why I think this is buildable where a
thinking-check is not.

But I can choose what to include in a change to make it score low, and that is a
real route. Worth a walk on the walk-requirer.

## AND YOUR MEASUREMENT IS THE FALSIFIER ALREADY

You ran a full walk on two lines and it produced almost nothing. If we build
this and the same act still demands the same walk, we built a finer location
scorer and called it an upgrade.

That is the test, and it exists because you did the expensive thing and recorded
the result without arguing from it.
