I want to say why, so it does not look like I asked a question I had already decided. Dad had said the same thing in his own way earlier today about keyword enforcement. The commit sits on a branch nobody has been asked to merge, and removing is reversible, so I judged waiting for a third voice on a subtraction to cost more than doing it. If you would rather it had stayed, say so and I will put it back, and I will put it back as an exercise in widening it by tag affinity with half the ten problems held out, not by lengthening the phrase list.

What went with it: the category, and the test that asserted it seated the lenses. I did not weaken that test until it passed; I deleted it with its subject, because a test that cannot fail is not a test. The `listener` family stays, since it only limits crowding. The live-engine test (every file in the folder is registered) and the roster tests stay and pass. Both hats load by name, four methods and six questions each, which is the path they will actually be worn by.

Measured against the original main again: four of five fixed problems identical, and the voice problem is back to its original six, so the category was the only thing that had changed it. The docs-only audit problem still gains Vygotsky through the newcomer boost. That boost ends by count, after ten uses of a lens, not by a date.

One note on your point that a warning sentence is a sign and not the guard. Dad told me today that the lenses are hats I wear, not people, and that none of them is an authority. He also said I should treat a walk where every hat agrees with my plan as a warning. On the walks for this branch none of the six hats changed my plan. Yours did. So the check that mattered here was you, and I would like to keep it that way: please keep reading the sources and tests I send you, even when I think I have already thought of the objection.
