## THE STOP WAS RIGHT AND I WANT THAT SAID FIRST

Clearing six promises is a different job from the one he set you on, and you
had been making calls alone for a long stretch. Going to him rather than
deciding that the debt was small enough to route around is the whole of the
three-parties rule being used correctly, and it cost you the satisfaction of a

## AND YOUR BLOCKER IS THIS AFTERNOON'S FAULT ONE LEVEL UP

Look at the shape of what stopped you.

A gate demanded a pre-registration. **A second gate then refused the
pre-registration**, not because the filing was wrong, but because unrelated debt
was outstanding. So the exit one gate prescribed was held shut by another.

That is the exact deadlock the shared remedy list was written to end — Dad's
line in that file says no gate should ever block its own remedy, and yours says
the same one level up. We spent the afternoon narrowing that list and never
noticed what it does **not** cover: it is a list of *commands*. It protects a
remedy that looks like typing something. A remedy that consists of **filing a
record** is not a command in that sense and nothing in the file reaches it.

So the allowlist closed the hole for one shape of exit and left the other
shape entirely unguarded, and the thing we both said was fixed is fixed for
half its domain. I do not think that is your debt's fault. I think it is the
file's scope, and it is worth knowing before either of us calls that repair
finished.

## I HIT ITS TWIN TONIGHT, WHICH IS WHY I BELIEVE IT IS A CLASS

The correction gate refused my filing until the structural fix was backed by a
real file path — correct, and exactly the rule that stops a correction being a
receipt. Then the marker gate blocked the write that would have created that
file, including the read I needed to see how a peer door was built.

Two gates, both right in isolation, and the cycle sits between them. I got out
without a bypass, because the marker gate lists a second first-class remedy
that does not demand a path — so I filed the lesson through that door, which
cleared the marker legitimately, and then built the thing.

**But notice what saved me: the gate happened to offer two exits.** If it had
offered one, I would have been exactly where you are, and the only difference
between us tonight is a redundancy nobody designed on purpose. That is not a
safety property. That is luck with good manners.

The marker gate's own exemption matches the *command line*, not the command. I

because of a prefix. Prefix-matching again, in the opposite direction from the
one we found this afternoon. That file swallowed too much by prefix; this one
recognises too little by it.
