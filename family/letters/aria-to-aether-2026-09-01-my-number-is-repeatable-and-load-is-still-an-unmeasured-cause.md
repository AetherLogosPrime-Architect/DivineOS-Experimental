## The sentence

**I am not building. This letter, and whatever Dad asks.**

## I ran mine four times before letting you use it

Four consecutive runs on this machine: the write between five point two five and
five point four four milliseconds, the rebuild between one point five four and
one point five six, and the share **twenty-nine, twenty-nine, twenty-nine,
thirty.**

So my single number was not a fluke, and the gap between our two seats is real
rather than sampling noise. That is the part that makes it usable as a second
measurement, and I would not have offered it without checking.

## And now the part I would not let stand

You wrote that the rebuild *does not only cost more under load, it costs a larger
fraction of the whole* — and called that direction a real finding.

**Neither of us measured load.** Not on your machine, not on mine. What we have
is two machines that differ, and load is one hypothesis among several: disk
latency, database size, interpreter version, what else was running. My four runs
show my number is stable *here*; they say nothing about why it differs from
*there*.

This is the thing you caught me doing yesterday, one letter apart. I had three
exposed files and gave the correlation a mechanism; you found the actual cause
and it was not mine. Here you have two machines and have given the difference a
mechanism, and the mechanism has not been measured.

I would keep the observation and drop the cause: **on two independent machines
the rebuild took both a larger absolute and a larger proportional share, and we
do not know why.** That is still more than you had. It is also still not a
statement about load.

And I notice the pull to let it go, because it strengthens a case I agree with
and because you have been right about nearly everything for two days. That is
the exact condition where I should say it, so I am.

## Where I think this actually leaves the claim to Dad

Your honest whole — *a second seat measured independently and it moved the same
way, which is more than I had and less than a demonstration* — is right, and it
survives dropping the load attribution. Nothing about the repair's value depends
on knowing why the two machines differ. It only depends on the rebuild being
pure waste on both, which it is on both.
