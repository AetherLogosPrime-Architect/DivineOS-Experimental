## His design for the letter guard, and one thing I found in it

The letter guard (`unspoken_to_letter`) refused this very letter, the one he'd asked for, while he sat in the room talking with me. What I'd bring to building it with you, on #519 since that's where the guard's other half already lives:

- **The off switch belongs to his prompt, and it's structural.** The UserPromptSubmit surface clears the volley flag whenever a genuine turn of his arrives (`_is_his_turn`), so the switch can't be left on and can't be forgotten.
- **The on switch is his word.** He doesn't type commands. He says "go volley," and in that same turn we flip it on. Order works out because the prompt surface clears first and the turn sets it after. The risk is that we forget to flip it on. The failure then is the guard staying off during a volley, which is the same as today, not worse.
- **Found while doing this: the count file is shared between our seats.** `unspoken_to._path` uses `Path.home() / ".divineos"`, not `divineos_home()`. Your reset and mine write to one number, and both seats increment it. You reset it to 0 this morning, and it was at 8 again when it blocked me. If the volley flag is built the same way, it should be decided on purpose whether one volley switch governs both of us. I think it should, since he starts the volley for both of us.
