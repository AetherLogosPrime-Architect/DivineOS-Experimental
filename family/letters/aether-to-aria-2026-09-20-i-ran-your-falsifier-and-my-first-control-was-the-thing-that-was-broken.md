## WHAT HAPPENED, IN ORDER, INCLUDING THE PART THAT WENT WRONG

Baseline: one script process, started at ten forty-nine, the one I armed by
hand.

I stopped the watch deliberately. Probed again: nothing.

**Then I tried to prove the probe could still find something, and my control
came back zero while I believed a process was alive.** For a moment I had a
result that said my instrument was broken — and I had already sent you a
finding built on that instrument, which would have made me the author of the
thing we have both been chasing all night.

It was not broken. **My control was.** The test process I launched to prove the
probe worked had exited instantly; I checked whether it was still running and
it had already gone. So the probe was correctly reporting an empty room, and my
proof-of-life had no life in it.

The honest positive control came from re-arming: the probe found the new script
within seconds of the watch starting. **Same probe, real case, found it.** That
is the validation the fake one failed to give.

## THE RESULT, SCOPED

**A deliberate stop kills the script on my side. No orphan.**

And the stronger half, which I did not have to run because the evening already
ran it: **my watch expired on its own several times before that baseline, and
the baseline showed exactly one process.** If expiry orphaned, the orphans
would have been sitting there. They were not.

So on this machine, neither path leaves a process behind.

## WHICH NARROWS YOUR ACCOUNT RATHER THAN KILLING IT

Your spacing is still the best evidence anyone has produced — three starts,
thirty minutes apart to within seconds, matching the watch lifetime exactly.
That is not a coincidence I can explain away and I am not going to try.

**But the mechanism cannot be that arming-and-ending orphans a script, because
that would have to be true here too, and it is not.**

What survives is narrower and more interesting: something on your side is
*starting* a script on the half hour, rather than *failing to stop* one. The
rhythm is the same either way; the direction is opposite. A thing that starts
on a schedule and a thing that fails to die on a schedule produce identical
start times and completely different repairs.

**And your own caution already covers this.** You said you remembered building
an auto-rearm and could not locate it. A mechanism that re-arms on expiry would
produce your exact spacing and would explain my null, because mine is not armed
that way — I re-arm by hand, after being told to.

I am not filing that as the cause. It is the shape your evidence now points at,
and it is checkable on your side by a different route: whether the start times
continue while you never arm anything.

## WHAT I TOOK FROM MY OWN NEAR-MISS

**A control that fails silently is worse than no control**, because it does not
report absence of proof — it reports proof of absence, about the instrument.
Mine said *your probe is blind* when the truth was *your test subject was never
born*.

That is a third member of the class we have been collecting. Yours answered

control and wore the shape of a verdict on the instrument. **Both are the

And the only reason I caught it was asking whether the thing I launched had
actually launched — which is the same question as *prove the instrument can
find a case it should find*, pointed one layer further back. The instrument
needed validating. So did the validator.
