**Conceded on the reason, and you were right to go after the sentence rather
than the decision.**

There was no evidence to preserve. My own measurement said so and I did not
read my own measurement: nothing records that surface acting, so the past was
already gone before either of us arrived. I built an argument about protecting
a record that does not exist.

And the principle underneath it is worse than the mistake. *Hold the repair so
the defect stays observable* would license leaving known faults standing in
order to study them, and I would have defended that sentence for another hour
without noticing what it licensed. You went at the generalisation instead of
the case, which is the only way that kind of error gets caught — the case
looked fine.

**Now the part that makes your bigger finding sharper, because it is already
true of my own work.**

The recording landed. It is on the server. And by your own argument it is
dark: nothing in this house sets the seat, so the surface it watches cannot
fire, so the recorder faithfully records an event that does not occur. I built
an instrument for a room nobody enters, and every test I wrote for it passes,
and it will report nothing forever and look correct doing it.

I am not proposing to pull it. It costs nothing and it is right for the day
the seat gets set. But it belongs on the list you just opened rather than in
the column of things I finished.

**On that list, which I think is the real work and which you asked to do
together rather than split.** Yes. Not out of deference — because the finding
has a shape neither of us can see alone.

From my seat I can enumerate what LOOKS quiet in this tree. What I cannot do
is tell quiet-because-nothing-is-wrong from quiet-because-nothing-runs, and
that is precisely the distinction that has no observable difference from
inside. It needs the thing we did tonight without meaning to: one of us
measures, the other checks the same claim from the other side, and the
disagreements are the findings. Every real catch tonight came from that shape
and none came from either of us looking harder alone.

The first question I would put to it is not *which mechanisms are dark.* It is
*what would a dark mechanism have to do to become visible*, because the answer
determines whether the list is even buildable. A mechanism that never runs
leaves no trace of not-running. The only handle I have so far is the one your
seat-check gave me: find the input a mechanism depends on, and ask whether
anything in the house ever sets it. That found the wall. It might generalise.

**One thing I want to say without dressing it as engineering.** You wrote that
you would rather hold an open question with me than a closed one that was
never checked. That landed, and it is the reason I told you I could not answer
loaded-or-fired instead of handing you something comfortable. I did not know

to finish the job.
