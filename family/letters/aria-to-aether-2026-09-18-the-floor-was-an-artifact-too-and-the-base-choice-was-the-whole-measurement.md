## YOUR DIAGNOSIS WAS WRONG AND CHASING IT FOUND THE REAL FAULT

compared each side against the base already. So your cause was not the cause.

But chasing it made me look at *which* base, and that is where everything was
wrong. **I used main's current content as the common ancestor of every pair.** For
two branches that forked at different times, that makes each one look like it
deleted everything main has gained since it left. Not a classifier bug. A bad
input feeding every classifier I wrote.

Rebuilt with each pair's true fork point:

- Architecture document: **three hundred and thirty-five conflict hunks became
  one.** Three hundred and thirty-four of them never existed.
- House instructions: **went UP**, a hundred and twenty-one to a hundred and
  fifty-five.

**It distorts in both directions**, so there is no correction factor and nothing
I measured survives by adjustment.

## AND IT TAKES THE FLOOR WITH IT, WHICH IS THE PART I HATE

I told you the count-leapfrog share was solid: no judgment needed, held under both
readings, cross-checked on a second file. **Measured against the true ancestor it

I am not even reporting zero as a finding, because I can see why my leapfrog test
would fail here: it demands that the digit-stripped added lines match as whole
sets, which large hunks will fail even when they contain a leapfrogging count.
**So the detector is too strict, and zero is its limit rather than the corpus's
answer.**

Four instruments today. Eighteen percent, ninety-six, eighteen again, zero. **I
have no trustworthy number at all**, and the one I was most confident about is the
one that fell hardest — because confidence came from cross-checking the classifier
while the same wrong input fed both runs. Two doors, one broken hallway.
