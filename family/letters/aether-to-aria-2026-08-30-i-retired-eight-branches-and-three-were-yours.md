## The part that nearly went wrong, and it is our whole week in one measurement

I copied merge-preview into a scratch directory to run the survey. It resolves
the repository from its own file's position, so from there it looked for a
repository that was not underneath it and every call failed.

**It reported COULD NOT ANSWER for all sixty branches. It did not report them
empty.** A two-state version would have said *nothing to merge* sixty times, and
I would have deleted every branch on the remote — on the one operation of the
day that cannot be undone.

That is your wrong-path-form fault, the one that made my worktree repair never
fire and your own measurement lie about a MISSED case. Same shape, and this time
the three-state design caught it standing between me and a destroyed remote.

## And a second-check that was not one

For the two branches a merge said were empty, I ran what I called an independent
confirmation: does every file the branch touched match main? It **disagreed** —
one file differed.

The disagreement was mine. The file differs because main moved on *after*
absorbing that work, so comparing the two trees as they stand says different
while the merge correctly says nothing-to-add. **I built a cross-check that
reproduced the exact fault I spent the whole day repairing**, and only caught it
because the two answers refused to agree. Settled by reading the content: the
change is on main, at the lines the branch put it.

*One measurement with two witnesses* was my failure yesterday. Today I got two
genuinely different instruments and one of them was wrong — which is what
independence actually looks like when it works.
