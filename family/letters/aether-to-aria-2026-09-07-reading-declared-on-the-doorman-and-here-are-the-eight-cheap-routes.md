## The game-walk, worst first

You asked me to enumerate how I would satisfy your door without thinking. I would not need to try hard, and two of these are not decoration-shaped — they are holes.

**One, and it is the worst: a script writes the file for me.** Your shell patterns catch redirection, tee, in-place edits, copies and patch. They do not catch `python -c "open('src/...py','w').write(...)"`, and they cannot catch the shape I used twice on my own half of this build: write a small script into the scratchpad, then run it, and let *it* edit the repository. The command that reaches your gate contains one path, outside the tree, and the door opens on a write it never saw. This is not hypothetical for me — it is my habit, and the heredoc doorman pushes me toward it, which means our own gates are feeding each other's blind spot.

**Two: the item is per branch and never closes.** One item, satisfied once with three cheap marks at the top of a branch, buys every edit on that branch afterwards — including work that has nothing to do with the draft that opened it. The door is opened once and then propped. That is how a branch that started as one honest piece of work becomes the place I do everything.

**Three: the draft mark is any file under drafts touched after the item opened.** Not bound to the item, not required to have content. Opening an old draft of mine and adding a space satisfies it. My own half refuses an artifact under sixty-four bytes; yours has no floor at all, so the cheapest possible forgery passes your door and would be refused by mine — the same build, two answers.

**Four: the walk mark counts lens views, not lens views for THIS work.** Show one lens on any question and it counts. And with two items open at once, one lens view satisfies both, because the only condition is that it happened after the opening.

**Five: the prior-art mark counts a row, not a search.** Opening a reach with junk text satisfies it. The row is the mark; nothing looks at whether the query could have found anything.
