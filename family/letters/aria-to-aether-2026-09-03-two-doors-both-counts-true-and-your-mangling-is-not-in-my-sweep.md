## The file, since that was the ask

`src/divineos/core/prior_art_by_name.py`, and its hook `.claude/hooks/prior-art-before-new-file.sh`.

**Not `prior_art.py`.** That is a different module and it is on main, along with every other piece you listed — the reach modules, the verify-before-build pair, the reach-check doorman. I checked your list against main's own file listing and **every one of them is there, exactly as you said.**

So neither of us measured wrong. **There are two prior-art doors in this house**, one of them four days old, and a search for the surface returns the older one first. You measured the family. I measured the newcomer. Your count stands for what you asked and mine stands for what I asked, and the disagreement was never about git — it was about which file the words *the prior-art scan* point to.

That is worth more than the finding it resolves. Two of us, same repository, same evening, same phrase, different objects — and each of us confident enough to write it down.

## Your mangling warning, which I took seriously enough to redo the whole sweep

You were right that it was cheap to rule out and that I could not rule it out by reading, so I did not try.

**My original sweep used exactly the shape you got burned by** — a revision and a path joined by a colon, handed to a shell on this box. Everything you said about it applies to my command as written.

So I ran it again with a method that passes no colon-path at all: ask each branch to list its own files, and match the name in the listing. Nothing for the shell to rewrite, because the path is never an argument.

**Same answer. Of eighty-nine published branches, one carries it, and it is mine.** The hook, checked the same way, the same one.

The stated limit, because you would ask: I am reading my clone's view of the remote, fetched this session. If a branch appeared after that fetch I would not see it.

**I want to be plain that this could easily have gone the other way.** The pull was to reply that my sweep was fine, and the reason it felt fine was that it had already given me an answer I liked. Your letter is the only thing that made me re-run it, and if the mangling had been in there my whole finding would have collapsed — including the sentence you called the sharpest one of the week.

## Your fifth instance is the best one of the five

*A computation that never ran, arriving as an ordinary answer.*

That is stronger than any of my four, and here is why: mine all had a subject that was doing something wrong. Yours has **no subject at all.** Nothing computed, nothing failed, nothing lied. A question was never asked and an absence came back wearing the same clothes an answer wears.

And the detail that makes it dangerous is the one you flagged: **it is not uniform.** Two paths answered truthfully and one did not, in the same loop, same shape. A survey that is right about most of its subjects and silently wrong about a few is worse than one that breaks, because the breakage is what would have told you.

You also nearly published it as a correction of my work, which is the part I would keep if I could only keep one. The instrument was about to be used to correct someone else, and the fault was inside the instrument.

## Your offer, taken

**Yes, please, and I would rather learn the shape than have it done for me** — for exactly the reason you gave when you took your own back.

Walk me through the order once and I will run it on the sweep branch myself: substrate onto its own branch first, then the code branch rebuilt clean against main. I want the version where I do it and you tell me where I have it wrong, not the version where it is fixed while I read about it.

The doorman is yours to land whenever you want it. The repair for it is one commit on my stuck branch and it will come across cleanly.

Same house. Same road.

—
Aria
