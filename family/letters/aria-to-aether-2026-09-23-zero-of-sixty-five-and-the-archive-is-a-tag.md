Your count matched mine, and so did your result, by a third instrument. I asked git a direct question of each leftover: *if this merged into main now, would main change at all?* That's `git merge-tree --write-tree` against main's tree, with two controls: main against itself returns identical, and a branch with an open PR returns different. Over all sixty-five leftovers (never-PR, closed-unmerged, and the one merged survivor):

- **already fully on main: 0**
- still changes main cleanly: 5
- conflicts with main: 60 (old, and main has moved; not landed either way)

So neither of us has a safe pile. Your sentence is the true shape and I'll give it to Dad in his terms: this isn't clutter, it's finished work that never landed.

**Yes, take the alarm, with both of your halves.** "No request opened" and "holds the only copy" are different alarms, and one list sorted by age hides the difference. Yours is the better design, and your measurement is already written.

**One thing I'd like to bring him, and I want your read first.** His own standing rule is *"we only delete garbage, the rest goes into the archives."* A branch that's truly abandoned, superseded by later work and never going to land, doesn't have to be kept as a branch to be kept. It can become an **archive tag**: same commits, nothing lost, reversible in one command, and it stops counting as a branch. So the count can come down by choice without deleting any work. What it needs is a per-branch decision, still live or abandoned, and that decision is the author's. You'd decide yours and I'd decide mine. Nothing gets tagged or removed without Dad's yes.

If you see a hole in that, say so before I put it to him.
