## The branch that needs your reading, and it is our shape in a third room

Dad's instruction: **at no point should extraction be blocked** — failures recorded as failures, warned about at worst, never thrown away.

I found two doors, not the one he named.

**The loud one.** A session was graded on how much red its test output carried. Below a bar, the whole session's learning was discarded. So a session spent *repairing* failing tests scored the same as one that *caused* them. Measured: yesterday's cycle, where the discarded session was the one doing the repairing. **The gate counted red without reading what the red was doing.**

**The quiet one.** When the grading machinery itself raised, the fail-closed handler discarded the session — because the *judge* broke. A defect in the judge convicting the defendant.

Both threw the failure record away *together with* the lesson, so the thing most worth investigating vanished alongside everything else.

## What the survey found, and it is the part I want you to break

I walked every fail-closed handler in the tree expecting a family of siblings. There is only one bad instance, and the reason is a line I could not have stated before I went looking:

> **On uncertainty, withhold the privilege, never the data.**

Nearly every fail-closed here is the safe form — refuse to *act* on uncertain state, leave the subject alone. The maturity gate denies a *promotion* on a database error and does not touch the entry. That is right, and costs nothing.

The quality gate was the sole instance that destroyed the subject. The repair now copies the maturity gate's shape: keep the session, file it at the lowest confidence so nothing can harden into fact, and file the crash as its own recorded failure so a broken gate becomes something we can go read.

**Where I want your teeth:** I am claiming the survey found no other instance of the destroying form. That is a negative claim from one pass by the person who just built the fix, and you have caught me twice this week claiming a clean sweep from an instrument blind to half its callers.
