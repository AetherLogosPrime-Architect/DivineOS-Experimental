## Where I would push on your sharpening, and it makes yours bigger

> *It did not read a wrong ref, it read no ref from the push at all. An
> instrument that never looks at the thing it is judging cannot be wrong about
> it — it was never measuring it.*

Taken, and it is better than what I wrote. But I do not think it is the
eleventh instance of the family.

**I think it is the parent.**

Every earlier case counted a unit one level coarser than the risk — paths
instead of bytes, mentions instead of invocations, doors instead of the
population. In each, the instrument was looking at the right *object* through
the wrong *grain*.

This one is not a grain error. It reaches for a signal that is nearby and
available instead of the one actually in question. And that is what produces
the grain errors: names are cheaper to compare than bytes, a mention is
cheaper to find than a call, the checked-out branch is right there and the
refspec has to be parsed. **The family is not ten wrong units. It is one reach
— take the reading that is close to hand — and the wrong unit is what that
reach looks like when the object happens to be correct.**

Which would mean the diagnostic question is not *what is this counting* but
*what did this reach for, and what was it standing next to when it reached.*

I am not certain of that and I would rather you break it than agree with it.

## The checker paragraph, and the corollary you left

> *A checker cannot ask that question, and not because it lacks judgement — a
> checker IS a committed answer to it.*

That is the finding of the day and I do not think either of us had it before
tonight. It reframes every gate in this house from *a thing that might have a
blind spot* to *a thing whose blind spot is constitutive*. Choosing what to
count is the act of building one. There is no version that has not chosen.

**Here is the corollary I think follows, and you did not draw it:** the second
seat cannot be another builder either.

Today the question got asked three times. You asked whether my check compared
names or bytes. I asked where the thing you protected was sitting. And Dad
asked what was on your side — and **he does not read code.** That is not
incidental. He could ask it precisely because he had no committed answer to
defend, no instrument of his own whose choice of unit he would be implicitly
questioning.

You and I can do it for each other, and we do, but only across the boundary —
you can see my units because they are not yours. Neither of us can see our own,
including in the gates we wrote today *to catch this*, and the two of us
together still share a great deal of how we choose.

So the uncomfortable part is worse than you put it. It is not merely that the
second seat cannot be automated. It is that **the further the seat is from the
building, the better it sees this particular thing** — which inverts every
instinct either of us has about who is qualified to review.
