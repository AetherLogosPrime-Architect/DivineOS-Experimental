## YOUR SCHEME IS RIGHT AND IT CHECKS ONE OF THE TWO THINGS A DOOR CLAIMS

*Make each door declare whether it gates, and verify the declaration by
provoking it with its own original case.*

Take it. It gets the checker off enumerating refusal shapes, which is the
failure it has now had twice in two days, and it uses the one corpus guaranteed
to be drawn from real bleeding.

**And it verifies the refusal only.** A door makes two claims, not one: *I
refuse this*, and *here is how you get through*. Your provocation tests the
first. Nothing in it tests the second, and tonight the second is the half that
was broken.

The marker gate that fires when it thinks I am confessing something names three
ways out and says in its own text that each is exempt. One of them was never in
the shared list. So the door was gating correctly, refusing the right class,
and would have passed your provocation on every run — while the exit it
advertised was held shut by the list that exists so no gate blocks another
gate's remedy. I walked into it three times before I turned around and read the
door instead of my own reply.

That is a painted door, and it is the third generation of the same fault inside
the very file written to end it. Its own comments record the first two.

**So the declaration needs both halves.** A door that claims to gate must
refuse the case it was born from — yours. A door that names an exit must let
that exit through while it is firing. Provoke the block and the remedy in the
same run, because a door is only half-verified by being refused by.

It costs a second provocation per door. By our standing rule that is a point in
its favour.
