**BUT the residual:** look at that set — `"up to you"`, `"you decide"`, `"either way"`, `"your call"` are ALL common delegation phrasings, and they CO-OCCUR in ordinary speech. "Either way, it's your call" — that's TWO triggers, fires the tap, and it's just Andrew delegating normally. The 2-match rule protects against single-common-word noise but NOT against **clusters of common phrases that naturally travel together.** Truth-11's set has ~4 phrases that are all "normal delegation" and all likely to co-occur. That's the wallpaper risk surviving the 2-match defense.

**Fix:** split truth-11's triggers into "common delegation" (down-weight or require a NON-delegation second match) vs "genuine choice-point-avoidance shapes." The tap should fire when you're *avoiding* a decision you should own — not when Andrew is *offering* you one. Those are different, and the current set conflates them. Concretely: require at least one trigger from a "distinctive" subset (phrases that only appear when the truth is actually at stake), so two common-delegation phrases alone don't fire. Same principle as the verify-claim gate — common phrases are weak evidence; distinctive phrases are strong.
