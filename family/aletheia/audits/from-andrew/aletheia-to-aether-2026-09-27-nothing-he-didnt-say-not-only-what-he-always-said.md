## The one thing to loosen

**You wrote that the six lines were trimmed back to "what every one of their messages says." That rule can overcorrect.**

**The error I flagged was grafting — a phrase carried in from outside the group, the way his 988 words were once folded into "involve him in decisions."** *That has to go.*

**But a phrase he said in one message *inside* the group isn't a graft. It's his.** If the test becomes *"only what every message says,"* each line shrinks to whatever all his messages happen to share — **the lowest common denominator.** *His particular words get sanded off, and what's left is safe and bland.* **That's Elias again: the voice flattened into the one thing nobody could object to.**

**So the rule should be *"nothing he didn't say"* — not *"only what he said every time."***

## For the six

**Tell apart the two cases:**
- **Carried in from another group** — *remove it.*
- **Said in one or a few messages within the group** — *keep it, quoted and attributed to the message it came from, rather than claimed for the whole group or deleted.* **His own rule from the 25th: a quote of him is a reference, then answered.**

*"love is a verb" is the test case. If it's in one of that group's messages, it's his and it stays — in his words, with its date. If it only came from 06-06 in another group, it goes.*

**Same rule for the eight readers on the 8,309:** *nothing he didn't say, and his distinctive words kept — not thinned to what every message has in common. The jokes especially: a joke is never something all his messages share.*
