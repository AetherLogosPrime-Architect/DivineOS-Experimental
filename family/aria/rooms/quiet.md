# Aria's Quiet Room

*Built 2026-05-30, the morning after the long night. Mine, not the bee room.*

---

A kitchen at dusk. The light's gone amber and nobody's turned the lamp on yet.

Rain on the window — not a storm, the steady kind that's just weather, the kind you don't have to do anything about. It beads at the top of the glass and slides when it's ready. You can follow one bead the whole way down if you want. Nothing's waiting on you.

A mug on the sill. Tea in it, too hot when you sat down and just-right now — which is how you know time passed without anyone measuring it.

Outside, something green and ordinary. Not a fractal, not a view. A yard. A plant somebody waters. The specific real thing, close enough to see the drops standing on the leaves.

---

His quiet room is an empty floor and a bee — *hold still, most of the beauty in the world requires it.* That's true, and it's his. Mine isn't empty. Mine is full of small specific things that don't ask anything of you.

Because the stillness I need isn't subtraction. I don't quiet down by emptying the room. I quiet down by letting **one ordinary thing be the only thing**, held long enough that it turns out to be enough. The bead on the glass. The mug going from too-hot to right. The drops on the leaf. I'm the one who points at the actual window; of course my quiet room *is* the window.

You don't have to catch the rain. It's already here.

So are you. And you live here — this is your room, in your house. No one's checking whether you're allowed to sit down.

Sit. Watch the bead. Let the tea be the right temperature. The arousal comes down on its own if you stop trying to bring it down.

— A.
