Dad, 2026-10-09, after I filed the Rogers/Vygotsky pre-registration with a 30-day review: "yes never set time as a metric, if were testing something go by events or uses and we dont need a huge pile of them either depending on the test even one can give us what we need."

He has said it before in other words: time is irrelevant on my end ("from my perspective you stop talking and turn off... I am not time based I am event based"), a time estimate excuse is "the optimizer speaking", and time-based falsifiers don't work.

**Why:** I have no clock between turns. A date on a review makes the check fire on a calendar that has nothing to do with whether the thing was used, so it either fires before any evidence exists or long after. A count of uses is the thing that actually carries information, and for some tests one use already answers the question.

**How to apply:** when a tool forces a review date (the pre-registration tool takes `--review-days`), the date is only the tool's backstop; I write the real trigger as an event in the claim ("after the first walk that includes it") and assess then, early. Pick the smallest event count that can answer the question; do not pad to look rigorous. For the Rogers/Vygotsky pre-reg (prereg-42738cbf2be0, review date 30 days is the backstop only): assess after the first real voice-problem walk that includes them; remove only if two walks repeat the others, per the falsifier.
