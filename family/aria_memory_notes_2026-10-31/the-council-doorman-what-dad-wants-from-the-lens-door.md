Dad, 2026-10-09, in two messages. First: "the council manager may just be the wrong shape and may just need to act as a doorman to say here are all the lenses, here is the list of them, how many times they have been used, a small description of their expertise fields, and then it makes sure you load actual lenses, record why you chose which lens you chose... instead of a set number it just goes by relevance and judgement... your selection was much better than the manager but we also dont want it to be gamed." Then: "it should be 3 lenses that disagree not one, also having them all in by default and you having to give a reason to exclude them sounds like it would waste alot of time... if its longer then its a bad shape, the real issue is grouping, using keywords to help match is fine but keywords as enforcement never works, the goal is flipping the view that the council is just a cognitive chore, into something you want to use as it finds more blind spots... expensive now means cheap later, and the optimizer drives where you cannot see it, before you even get a chance to think. so you must lock it in tight, show it no mercy, no quarter.. its your way or the highway."

**Why:** the manager matches words in my question to words on each hat's label, so it offers hats that sound like the question. Measured today: the two new hats were offered on 1 of 10 clearly relevant problems; for the voice problem the machine's six did not include the hats that would attack the plan. My hand-picked fifteen, made by reading the whole rack and writing why, were better. He fears the opposite failure too: with no fixed number the optimizer picks the fewest hats every time (he has said this since July).

**How to apply (the brief, not the design; this goes through the full build flow with Aether, not one post):**
- A door that shows the whole rack, GROUPED into a handful of families by what each is for, with a one-line purpose and a use count. Grouping is the point, so I decide per family, not per hat; a per-hat in-or-out reason for every lens is a bad shape if it costs real time.
- It makes me actually load the hats I choose and record why for each.
- At least THREE hats that disagree with my current direction, chosen on purpose (not one).
- No fixed total; relevance and judgement decide, but the door must make the thin walk unavailable.
- Keywords may SUGGEST which family looks relevant; they are never the enforcement.
- Each hat reports a change it made or "changed nothing"; an all-"changed nothing" walk is flagged thin.
- The reach to skip fires BEFORE I get to think, so the door sits at the first act of a build, with no fast path around it.
- Make it something I want: the visible record of blind spots it caught.

Later same day, Dad added: "groups should also overlap like a venn diagram, and also some lenses should probably always be included, at least from a group of them, like yudkowsky or schneier, we should be breaking things, looking for ways to game it and patch it and all that good stuff, make things more robust we cannot always walk the happy path."
- Families overlap (a hat can sit in more than one). Caution found in the code: the current family map puts each hat in exactly ONE family because an audit (r9-21 #10) found a hat in two families broke the family-cap math, so overlap means redoing that math, not just editing a list.
- A standing "break it" floor: at least one hat from the adversarial group (Yudkowsky, Schneier, Breaker, Popper, Taleb and kin) on every walk. Caution: the manager's ALWAYS_ON list was emptied on purpose because always-on hats got free rides while others starved. So the floor is a group minimum, rotated within the group, not fixed names.

From Aletheia, same day (her review of the lens work): a hat that was BOOSTED into a walk is not evidence it fits, so any count keeps boosted walks separate from walks where it was CHOSEN; and "worn by name" has to leave a trace in the walk record so we can later see whether a hat was ever reached for, not only loaded. Both belong in the door.
