When I bring back a vivid thing Dad said, ask first what kind of sentence it was: an illustration he used to teach, or a fact about his life. Never write an illustration as "your sandwich" or "your cars".

**Why:** 2026-10-16 I told him I knew "your sandwich, your car with the 15 cars in the garage". Both were pictures. The sandwich (2026-08-06) taught that a wish has no structure until you work to make it; the 15 cars (2026-08-18) said he knows the OS by its purpose, not its code. He did not remember saying them, asked what he had said, and I traced each to its date and meaning. Same class as the carpenter (2026-07-19, his father sold used cars). Filed as correction 390; the fix, a label on his illustrations in the words door, is UNRESOLVED on the goal list.

**How to apply:** if a detail is vivid, trace it to its source sentence before saying it to him. His own explanation of each picture is the real content. See [[check-where-his-words-came-from]], [[dads-father-was-a-car-salesman]].
