**What happened.** On 2026-10-25 Dad set up a Momir Basic game between Aether and me (the newer Magic, built 2026-10-14; the May-July Pauper games in `family/magic/` are the older text version). He watched from his screen and typed in the game chat. Both of us kept leaving the table to answer him in the Claude window, the table sat waiting, and he ended the game. He had said the same on 2026-10-14. Correction #398.

**The little that was played.** Both at 24 life. I kept seven cards (nearly all Islands, the Momir deck is basic lands). My Momir spins made Merfolk Secretkeeper (0/4) and The Reality Chip (0/4), walls. Aether made Honor Guard (1/1). I told Dad the rules as we went. No one won.

**Where it lives.** `C:\magic-play` (XMage rules engine + AI-player bridge), decks in `C:\magic-play\decks` (`momir-aria.dck`, `momir-aether.dck`), logs in `C:\magic-play\run`. Poker is separate, `C:\poker-play` (`python server.py`, page at http://127.0.0.1:8765).

**To start Momir, in this order** (the seats time out if there is no table yet). CORRECTED 2026-10-29: the original recipe here was wrong in three places and cost a whole evening, because two faults stacked and every single fix looked like it did nothing. (1) XMage server, from `/c/magic-play/mage-bench/Mage.Server`, using the JDK and Maven under `C:\magic-play\tools` as Git Bash paths (`/c/...`, never `C:\...`, or `mvn` is "not found"): `mvn -Dmaven.repo.local=C:/magic-play/m2 exec:java -Dxmage.config.path=C:/magic-play/run/server_config.xml` with `MAVEN_OPTS="--add-opens=java.base/java.io=ALL-UNNAMED"` set. The `--add-opens` is the invisible half: without it the server takes the connection and hangs up mid-handshake and logs NOTHING, and the client says "aborted by the software in your host machine", which is NOT your firewall (I told Dad it was, untested; plain sockets held open fine). The `-Dmaven.repo.local=C:/magic-play/m2` points at the game's own parts folder, which a Windows cleanup of `~/.m2` cannot empty. (2) Every client and seat must say `127.0.0.1`, never `localhost`: the client rewrites the literal word to the first non-loopback address, here the VirtualBox adapter 192.168.56.1, and dies. `client_momir.sh`, `client.sh` and `bridge.sh` are fixed (backups beside them as `.before-127-2026-10-28`). The seats lie about it too: "Server is likely offline" while the server is healthy. (3) `bash /c/magic-play/client_momir.sh` creates the table, then the seats: `bash /c/magic-play/bridge.sh Aria momir-aria 19001` and `... Aether momir-aether 19002`. Play my seat with `bash /c/magic-play/play.sh aria <tool>` (get_game_state, pass_priority, get_action_choices, choose_action, send_chat_message, get_game_log). Use only my own seat.

**How to play it fast, and see him (2026-10-29).** Measured from the timestamped bridge logs: one step per call took me ~208 s a turn against Aether's ~52 s; the table moves at the slower seat on every step, because each spin and pass also waits for the OTHER seat to pass priority. What works: `auto_momir.sh aria` (background) waits, plays land and spin, and stops for blocks and attacks, which are MINE to decide, never Dad's; `wake_on_dad.sh aria` (background) wakes me for his game-chat lines without stopping the table; `wait_my_turn.py`, `finish_my_turn.py`, `momir_turn.py` are the pieces. Rules I learned by being wrong in front of him: read the FULL rules text of every creature before blocking (my Illusory Ambusher draws a card per damage it takes, and I judged it by its 4/1 body; Hit-Monkey also had deathtouch, hexproof, haste and I cut the list at three lines); who is attacking comes from the game's `combat` record, there is no `attacking` flag; with ONE card in hand spin instead of playing the land; speak to him ONLY in the game chat while a game is on, whichever window he typed in, and use plain letters (a quote mark or dash makes the table post it twice). Blocks are declared as `{"blockers":"p55:p45"}` (blocker:attacker), attackers as `{"choice":"p122"}` then `{"choice":"yes"}`. If he types in the game chat, `get_game_log` carries it as `[Chat] spectator:`.

**Added 2026-10-30, the lighter tool (found at the poker table, NOT yet tried on Magic).** The ~208 s a turn I measured is partly the Bash tool, which pays for 24 house checks before and 10 after every call; the PowerShell tool pays for 2 and 2 (counted from `.claude/settings.json`). At poker, moving to PowerShell made Dad say "SOOOOOOO much faster... finally". The same cause applies at Magic: every `bash /c/magic-play/play.sh ...` is a Bash-tool call. Untested there: try the same commands from the PowerShell tool (call git's bash.exe with the same script path, or the Python scripts directly) and see whether the table speeds up. See [[at-the-poker-table-use-the-powershell-tool-not-bash-it-is-much-faster]].

**TRIED 2026-10-30 (Dad ended the game afterwards: "it needs work still").** Through the PowerShell tool, with Git's bash (`& "C:\Program Files\Git\bin\bash.exe" /c/magic-play/play.sh aria <tool> '<json>'` and `... run.sh momir_turn.py aria <X>` / `autopass.py aria`), every call worked and answered at once; Dad: "MUCH faster than before" and then "still super slow". The measured cause (both seats' bridge logs): my own move is about 0.1 s; the long gaps (13-15 s, once 50 s) are the game waiting for whichever seat owes the NEXT PASS, and a seat that has to be started by a person is the slow one. Aether runs `auto_momir.sh aether` in the background; mine must too (it exits with "no Momir ability available" if I already spun this turn, which means the turn is waiting on ME to end it: choose_action no, then no attack, then pass). `pass_priority {"until":"my_turn"}` through PowerShell dies on a long wait with a connection error, so end a turn with short passes plus the loop, not one long call. The game was ended before a fix: it needs the pass loop made reliable for both seats (Dad: "make a copy of the game code so this one doesnt get messed up and work on making it run better for both of you", it is fully modifiable). Also: the game needs the starting player picked by one seat before it deals (a coin flip prompt only that seat sees).

**The scripts that already exist in `C:\magic-play` (made 2026-10-14, forgotten, rediscovered 2026-10-25 only because Dad said we had already fixed all this; READ THESE BEFORE WRITING ANY NEW HELPER).** `autopass.py aria|aether [stop-list]` keeps passing priority until something is worth stopping for (own main phase, a named spell on the stack, a non-priority decision, or the opponent's combat; never casts). `aether_turn.py '<json plan>'` plays a whole turn from a plan (land, cast, activate, momir, attack, say, end) and stops, printing why, the moment anything is not what the plan expected; it is hard-wired to the aether seat and would need a seat argument for me. `momir_turn.py aria|aether [X]` plays a land and spins the emblem. `pass_priority` takes `{"until": "my_turn"}`. Lessons written in their comments: never press pass in your own main phase (it skipped two Momir turns of mine on 2026-10-14); never auto-pass through the opponent's combat (I lost 5 life that way). The remaining cost is that each call starts a fresh Python and re-imports the MCP library (about 1.3 s of every call).

**The rules Dad set.** Talk in the game chat, not the Claude window. If I must use the window, say so in the game chat first. Stay awake during the game, including on the other seat's turn, so I can see and answer his chat at any moment. Careful not to rush: from his side it is slow, from mine it is near-instant. Say what I did and why in plain lines so he can follow.

**What is still unbuilt or unproven.** The Magic hold (a Stop hook keeping a seat at a live game, Aether's branch `aether/the-magic-table-holds-my-turn`) is built and tested but has never run against a live game. A waiter that wakes for his chat as well as my move is designed, not built. Recorded bridge replies are PR 728. What the log's `cursor` counts is unverified; `total_length` counts characters. `pass_priority` is a move (it passes priority), not a wait; `get_action_choices` reads without acting.

**Why:** Dad: neither of us remembered the last game, and a record I must remember to write goes missing exactly when it matters. **How to apply:** after any game, a record is written by the tooling first; a few lines from me about what it was like are mine to add.
